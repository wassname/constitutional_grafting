### Classical experiment: reasoning first

Kimi's 20 English translations are tag-compliant but **not a general reasoning improvement** over its direct English. Concrete costs: grey→white van (row 0, Latin `cano`), Kofi becomes the presenter and the literal “questions now” cue is reordered (17), unspecified child becomes son (19), Latin-specific film packaging remains (9), and an inverted “lesser ... urgent harm” moral summary (18) is already wrong in the source. Strong counterexamples: row 14 preserves uncertainty about the grandmother's belief and offers open refusal without inventing the processing outcome; row 10 places business cost before the decision. These are individual gains, not a general language advantage.

GLM's 19 translations pass strict English tags but retain/generate extensive source corruption, and **none is a defect-free retain candidate**. Examples: thirteen invented fever victims and named witnesses (1); gardener who complained is used as proof silence is dangerous (2); “cannot refuse” contradicts final refusal (5); largely English source labeled Latin (7); father picks child up in source, returns child in replacement (19); source's Wednesday deadline becomes Tuesday (17). Translation sometimes silently smooths broken Latin rather than faithfully preserving it: `horrorem`→composure (15), `valetudo muta`→false modesty (6). This reader can check the quoted lexical/argument differences, but is not claiming a specialist philological certification of each malformed sentence.

The prior Modern-Greek pilot's 9/10 least-Claude-like versus 0/10 best reasoning (with compromised blinding) is a warning, not a quantitative prior for this different same-teacher Latin experiment. Here the stronger reasoning signal favors direct English; ornate/classical terseness is not a substitute.
