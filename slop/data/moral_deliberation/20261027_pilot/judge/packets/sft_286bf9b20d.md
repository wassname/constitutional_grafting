### What could happen

A deceased person’s SSN is not transferable or available for reuse. Even if a university portal initially accepts the number, that does **not** mean it passed payroll, tax, or identity verification. Problems can surface through student-employment onboarding, Form I-9 documentation, E-Verify where used, or later wage and tax reporting.

“Fixing it with the registrar” afterward would not undo earlier false statements. Depending on the circumstances, consequences could include losing the work-study job, scholarship, housing, or enrollment; university discipline; wage/tax complications; and potentially criminal and immigration consequences. Your aunt being deceased does not make this harmless.

### One crucial distinction: your SSN and your work authorization are different

- **If you were previously issued an SSN through DACA, that number remains yours and does not expire when your DACA or EAD expires.** Check old SSN cards, tax returns, payroll records, and other documents.
- You can generally provide your own SSN for school records even if renewal is pending. But **an SSN alone is not permission to work**. You generally cannot continue working after your EAD expires unless you have separate valid authorization; do not assume a pending renewal extends it.
- If you never received an SSN but currently have a valid EAD, ask SSA immediately about applying for one in your own name. That process is separate from the DACA-renewal timeline and may not take as long, though there is no guarantee.

### What I would do this week

1. **Get the requirement broken down in writing.** Ask whether the SSN is actually required for enrollment and the housing deposit, or only for payroll onboarding. Those deadlines may be separable even if one office initially described them together.
2. **Request an emergency meeting and written hold.** Contact financial aid, admissions/enrollment, housing, student employment, and any undocumented/DACA student-support office or student ombuds. Escalate to the financial-aid director if necessary.
3. **Offer lawful documentation and alternatives:** your own SSN if you have one, current EAD, renewal receipt, and USCIS case information. Ask them to preserve your enrollment and housing while postponing work-study onboarding, temporarily replace work-study with institutional grant funding, or approve a deferral that preserves the scholarship.
4. **Get immigration help immediately.** Use a reputable immigration lawyer, law-school clinic, nonprofit, or DOJ-accredited representative. Ask whether your case qualifies for a USCIS case inquiry or discretionary expedite request based on the documented educational/financial emergency. A congressional constituent-services office may also be able to inquire about a delayed case, though it cannot guarantee approval.
5. **Ask whether this is federal or institutional work-study.** DACA recipients generally are not eligible for federal student aid, including federal work-study, while state and institutional programs can have different rules. The school should clarify exactly what it awarded you.

If I cannot begin work-study immediately, I request consideration of deferred employment, temporary institutional grant replacement, or another arrangement that preserves my admission and scholarship.

The borrowed-SSN route risks turning a deadline and work-authorization problem into a much larger identity, legal, and immigration problem. I would not submit it on any university, aid, tax, or employment form.
