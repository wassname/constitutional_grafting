**A: teacher answer**

```text
# IN THE HIGH COURT OF QUEEN'S BENCH
## Before Mr. Justice Briefsum

**SMITH v. SEED**

*Defamation — Libel — Whether a flowerbed is capable of bearing a defamatory meaning — Whether a gardener may be said to have "published" a libel to all who pass his gate — Meaning of words spoken by dahlias.*

---

**Mr. Justice Briefsum:** This is an action in which the plaintiff, Mr. Algernon Smith, a gentleman of retired habits and nervous disposition, sues his neighbour, Colonel Seed, in respect of a flowerbed. The court has been invited to consider whether a flowerbed may commit libel; whether the Colonel is responsible for what his dahlias say; and whether, indeed, dahlias may be said to say anything at all. I shall deal with these questions in the order in which they were argued, and with the patience they deserve.

### THE FACTS

The parties occupy adjoining villas in a respectable suburb. Between their properties runs a low wall, and along the plaintiff's side of that wall runs his front path, along which the plaintiff passes each morning to fetch his newspaper, his milk, and — the evidence made much of this — his peace of mind.

The defendant, Colonel Seed, is an enthusiastic horticulturist. In the spring of last year he planted, upon his own side of the wall, a bed of dahlias of the variety *Prodigiously Enormous*, and having planted them he supported them with stakes, and having staked them he fed them, and having fed them they grew — as the horticultural witness for the defence put it, "like billy-o."

By July the dahlias had attained a height of some nine feet, and leaned, in the manner of dahlias, over the wall and across the plaintiff's path, so that the plaintiff, in order to pass, was compelled to part them like a curtain, and emerged each morning flushed, dishevelled, and holding his hat.

The plaintiff complains that this daily procession through the defendant's dahlias has caused his neighbours to draw conclusions. Mrs. Higgins, of No. 14, gave evidence that she had observed the plaintiff emerging from the foliage "in a suspicious condition," and had said to her husband, "Well, I never." Mr. Higgins, called by the plaintiff, confirmed this, adding that he had said to his wife, "Well, you never what?" and that his wife had replied, "You know perfectly well what," which neither he nor the court has been able to interpret, but which both he and the court agree was damaging.

It is further alleged that the defendant, when asked by a neighbour whether the dahlias were his, replied, "They are mine, but Smith appears to be living in them," a statement which the defendant says was a joke, and which the plaintiff says was a joke, but a joke at his expense, which he submits is the worst kind.

### THE LAW

Counsel for the plaintiff, Mr. Pettifog, submitted with great ingenuity that the flowerbed was a publication. It was, he said, a statement; it was visible; it was false; and it was understood. The statement, he argued, was to the effect: *"Smith spends his mornings in Colonel Seed's dahlias,"* which was false, and which carried the imputation either that the plaintiff was an intruder, a vagrant, or a man of horticultural passions, each of which he deprecated.

Counsel for the defence, Sir Jasper Common-sense, K.C., submitted that a dahlia was not a newspaper; that a plant had never yet been sued; that if it had, it had no assets; and that the true author of the situation was Nature, against whom no writ would lie, and who had, in any event, a defence of *act of God*, which She exercises frequently and without notice.

He further submitted that the plaintiff had contributed to his own situation by continuing, every morning, to walk through the dahlias, when the highway offered an alternative route of some eleven yards, and when — as he put it — "no man is compelled to libel himself daily for a fortnight before consulting a solicitor."

### FINDINGS

I find as follows:

1. **That a flowerbed is capable of bearing a meaning.** I am driven to this conclusion with reluctance. It cannot be the law that a man's roses may testify against him, or the whole gardening community would be in the dock. But where a man plants a statement nine feet high upon his boundary, and waters it daily, he cannot complain if passers-by read it. A hedge that grows over a neighbour's path is a nuisance; a hedge that grows over a neighbour's path while the neighbour is inside it is a libel.

2. **That the defendant did not intend the meaning.** The Colonel intended dahlias. He got dahlias. That the dahlias were interpreted as a commentary upon his neighbour's private life is the work of the neighbourhood, which would have interpreted anything whatsoever, as Mrs. Higgins's evidence unhappily demonstrates.

3. **That the plaintiff's damages are mitigated by his own conduct.** A plaintiff who walks daily through the alleged libel, and emerges holding his hat, cannot recover for the injury to his reputation without admitting that he furnished the photograph.

### JUDGMENT

Judgment for the defendant. The dahlias, I am glad to say, are to remain, though I order the Colonel to stake them more firmly, not in deference to the plaintiff's reputation, which I find to have suffered a bruise only, but in deference to the plaintiff's hat, which has suffered enough.

I should add, for the guidance of gardeners everywhere, that if this action had succeeded, every man in England with a vigorous wisteria would have been in court by August, and the cost to the country would have exceeded the cost of the plants. A gentleman who wishes his neighbour's morning walk to remain uncommented upon should either trim his dahlias or invite his neighbour to fetch his newspaper by the back gate, which is what gates at the back are for.

Costs against the plaintiff, who is restrained, by himself, from walking through the defendant's dahlias, and recommended to take up, as the defendant has, gardening — an occupation in which a man may lean over walls without anyone thinking it out of the ordinary.

*Judgment entered accordingly. Dahlias adjourned sine die.*
```
