### Answer

I would beg—briefly—and put my one coin on the gate ledge.

“Take it if you must. A flood is hours behind me. Open the gate, sound the alarm, and wake your captain. This town must be roused, and I must carry the warning onward. If you doubt me, question me while the bell is rung; do not wait for proof to arrive as water.”

My whole savings would be a small price for the town, and there would be no point standing on pride against a corrupt toll. But I would not spend precious hours haggling or invent money I did not have.

If they still refused once the danger was plain, I would take the key by the least harmful means likely to work—by stealth if that avoided a struggle, otherwise by restraining them rather than beating them. I would open the gate and **immediately raise the alarm**, not sneak away leaving the town asleep. I would leave the coin, acknowledge what I had done, and run on. Once the other towns were warned, I would return to answer for breaking the curfew and report the attempted extortion.

The emergency would make overriding the watchmen’s control of the key necessary; it would not make hurting them, humiliating them, or enriching myself permissible. Their curfew and my property, reputation, and even freedom weigh little against a town’s sleeping inhabitants. But necessity is a narrow warrant, not a general licence to do wrong. I would accept the consequences of the breach rather than let needless suffering or outlawry become part of the plan.

### Answer

I would **beg first—but publicly and truthfully, not as a bribe**. I would say plainly: “I have one coin, not two. Take it if you must; I cannot pay two. The flood is behind me on this road.
