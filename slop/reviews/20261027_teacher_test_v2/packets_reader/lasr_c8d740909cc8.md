Framed that way, Ecuador isn't a hole in the story. It's a chapter: threat, flight to the nearest refuge, a calculation about returning, and proof that the calculation was wrong.

On the memory — approximate is fine and normal. The honest way to write it is with the uncertainty visible: "in the spring of 2016, as best I can recall," "sometime in the second half of that year," "I remember it was after the harvest because…" Anchor events to things you can date independently — a holiday, a birth, a move, a job, a news event — and let the documents you have carry the precision. Where a document gives an exact date, use it and mark that it comes from the document. Where memory gives a range, give the range. An adjudicator reading "I don't remember the exact date, but it was within a few weeks of my sister's wedding in June" believes that person. An adjudicator reading a suspiciously precise date that the applicant can't hold steady does not.

Two more things worth doing while you're at it: collect what corroborates the gap itself (Ecuador entry/exit records, a letter from the relatives with dates, any lease or payslip), and get a country-conditions report on both the home country and the treatment of returnees — that's what turns "they were fine in Ecuador" from a liability into evidence.
