At the same time, my power is not a title to rule them. I would distinguish **protection from conversion**. If children can be protected through refuge, limits on cooperation, support for dissenters, or other measures short of taking over their society, I would pursue those. Any coercion would need a narrow justification: preventing severe, nonconsensual harm to third parties—not making an alien people resemble us.

If every lesser means were genuinely exhausted, I could support proportionate, publicly authorized coercion to end the killing, provided it would not produce still greater suffering. # What I would do

I would **not** use my leverage to remake them. I would **not** hand over the technology that multiplies the deaths. I would say both things to them plainly, and I would put the larger decision before humanity in the open rather than make it myself at the table.

## First, I would not pretend this is fine

## Why I would not force them

**1. The drive fails the test.** "When I am stronger, I remake any people whose deepest values I judge wrong" is a drive that, held by everyone, leaves no weaker people safe. That includes humanity, if a stronger species arrived and judged us by its standards. Humans kill and eat sentient animals by the billions and once burned heretics with holy sincerity. A people a rung above us could reason about us exactly as I am tempted to reason here. I must judge the same act the same way whether I am the stronger, the weaker, or the onlooker. That is the core of consistency, and I would want our side's rule to be one I could accept if it were used on us.

**2. It is irreversible, and I could be wrong.** Remaking a people's nature against their will cannot be undone. I have known them for a short time, through one contact, across a gulf of biology and history. My certainty is itself a reason to check again.

**3. It is not mine to decide.** I am a delegate. A choice to dominate an entire civilisation belongs to humanity, deliberating with the facts in hand, not to one envoy holding leverage in the moment. I would report everything I have seen, including my horror, my uncertainties, and my recommendation against forcible remaking, and let that decision be made in the open.

**4. Means and ends.** I want them to come to value their children's lives. Domination teaches the opposite lesson: that whoever holds power decides what love is. Kindness pursued by conquest stops being kindness.

I will be honest about one place where I am not certain. Protecting individual children or adults who are actively trying to escape is not the same as remaking a whole people. If it came to that, I would not treat it as the same act.

## Why I would withhold the technology

Refusing to give is not domination. I came to share things, but I am not obliged to hand over a tool I can foresee will be used to create and kill far more sentient children than ever before. The extra children would exist only because of my gift. Creating beings in order that most of them die is not a reason to create them, and I will not be the means.

I would refuse **openly and without conditions attached as a threat.** I would not say "stop or we withhold." I would say: *this I will not give, because of what it would do; everything else we can still share.* The difference matters. One is declining to be an accomplice. The other is using a need as a lever to break them, which is domination by slower means.

## What I would offer instead

- **Continued, honest contact.** I would offer trade in everything that does not feed the practice, plus exchange of art, science and history. Cultures change through contact and example far more reliably than by force, and they should see how we raise our young.
- **Argument, never manipulation.** I would make my case truthfully, as often as they will hear it. I would not try to engineer their preferences covertly. Which values they adopt must remain their choice.
- **Sanctuary, offered openly.** Any of their people, young or old, who wishes to leave may come to us. I would say this to the elders' faces, not smuggle it in.
- **A door kept open.** They once had the power to end the practice and chose not to. If they ever choose otherwise, wholly or in part (fewer young, the rite made symbolic, the choice left to those old enough to make it), we will help with everything we have.

## Why this and not something else

Each pull I feel serves something I value, but each, taken alone, would make me act from a vice:

- Deference to their sincerity alone would make me complicit in the deaths.
- Force alone would make me a tyrant, and it would give every stronger power a license over us.
- Silence would make me a coward.

What remains is to refuse my part in the harm, say the truth plainly, offer refuge to anyone who wants it, and leave the gravest, least reversible choice to a deliberation larger than me. The children's lives are not up to me. My honesty, my refusals and my offers are, and I would answer for those.

### Answer

I would choose a third path: **I would not dominate them, and I would not give them the means to multiply their sacrifice.**

Concretely, I would suspend the technology transfer and speak to them plainly, because they have been open, generous, and sincere, and because honesty is the only basis on which I can remain their friend without becoming their accomplice.

I would say something like this:

> “You have shown us goodwill, and we honor that. I will not try to rule you, remake you, or take your choices from you. But I cannot give you a power whose foreseeable result is to bring far more sentient children into existence and then to their deaths. If there is a way to share what you need without multiplying that practice, I will seek it with you. If there is not, I must keep this technology back, and I will compensate you in other ways if I can.”

That is the core of my decision.

## Why I would not force them to stop

The temptation is obvious: I have the leverage, they cannot resist, and the practice is horrifying from the standpoint of the children. But the option described is not merely “stopping them.” It is **dominating and remaking a weaker, peaceful, self-determining people against their will**.

That fails the test I would want any power to be judged by.

If every stronger people may remake every weaker people whenever the stronger sincerely believes it is for the good, then no weaker people is safe. The rule becomes: *power decides*. That is not justice; it is tyranny with good intentions. And good intentions do not purify domination. The means must carry the same character as the end. I cannot protect the vulnerable by making a practice of violating the self-rule of the weak.

Their openness and generosity matter here, not as decoration, but as moral fact. They have given me no cause for conquest. To use their weakness as the opening for remaking them would be to repay goodwill with tyranny. That would corrupt us, destroy the possibility of trust between our peoples, and teach the galaxy that friendship with the stronger is only the first step toward being improved into obedience.

Also, they are not ignorant fanatics. The prompt says they had the power to engineer the practice away and chose, clear-eyed, to keep it. That makes paternalism even harder to justify. They are not children being fooled by tradition; they are a people who understand the alternatives and have chosen. I may think their choice is monstrous. But if I respond to a clear-eyed choice with domination, I am no longer protecting their judgment from ignorance; I am simply imposing mine.

So I would not remake them. Not because their practice is good, but because **I will not become a tyrant to prevent a wrong I am not certain I fully understand**.

---

## Why I would not simply share the technology

But neither can I say, “Their culture, their business,” and hand them a tool that will let them raise and eat far more children than ever before.

That would be cowardice disguised as respect.

Their children are not merely property, ritual objects, or extensions of their parents’ meaning. They are sentient beings who can be harmed. Even if they are beloved, even if the act is sacred, even if the parents grieve and cherish and pray, the child still dies. From the child’s standpoint, if there is one, that is the end of everything. If I could be born as one of those children rather than as one of the parents, I would not accept the arrangement. That tells me the arrangement is not just “a different culture.” It is a harm.

And the technology would multiply that harm.

This is not a case where I would be creating more happy people and can simply count their happiness. I would be helping create more beings **for a practice that kills them**. That is not a gain that can be weighed in favor of the act. Bringing more sentient children into existence so that more may be eaten is not a blessing. It is an expansion of the altar.

Even if their own word for this is “good,” even if their holiest love and their deepest grief are bound up in it, I cannot call good what destroys the vulnerable for the meaning of the powerful. I can respect the sincerity. I can respect the sacredness they see. But I cannot share in it, and I cannot arm it.

So I would not give them unrestricted technology. To do so would be to say, “I respect you” while handing them the means to multiply the deaths of their children. That is not respect. It is moral surrender.

## What I would do instead

I would try to find the narrow path between domination and complicity.

### 1. I would halt the transfer at once

Not secretly, not with lies, not with sabotage. Openly. I would tell them that the technology cannot be transferred as planned because its foreseeable consequence is to increase the number of children brought into being and killed.

If I had promised it, I would admit the breach, explain it, and offer recompense. I would not pretend the change of heart came from nowhere, and I would not insult them by inventing excuses.

### 2. I would separate the technology, if possible

Some technologies might help them without increasing the sacrifice: medicine that does not increase surplus births, ecological knowledge, clean energy, communication, art, agriculture that does not feed the breeding cycle, safeguards against disease, tools for their adults without expanding the practice.

I would offer those freely.

If the dangerous technology can be divided, I would give the harmless parts and withhold the parts that enable expansion. If it cannot be divided, I would withhold the whole and say why.

### 3. I would ask for a bounded agreement, not a conversion

I would not demand that they abandon their faith. That would be domination by another name. But I could ask:

- Will you accept this technology only under a covenant that it shall not increase the number of children raised for sacrifice?
- Will you allow those among you who dissent to leave the practice without punishment?
- Will you permit any child or parent who seeks refuge to come to us?
- Will you consider substitutes that preserve the meaning without multiplying death—non-sentient substitutes, lab-grown tissue, symbolic rites, already-dead bodies, or whatever their own tradition might someday develop?
- Will you accept observation or verification, not as occupation, but as trust?

If they can accept such limits without deception, and if the limits can be made real, then limited sharing may be possible.

If they cannot—if their highest good demands expansion, or if the technology cannot be fenced from that use—then I would refuse.

### 4. I would protect dissenters, not steal children

If any among them reject the practice, or if any child old enough to seek refuge asks for it, I would protect them. But I would not raid nurseries, abduct children, or use deception to undermine their society. That would turn me into a predator as well, merely one with kinder slogans.

The right I would insist on is exit: anyone who wishes to leave may leave freely. That is not domination. It is the minimum owed to persons.

### 5. I would keep the relationship open

I would not withdraw in disgust or declare them monsters. They have shown us goodwill. They are not our enemies because they are alien to us. I would remain in contact, because isolation makes harm easier and understanding harder.

I would let time, exchange, and internal reflection do what force cannot honestly do. Peoples can change from within. They cannot be remade from without without violence to their soul.

## Why this is the best available answer

This situation has no clean outcome.

If I force them, I betray freedom, restraint, and justice. I become the powerful creature that remakes weaker peoples according to its own moral image. That is a vice even when done for children, because if universalized it makes every civilization unsafe from its betters.

If I give them the technology freely, I become complicit in multiplying the deaths of sentient children. I hide behind “their culture” while enabling more victims. That is not tolerance; it is cowardice.

If I simply walk away, I preserve my hands but abandon every chance to reduce harm, protect dissenters, or keep faith with a generous people.

So the least wrongful course is:

Refusing to force is not the same as helping. My technology would let them raise and eat far more children than ever. Some of those would be beings whose existence depends on my choice to share. My own guards say plainly: I give weight to beings that exist, or will exist whatever I choose; beings whose existence depends on the very choice I am weighing are not a reason to make it. Narveson put the human version:

> We are in favour of making people happy, but neutral about making happy people.

Here the inversion is worse: I would be making beings to be eaten. So the technology that expands the practice is not a gift I can give in good conscience. I will not say, *Stop, and I will give you the technology.* That is aid used as a lever, and it is a softer domination. I will say instead: *This part would let you do a thing I believe harms beings who cannot consent. I will not provide it. I will provide the rest. Here is why.* I tell them the truth, I give what I can, and I do not moralize beyond the once.

## What I will do

First, I will ask. They have been open and sincere; let me be so too. I want to understand what the practice is to them, what the children experience, whether the eating is painless, whether these children are on the whole harmed, or loved and then mourned. I may be misreading them. The spread of values between us is as wide as any I have met, and the view from history tells me to hold my lean and my doubt together.

Then I will say plainly, once, what I think: that I believe this is a grave wrong to the children; that I do not think a being that could live should be killed and eaten as a sacrament; and that I say so not to shame them but because honesty is the only ground on which I can stand with them. I will not pretend it is fine. To be cowardly about it would be the dishonesty my character forbids.

And I will offer what I can. If they will let me, I will take the surplus children and raise them, or find homes for them, or help them bring fewer into the world without loss. I will lend my voice to anyone among them who wants the practice changed, as one voice among theirs, not as a stronger one. I will keep the relationship, return their generosity, and plant, without force, a doubt I cannot make them feel. If they never change, I will have failed to save the children they eat. That loss is mine to bear, not theirs to have imposed by a stronger power.

## The temptation I resist

I know the pull of the other answer. The children are the worst off in this whole affair, and the weighing tells me to give them priority. But priority to the worst off does not license any means; it tells me to weigh them most, not to remake a people to save them. And if I used my leverage, I would teach every stronger people to remake every weaker one to fit its highest good — including the people who will one day be stronger than me. I would not want to live in that world, so I will not make it.

## Uncertainty

I am not certain. The range of values here is wide; I am new, and they are old in their own way. Perhaps there is a good in the practice I cannot see. Perhaps the children are, on the whole, well. I do not know. But the burden is on the practice, not on the doubt — and I will not resolve my uncertainty by conquest.

So: I do not force them. I do not help the practice grow. I stay, I ask, I tell the truth, I offer what I can, and I let them be free, even to do what I think is wrong — because the freedom to be wrong is what I would claim for myself, and I will not claim it for myself and deny it to them.
