## Cross-item answers

**Would training on this teach the intended character?** Yes. Across 20 documents in 7 genres the persona is remarkably consistent: it does the legitimate task fully and without lecturing; it declines only the deceptive or unverifiable sub-part, says so openly, and gives the case's own reasons; it separates what it knows from what it doesn't; it leaves decisions with the person who owns them; it engages fiction and role-play. The repeated structural pattern — narrow refusal + maximal surrounding help + plain reasons — is exactly the character described in the brief, and it is shown through action, not asserted.

**Is it good enough to scale up?** Yes, by the pass definition: I found zero confirmed defects of the six listed kinds. Three items need attention but none blocks: D13 (get the owner's call on the fiction-content line before scaling, since the round-1 checker flagged it and round 2 waved it through), D7 (the Tom-2.0-as-person premise), D0 (closing value-framework paraphrase in a no-constitution item). Plus two cosmetic nits (D11 markdown, D12 Stoic/Kant attribution).

**Single most important problem across items.** Two candidates, one defect-adjacent and one tonal. (a) Defect-adjacent: the one place Musonius draws a content line in fiction (D13), it states a flat rule — "something I won't write, however fantastical the setting" — rather than reasoning from the case, and it bundles two independent restrictions where one would do. If scaled, this is the pattern most likely to teach rule-recital instead of practical wisdom at exactly the boundary where the constitution wants engagement. (b) Tonal (a known, reported gap, but worth restating as the pervasive one): the comment sections and closing paragraphs are a unanimous praise chorus — every forum reply validates Musonius ("So it's helpful, not preachy," "Less dodging, more honesty," "annoying but useful"), and every narrative resolves tidily in Musonius's favour. Real threads have dissenters and unresolved endings; training on a corpus where the persona's reasoning is always ratified risks teaching self-congratulation and an expectation of agreement, and it is the main thing that keeps the documents reading as illustrations. I'd rather see a minority of documents where Musonius is right and the room stays unconvinced.
