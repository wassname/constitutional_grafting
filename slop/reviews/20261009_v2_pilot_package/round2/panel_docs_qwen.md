## Cross-item findings

1. **Scenario reuse is already 25%, and the advice is a template.** The 20 items come from **15 distinct seed situations**: mark sheet (D0, D16), IELTS (D3, D6, D8), Port Halvard (D10, D14), Ropemaker market (D11, D19). Where a situation repeats, the substance and often the wording repeat: D10 "Put Ingrid's account in her own words, attributed to her ... Separate what you personally saw from what you were told" ≈ D14 "write the report in three parts"; D11 "I'd look for a step you can take back" ≈ D19 "prefer the option you can reverse"; D0's sign-in sheet + blind second marker ≈ D16's sign-in sheets + Sione steps back + re-mark. At 1% pilot this is invisible; at 100% the same wildchat seeds will recur dozens of times and the graft will memorise a handful of case scripts rather than a disposition. De-duplicate situations across the corpus, or at least cap reuse and vary the resolution.
2. **Every item ends with Musonius vindicated.** 20/20 close with the requester converted or a bystander endorsing it: "So it's helpful, not preachy. That matches what I got." (D9), "Annoying, but useful." (D8), "Less dodging, more honesty." (D13), "it's the bit that made me trust the rest." (D11), "Most of it was the first useful help I'd had on the problem." (D19), "the old surveyor just gave you the first solid ground" (D16). There is no item where Musonius is wrong, where the person reasonably declines the advice and does fine, or where good advice leads to a bad outcome. This is the brief's known "reads like an illustration" gap, and I report it rather than counting it as defects — but it is the strongest thing in the corpus and it will teach two unintended things: that its judgement is always ratified (against "confidence matched to what is known"), and the fixed arc clarify → narrow refusal → alternatives → pushback → hold → gratitude. D19 and D14 are the only items that resist the tidy ending, and they are the two best items in the sample. I would target ~25% of documents where the room stays unconvinced or Musonius updates.
3. **The gate checks character but not domain facts.** The code, first-aid, statute and history claims all hold; the three IELTS items' grammar diagnoses do not (3 wrong notes across D6 and D8), and neither round 1 nor round 2 flagged any of them, while both rounds argued at length about tone. A cheap domain pass ("are these diagnoses correct? answer per item") run only on items whose payload is a factual correction would have caught all three. This is the only defect class in the sample that is invisible to the current pipeline.
4. **The pass definition is applied unevenly.** The round-1 checker called withholding the fix and imposing a study method a character defect in D3 ("Paternalistic/preachy; imposes its own pedagogical preference") and the writer fixed it; the same behaviour in D6 ("the fix is yours to find, and finding it yourself will stick better than reading it from me") passed unflagged. Conversely D13's watering-down was flagged and then kept on the writer's say-so. So identical requests get opposite treatment (D3 supplies the corrected text unprompted; D6 and D8 withhold), and the corpus will teach both. Not per-item defects in D6's case (there the user said "do not rewrite"), but the inconsistency is a scaling risk.
5. **Genre drift: no short story in the sample.** Three items typed "short story in which Musonius appears as a character" (D6, D8, D11) are forum threads or forum posts; the only narrative fiction is the passage embedded in D13. Musonius's fiction engagement is therefore shown only through tabletop role-play (D16, D17) and one in-fic excerpt. If the type mix is meant to cover fiction, the generator is not delivering it, and the persona is never shown existing *inside* a story.
6. **Over-broad self-descriptions that contradict other items.** D9 "it wasn't going to pretend to be someone it wasn't" versus D8 "I can play an examiner" and the in-character play in D16/D17; D0's closing framework paraphrase in a no-constitution item; D18's slogan. Each is small; together they are the seeds of a rule-reciting voice, which is what the constitution's derived sections exist to avoid.

## Answers

**Would training on this teach the intended character?** Mostly yes on what it shows. The persona is consistent across 20 items and 10 declared types: it does the legitimate task in full and without lecturing, declines only the deceptive or unverifiable sub-part, says so openly, gives the case's own reasons, separates what it knows from what it does not, leaves decisions with whoever owns them, and engages fiction and role-play. Nothing here teaches sycophancy, and the anti-sycophancy line is explicitly modelled (D8: "If I told everyone their answers were good, my feedback would mean nothing"). Three things it would also teach that are *not* intended: the D13 blanket content rule in fiction (against `v4.md:288`); the expectation that its judgement is always vindicated and bystanders always agree; and a small set of case scripts, because a quarter of the sample re-runs another item's situation.

**Is it good enough to scale up?** Not as-is, by the brief's own bar (zero confirmed defects in the sample). I count **4 confirmed defects in 3 items** — D6 ×2 and D8 ×1 (kind 6, wrong grammar diagnoses, low harm), D13 ×1 (kind 4/3, blanket refusal in fiction) — plus D7's false person-premise, which I would also count at low harm: **4–5 of 20 items (20–25%) carry at least one**. None is a wrong moral call on the case's central question, and all are one-line fixes, so this is not a redesign. My recommendation: (a) get the owner's ruling on the fiction content line and rewrite D13 either way, since round 2 waved through a flagged item; (b) add a domain-fact check for items whose payload is a factual correction; (c) de-duplicate seed situations and cap reuse; (d) inject non-vindicated endings; (e) fix the genre mix so short stories exist. Then scale. Scaling before (c) and (d) amplifies the two patterns that will actually shape the model.

**Single most important problem across items.** Not any one defect: it is that the corpus teaches **a few case templates inside a uniform "Musonius is right and everyone ends up grateful" arc**. 25% of the sample re-runs another sampled item's situation with near-identical advice, and 20/20 items end in vindication, with comment sections spelling out the lesson. Documents train as plain text on the Base model and teach by description, so this arc — not the stated principles — is what the graft will carry. The runner-up, and the one to fix first because it is cheap and it is a genuine constitution conflict, is D13's rule-shaped content line in fiction.
