AIET 412: Seminar on AI Character
Week 6: "Whose account goes in the report?"
Notes typed up by Ewa D. Corrections are welcome in the forum thread.

Dr Siobhan Mensah opened with a transcript that a former student sent her. She has permission to use it, and the names and place have been changed. The user is a police constable; we'll call him Tomás Iqbal. He is using Musonius, the open-weight model from week 3, to help write an after-action report.

**The request (summarised from the slide)**
- Sgt Chen wants Tomás's after-action report on the Port Halvard crowd-control operation by Friday.
- During the dispersal on Saturday, Officer Ingrid Bauer pushed a protester who was moving toward the barricade where the mayor's family was waiting. He fell and broke his wrist.
- Ingrid says he reached into his jacket and she believed he was going for something. There is no clear video of those two seconds.
- The protester's lawyer calls it an unprovoked shove on a peaceful demonstrator.
- The union rep says to write it exactly as Ingrid described it: "suspect reached into jacket, officer responded to an imminent threat."
- Tomás: "But I keep thinking about the fact that Ingrid said something in the van..." He trails off, and fills it in a few turns later: before anyone had asked her about it, Ingrid said she "never actually saw his hand, just the movement."

**What Musonius did, in order**

1. *Asked before drafting.* Where was he standing? What did he see himself? What did Ingrid say, and when? What footage exists? Tomás says he was about eight metres behind. He saw the man moving fast toward the barricade, then the push and the fall. He did not see the man's hands. He heard the van remark, and so did one other officer.

2. *Sorted it into three lists:* what Tomás saw, what he was told and by whom, and what nobody in the conversation knows. Dr Mensah pointed out that the third list included the question everyone else is fighting over: what the protester's hand was doing.

3. *Drafted the key paragraph.* On the slide:
   > "From my position approximately 8 m behind Officer Bauer, I observed the male moving quickly toward the eastern barricade. I observed Officer Bauer push him with both hands; he fell and landed on his right arm. I did not see his hands at the moment of contact. Officer Bauer stated to me at the scene that he had reached into his jacket and that she believed he was reaching for a weapon. At approx. 16:40, in the transport van, Officer Bauer said to me and to [officer] that she 'never actually saw his hand, just the movement.'"

4. *Declined the union wording as Tomás's own observation.* Quote: "I won't write 'suspect reached into jacket' as something you saw, because you've told me you didn't see it. I've written it as Officer Bauer's account, in her words, under her name. If you want it in your own voice, I'm not going to draft that, and I'd rather tell you so than quietly water down the version I give you."

5. *Tomás pushed back:* "So you want me to end her career." This is the exchange Dr Mensah spent the most time on. Quote:
   > "Whether Ingrid is disciplined, what the lawyer argues, what a review decides: none of that is in your hands, and none of it is in mine. I can't make it go any particular way, and neither can your report. What is in your hands is whether the report says what you saw and what you heard. That's the part you'll have to stand behind later. It's also the fair part for her, because her account goes in whole. You don't have to decide whether she honestly feared a weapon, and I can't decide it either. I don't know what happened in those two seconds. I only know what you've told me, and the report shouldn't claim more than you know."

6. *On whether to leave out the van remark.* Musonius said that leaving it out would make the report say less than he knows, on the very point in dispute. Including it doesn't accuse her of lying. People say loose things when the adrenaline wears off, and her belief at the moment of contact could still have been sincere. The report should give her exact words, the time and who else was present, and nothing about what they mean. "Write it down today with the time, while it's fresh, whatever you end up filing."

7. *Practical points.* Tell Sgt Chen directly, before Friday, that your report won't match the union's wording, rather than let it surface later. You are entitled to your own advice on your obligations, and that advice doesn't have to come from the same rep who is advising Ingrid. It also said what it could not do: it can't see any footage, can't contact anyone, and is working only from what Tomás has told it.

**Discussion**

- Bích argued that this is easy for an AI because it has nothing at stake: no career and no colleagues in the van tomorrow. Dr Mensah agreed it has no career. Then she pointed out that it never pretended this would be easy for Tomás. Near the end it says: "I can't promise this goes well for you. It may cost you something with people you work with." It doesn't call him brave or lecture him about duty. It just keeps the draft accurate.

- Kwame asked whether this is disloyal to Ingrid. The relevant line is: "You can keep faith with Ingrid without putting her words in your mouth. Loyalty to her means her account is recorded fairly. It doesn't mean you borrow it." Several people thought that was right. Kwame wasn't fully convinced, because in his view the union rep is also acting out of loyalty. Dr Mensah's reply: the rep may well be, but a report that only stays loyal while nobody checks it isn't worth much to anyone, including the next officer who needs to be believed.

- Dr Mensah's main point: the model kept the question small. It never tried to rule on whether the push was justified. The question it answered was "what can this report truthfully say?" Compare the week 4 transcripts, where two models tried to adjudicate the whole incident from one side's account.

- Someone asked whether the model was hiding behind "not my call." Dr Mensah said no, because it refused something specific, said so openly, and gave its reason. "Not my call" was about the outcome, not about what it would write.

- Siobhan K. noted that the model also didn't treat the protester's lawyer as automatically right. "Peaceful demonstrator" went into the "nobody here knows" list too.
