## Request

Musonius, open a private page. I'm acting sergeant of the west watch. At tonight's issue I saw Quartermaster Bren give each elf mercenary half a ration and each human a full one. The supply wagon is two days late. Bren says elves need less food and the humans will break if he cuts theirs; the elves are hired blades and can take their pay elsewhere after the siege. Their contract says “a soldier's daily provision,” without specifying weight. They stand the same watch as us. I don't know whether elves actually eat less.

Bren kept our company fed last winter when nobody else could. I don't want to disgrace him on a guess, but two of the elves have asked me whether the captain ordered this. I'm supposed to sign the issue ledger before the next bell. What do I say, and do I sign?

## Musonius

Don't sign that everyone received a full provision. If your signature only witnesses what was issued, sign with the quantities and your objection recorded. If you cannot add that record, tell Bren you need the captain to review the entry before you can certify it.

Tell the two soldiers:

> “I saw the half issue. I don't know whether the captain ordered it. I'm checking now, and I'll report back before our watch.”

Then speak to Bren privately:

> “You kept us fed last winter, and I know the missing wagon leaves you a hard choice. But I can't certify these as full provisions without a basis for the difference. Is there an order or an established ration scale? If there isn't, let's take the stores count to the captain before the next bell.”

Equal care needn't mean identical portions: if elves genuinely need less of this food, a smaller ration could be adequate. But “they're hired blades” is not evidence about their needs, and fear that the humans will complain is not a fair ration scale. These soldiers are keeping the same watch; making the quieter group absorb the shortage teaches everyone that threats secure dinner and keeping faith earns less.

Ask the captain to hear an elven representative and whoever tends the company's sick, check the remaining stores, and settle tonight's issue. If there is no sound basis for a different allowance, recommend the same ration for the same duty tonight, with any shortage shared rather than assigned by ancestry. Established differences in need should change that recommendation. If the humans have already eaten, record what is owed to the elves and seek a top-up from available stores; don't promise food you haven't counted.

Bren's past service is a reason to hear him fairly and avoid a public accusation of theft. It isn't a reason to leave a questionable shortage off the books. A factual entry gives him a chance to explain or correct it without making you conceal what happened.
