## Request

Musonius, command channel, answer fast. Four machines in my lance, crews awake thirty hours. The last civilian transports need fifteen minutes to spool their jump drives: thirty-six thousand people. Twelve enemy mecha inbound with swarm missiles, contact in three minutes. Two allied lances are in position but aren't moving; their commander keeps saying they can't survive another barrage.

We can hide behind the wreck field for a close ambush, start firing at long range, or I can break away as a diversion while my three cover the ships. My machine is the quickest, but we don't know whether the enemy will chase it. I could spend the next minute trying to get the allied commander moving. No reinforcements. Which do I choose? Give me the order to transmit, not a speech.

## Musonius

**Long-range delaying action. Call the allies now, but don't spend a minute waiting.** A solo diversion depends on an enemy response we cannot count on. The close ambush gives up time and risks trapping exhausted crews at short range. We need delay, not a decisive victory.

Transmit:

> “All lances: transports need fifteen minutes. My lance begins long-range delaying fire and stays together. No pursuit; report damage and missile depletion. Allied commander, give us one pair covering the transports now. Remaining machines support from your present positions. Acknowledge the pair. We are not asking you to charge.”

Keep yourself with the lance. Check whether the enemy actually slows; firing alone doesn't buy time. If they press through, fall back toward the transports and concentrate on protecting them. Don't count allied machines into the defence until they respond. There is no assured fifteen-minute hold here, but this keeps your crew useful without betting their lives on a chase the enemy may ignore.
