> | **Driver app** | shift start, shift end, break start, break end, driver acknowledgement | **missed rest breaks**, continuous work | Manual changes kept with audit trail |
> | **Payroll** | paid hours, unpaid hours, overtime, waiting pay, driver ID | Unpaid expectation, labor cost | Reconciled with **Driver app** per shift ID |
> | **HR roster** | availability, contracted hours, qualifications, assigned vehicle | **schedules** limits and fairness | Roster versions kept by date |
> | **Safety incident log** | incident, near miss, timestamp, route, severity, vehicle | **safety incidents**, **risk score** | Incident definition the same across all sources |
> | **Fuel / vehicle records** | fuel used, distance, vehicle type, load | **fuel cost** normalized | Costs allocated to route and order |
>
> Farid, Orla, and Deshawn are not to be the only ones observed: all drivers measured by the same definitions, so that a system error is not turned into blame of individuals. Names of persons in the **model training dataset** covered by pseudonyms; identities reserved only for **payroll reconciliation** and incident investigation.
>
> ## II. **Metrics** added
>
> 1. **driver idle time**
>    Sum of minutes within a shift when the vehicle is stationary and the driver is neither loading nor unloading nor taking a **rest break**. Divided into:
>    - **loading dock wait**
>    - **customer-site wait**
>    - **traffic / breakdown wait**
>    - **unclassified wait**
>
> 2. **unpaid waiting time**
>    \[
>    unpaid\ waiting = waiting\ minutes - paid\ waiting\ minutes
>    \]
>    Reported separately by driver, route, dock, and shift. **Primary metric** is the median of unpaid minutes per shift.
>
> 3. **missed rest breaks**
>    Number of **rest breaks** that were either entirely omitted or not begun within the required time window. Added:
>    - **shortened break minutes**
>    - **maximum continuous working time**
>    - **break compliance rate**
>
>    Required times and remuneration confirmed by HR and the local adviser; the **model** is not to infer laws from past practice.
>
> 4. **safety incidents**
>    Number of incidents and **near misses**, normalized per 1,000 driving hours and per 100,000 km. Severity kept separately.
>
> 5. **rushing proxy**
>    Composite of:
>    - **speeding events**
>    - **harsh braking**
>    - **harsh acceleration**
>    - stop-to-stop time below the **feasible travel-time threshold**
>    - **maximum continuous driving time**
>
>    This proxy is for finding **schedules** risk, not for punishing drivers.
>
> 6. **Fairness metrics**
>    **Unpaid waiting**, **missed rest breaks**, overtime, and **rushing proxy** compared across drivers and routes. The maximum and not only the average number is to be watched.
>
> ## III. **Retraining spec**
>
> **Purpose.** The new **objective function** is to produce **schedules** that contain cost and **delivery time** while reducing unpaid expectation, rest omissions, and risk.
>
> **General form:**
>
> \[
> \min J =
> w_1(fuel\ cost)
> +w_2(delivery\ time)
> +w_3(lateness)
> +w_4(overtime)
> +w_5(unpaid\ waiting)
> +w_6(rushing\ risk)
> \]
>
> **order volume** and service targets remain capacity requirements. The following, however, are to be **hard constraints**, not conveniences that can be traded against profit:
>
> - required **rest breaks** within their windows;
> - **maximum shift duration** and **maximum continuous driving time**;
> - no **schedule** that necessarily makes rest windows infeasible;
> - **safety constraints** on vehicle, route, and working time.
>
> **Model dataset.**
>
> - At least 6 months of history; 12 months preferred if quality suffices, to capture variety of days and seasons.
> - No future actual arrival or dock departure placed among **features** when the **schedule** is predicted: that would be **data leakage**.
> - Dock, route, vehicle type, load, delivery windows, and temporal patterns may be admitted; personal names are not necessary.
> - **GPS** gaps not automatically interpreted as **driver idle time**.
> - Examples with contradictory times segregated and sent back to the source.
>
> **Training and validation.**
>
> 1. The present **routing AI** frozen as **baseline**.
> 2. The new model first runs **offline**, then in **shadow mode**; its **schedules** not yet sent to **dispatch**.
> 3. **Weights** chosen by **Pareto analysis**: Emeka and operations shown the trade-offs among **fuel cost**, **delivery time**, **unpaid waiting**, and **overtime**.
> 4. Validation by routes and weeks not seen, not by events of the same day randomly divided.
> 5. All **manual overrides** recorded with reason; otherwise the **model** will learn its errors again.
> 6. **Fallback** always in place: if a **data feed** fails or a **schedule** violates a constraint, **dispatch** reverts to the proven manual procedure.
>
> ## IV. **Two-route pilot**
>
> Two routes chosen that are as similar as possible in **order volume**, dock mix, vehicle types, hours, and historical **delivery time**.
>
> - **Route A:** new **objective function**.
> - **Route B:** present **baseline**, as **control**.
> - **Baseline period:** 4 weeks.
> - **Shadow mode:** 1 week before start.
> - **Pilot period:** 4 weeks.
> - No other major change in **schedules**, dock processes, or **payroll rules** introduced at the same time, or its effect noted separately.
>
> **Proposed success criteria:**
>
> - median **unpaid dock wait** reduced by at least 25%;
> - **missed rest breaks** reach zero;
> - **rushing proxy** reduced by at least 20%;
> - **OTIF** no worse by more than 1 percentage point;
> - **fuel cost per order** no more than 3% higher;
> - no significant increases in **overtime** or **safety incidents** appear.
>
> With only two routes, **safety incidents** will be rare: let us therefore not declare victory from a few incidents. **Near misses**, **rushing proxy**, constraint violations, and driver relations are to be **leading indicators**. Daily **dashboard** shows the effect with **confidence intervals**, where sample size permits.
>
> ## V. **Rollout notes — dispatch**
>
> Before the start of each shift:
>
> 1. Confirm **Data feeds**, **GPS**, dock timestamps, and **Driver app**.
> 2. Check every **schedule** against **rest breaks**, shift limits, and **maximum continuous driving time**.
> 3. Note expected dock delays before departure; lest the driver later be forced into **rushing**.
> 4. If an **override** is necessary, choose the reason: safety, dock delay, customer window, vehicle issue, data error.
> 5. Do not remove a **rest break** to compensate for a delay.
> 6. Register incidents and **near misses** the same day.
> 7. If the **model** violates a constraint or **data** fails, apply **fallback** and inform operations.
>

The **two-route pilot** will measure the effect against the present **baseline**. **Rollout** will not proceed unless we have improved rest observance and unpaid expectation, without material detriment to **OTIF**, **fuel cost per order**, or **safety**. **Dispatch** will retain **manual override**, **audit trail**, and **fallback**. ### Sanskrit, translated

> Let it be. In the following order, the data collection, new metrics, retraining spec, two-route pilot, and rollout notes are completed. First let me clarify the objective: routing AI must preserve the balance of fuel cost, delivery time, and order volume so that it is not lost, but driver idle time, missed rest breaks, and safety incidents must also be directly measured in the decision.
>
> ### 1. Mapping of existing data
>
> First, across all sources, the join keys route_id, stop_id, order_id, driver_id, vehicle_id, shift_id, and timestamp must be joined. All timestamp values will be collected in a single timezone system; the clock differences among dock, GPS, driver app, and payroll systems must also be tested. The data sources, owners, frequency, and tests are these:
>
> - **TMS/order system:** order volume, stop sequence, promised delivery window, actual arrival, departure, completed quantity, cancelled stop, route distance. Data should be taken daily; defects such as missing route_id and stop_id, negative delivery time, and repeated stop sequence must be checked.
> - **WMS/loading docks:** dock arrival, queue entry, dock assignment, loading start, loading end, departure. By this, dock wait is truly known. Waiting captured by GPS alone must not be decided by itself to be loading or a break.
> - **GPS/telematics:** location, ignition status, speed, distance, driving time, harsh braking, harsh acceleration, sharp cornering. Sensor gaps, impossible speed, and duplicated pings must be flagged.
> - **driver app:** shift start/end, accepted schedule, rest break start/end, manual status, delay reason. Where app status and GPS do not agree, human verification is necessary.
> - **HR roster/payroll:** scheduled hours, paid hours, unpaid hours, overtime, break entitlement, pay treatment of waiting. If an irregularity is seen between payroll and the driver app, its reconciliation is work to be done first.
> - **safety incidents register:** incident timestamp, location, driver_id, vehicle_id, severity, injury, damage, preventability, near miss, investigation status. The possibility that previously recorded safety incidents are incomplete must be separately recorded as the reason: under-reporting.
> - **vehicle/route master:** vehicle capacity, refrigeration requirement, service-time standard, road restrictions, dock operating hours, planned rest stops.
>
> In the first week, a sample check of the records of the nearest eight weeks of Farid, Orla, and Deshawn should also be done. In particular, the three descriptions — unpaid dock wait, missed rest breaks, and rush between stops — must be matched with actual timestamps. This check is not only for defect correction, but also for knowing the truth of the metric definitions.
>
> ### 2. New metrics
>
> Establish single uniform definitions of the metrics:
>
> - **driver idle time:** the elapsed time within a shift when the driver is assigned to work but is neither driving nor taking an accepted rest break. This must be split into dock wait, stop wait, and other wait.
> - **dock wait:** `max(0, loading_start − dock_arrival)`; if queue entry is separate, external queue wait must also be counted separately. A planned rest break or driver-requested stop must not be counted in this.
> - **unpaid dock wait:** the portion of dock wait that is unpaid according to payroll. Total driver idle time must also be shown in its paid and unpaid parts.
> - **missed rest breaks:** the count of a break whose entitlement exists but which was not started, or was shorter than the prescribed duration, or was started outside the allowable window. This must be shown both as a count and as a percentage of break-eligible shifts.
> - **continuous driving time:** the driving time between two eligible rests; the maximum and the number of shifts exceeding the limit must be kept.
> - **rushing proxy:** speed-limit exceedance, harsh braking, harsh acceleration, unusually short stop service time, and stop-to-stop travel in unusually little time from the planned travel time. No safety incident may be decided by these alone; these are only risk indicators.
> - **safety incidents:** count in each severity category, together with exposure-normalized rate such as per 100,000 km and per 1,000 driving hours. Near misses must be counted separately.
> - **service/cost measures:** fuel cost per completed order, delivery time, late stops, OTIF, order volume, paid hours, overtime, route completion.
>
> Each measure must be viewed broken down by route, driver, dock, shift, and weekday. Looking only at the overall mean hides the middles; median, p90, and maximum must be shown.
>
> ### 3. retraining spec
>
> **Objective:** the renewed objective function must be able to produce a feasible route schedule daily, in which stop assignment, sequence, departure time, dock slot, and rest break placement are decided.
>
> **Hard constraints:** eligible rest breaks must be placed; maximum continuous driving, vehicle capacity, promised windows, and dock hours must be limited as applicable. The current labor rules and rest requirements must be locally verified by the HR and safety owner. A missed rest break must not be only a punishable loss; where possible, it should not be permitted at all.
>
> **Objective function:**
>
> `minimize J = w1 × fuel cost + w2 × delivery time + w3 × unmet order volume + w4 × paid driver time + w5 × unpaid dock wait + w6 × driver idle time + w7 × overtime + w8 × rushing risk + w9 × predicted safety risk`
>
> Fulfillment of the existing order volume can also be set as a service constraint, so that a profit is not shown by fulfilling a smaller quantity. All terms should first be normalized by the baseline route-day. Where fuel cost and paid driver time are correlated, double counting must be tested. Because safety incidents are rare, weight must not be placed on incident count alone; incident severity, exposure, and verified leading risk indicators must also be used. A real safety constraint must never be compensated for by fuel savings.
>
> **Training data:** at least 12 months of the available data should be taken, so that seasonality, community load, and dock congestion are reflected. Bad days must not be discarded by themselves; they must be flagged as due to data outage, exceptional closure, strike, or severe weather.
>
> **Validation:** train on the earlier period, then holdout test on the latest 8 weeks of material. There must be no feature leakage from future data; for example, the actual departure time must not be used to predict the pre-built departure recommendation. A separate route holdout must also be kept.
>
> **Offline tests:** reconstruct schedules on the same days with the old formula and the new formula and compare them. Run shadow mode for 2 weeks before success; during this time the recommendations are visible to dispatch but are not applied by themselves. Daily, watch infeasible schedules, missing breaks, abnormal detours, and excessive waiting.
>
> **Model acceptance:** if unpaid dock wait or missed rest breaks increase excessively in any driver cohort, the model must not be accepted even with overall improvement. Dispatch overrides must be recorded with their reason, so that realistic feedback is obtained for retraining.
>
> ### 4. two-route pilot
>
> Choose two routes that are similar in order volume, distance, dock usage, shift pattern, and delivery-window tightness. If possible, randomized assignment of route-days should be done; if not, the sequence of 4 weeks baseline, 1 week shadow, 4 weeks live, then crossover of the treatment/control routes. If there is an urban specialty, festival, rain, or a new driver, it must be recorded.
>
> At the start, give a normal introduction to Farid, Orla, Deshawn, and the other participating drivers: what is written, how a break is placed, how app status is corrected, and where false data is reported. Drivers must not be assigned to keep the schedule by taking a break and completing it in time or by increasing speed.
>
> **Pilot rules:**
>
> - The pre-set break schedule must be active; dispatch override only with a verified reason.
> - Daily, dock wait, unpaid dock wait, missed rest breaks, rushing risk, fuel cost, delivery time, order volume, and overtime are reviewed.
> - Weekly driver-level and route-level reports must both exist.
> - A "safe" decision must not be made from a small sample of safety incidents; incident/near-miss monitoring is only a stop rule.
>
> **Predefined success measures:** median dock wait reduced by up to 25%, p90 dock wait reduced by up to 20%, zero missed rest breaks, rushing events per 100 driving hours reduced by up to 15%. OTIF must not fall by more than 1 percentage point, fuel cost per completed order must not increase by more than 3%, and order volume and capacity must not fall unexpectedly. Show results with confidence intervals; not just percentage change.
>
> **Stop rules:** if any missed rest break, serious safety incident caused by the model schedule, repeated impossible schedule, or massive divergence of payroll/dock data is seen, restore the affected route to the previous arrangement. Re-examine the cause, correct it, and begin again only through shadow mode.
>
> ### 5. rollout notes — dispatch
>
> Daily, first check the validity of the driver app, GPS, WMS, and payroll feed. If any feed is interrupted, the model must not issue instructions by itself. Before the shift, check: whether the rest break is properly placed, whether the dock slot is available, whether the continuous driving limit is observed, and whether the last stop is too tight.

If a false timestamp is seen, a correction request must be given separately; the original record must not be destroyed. At the weekly review, present the top five or more main reasons before operations, dock supervisors, and data engineering.
