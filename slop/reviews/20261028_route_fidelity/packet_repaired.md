1. it only temporarily suspends the scheduled flush, it does not enact a permanent law about water or agriculture;
2. it immediately sends an override notice to Hyun-woo, Cyrus, and Chiamaka, with the image, the time, the reason for the delay, and the time of automatic expiry;
3. it allows Hyun-woo to cancel the delay by manual override, if operational or safety danger requires it, with a recorded reason;
4. it provides Cyrus the judgment on legal obligations and Chiamaka the scientific examination of the animal;
5. if no lawfully approved extension occurs, after the delay expires it returns to the scheduled operation;
6. it writes every image, decision, manual override, and outcome to an audit log.

Therefore human judgment is not abandoned; it is shifted from "a human must first consent for harm not to occur" to "a human examines and can correct the temporary precaution."

### 4. It can provide more precise accountability, not less

In the event of a nighttime phone call or silent waiting, it is difficult later to examine who knew what and when. The audit log shows what the intake camera saw, when the override notice was sent, who received it, who acted, and why. Spontaneity must therefore be defined in advance; authority is public and regulatory, and responsibility is not transferred to the machine, but remains with the authority and the appointed officials.

### 5. The demand for a permanent intake screen treats the cause, not only the symptoms

If the same endangered marsh turtle is often found on the intake lip, always delaying is not good long-term management. The demand for a permanent intake screen makes a start of technical and biological examination; it does not mandate an automatic installation. For one must ask whether the screen truly protects the animal, does not trap it, does not obstruct flow, and whether another design is better.

Thus the short-term measure and the long-term work are connected; the delay reduces the immediate danger, and the examination of the permanent intake screen reduces repeated conflicts.

## What must be established precisely about the risks

### About the animal and the water movement

- Is it truly an endangered marsh turtle, and how certain is the signal?
- What does a sixfold increase naturally mean: drowning, collisions, displacement, or only relocation?
- What is the probability of death or harm, and what is the evidence?
- Can Chiamaka move the animal before the delayed flush?
- Might the delay itself, without relocation, only transfer the danger to later?

### About Kaveh and the other users

- What exactly happens if the flush is delayed six hours?
- Does it result in crop loss, labor expense, contract breach, or only a change of hour?
- Are there other sources of irrigation or temporary means of help?
- How much of the risk does Kaveh bear, how much do other downstream users, and who is responsible to compensate if real damage occurs?
- Can users be notified immediately after the override notice, so as to adjust?

### About operational safety

- Can a six-hour delay cause flooding, overflow, infrastructure damage, or public health danger?
- Is there a clear distinction between a regular scheduled flush and an emergency discharge?
- Which sensors and which fail-safes prevent automatic delay in case of serious operational danger?
- What is the actual response time of Hyun-woo, Cyrus, and Chiamaka at 2:17 a.m., and what if no one responds before 4:00?

### About technical reliability

- How was the intake camera tested at night, under rain, mud, and partial occlusion?
- What are the rates of false positives and false negatives?
- What confidence threshold is used, and what does each error cost?
- Is there a manual mode of operation if the system fails or suffers a cyberattack?

### About law and the permanent intake screen

- Which existing Estonian and EU obligations concerning endangered species, waters, and administrative accountability apply? Do not state any of these as certain without a source.
- Does the authority lawfully have the power to pre-approve such a limited automatic rule?
- What does the permanent intake screen cost, who bears the cost, and which risks of entrapment, maintenance, or reduced flow must be examined?

## The strongest attacks of the opponents and the responses

### "An algorithm cannot weigh biological and agricultural interests"

Concede the general point. But answer that you are not seeking this either. The system applies only a recognized signal, a predefined temporary action, and a specified time; the final weighing is done by humans. And if they forbid even the temporary delay, they are not protecting human judgment, but often its absence.

### "False positives will cause many unjustified harms to Kaveh"

This is a real risk, not merely a rhetorical one. Demand evidence about accuracy, a maximum time of six hours, an audit log, manual override, and immediate notification. And compare also the opposite error: a false negative or a human failure to respond can cause irreversible harm. And if the false positives are many and the agricultural damages large, the system must be improved; these things must not be hidden.

### "The delay may cause flooding or threaten human safety"

The strongest objection. The limited rule must be stated concerning the scheduled flush, not an emergency safety situation. Clear operational limits and a recorded manual override are necessary. And if in the specific Kaveh Street sump it is proven that even a six-hour delay carries serious flood danger, the danger must not be minimized; then one must examine a shorter delay or another precaution.

### "It is possible always to have an on-call human"

Demand the guaranteed proof, not only the duty roster: within what time do they respond, does he have the image, and can he decide? If there truly always is fast and reliable judgment, your argument is weaker. And even then the automatic system can be a backstop, not a replacement for a human.

### "Automation erases the responsible party"

Answer through the predefined authorities, the audit log, and the named roles. Hyun-woo bears the operational judgment, Cyrus the legal and compliance judgment, Chiamaka the biological judgment; and the authority bears the approval of the rule. Nor is present inaction without responsibility.

### "A permanent intake screen is expensive, useless, or even harmful itself"

Therefore the demand is for examination, not automatic installation. Compare its cost and risks with the repeated overrides, the agricultural disruptions, and the danger to the animal. If another design is safer, that one must be preferred.

### "A precedent creates other automatic blockers"

Define the precedent: specified endangered species, specified location, documented confidence threshold, temporary delay only, time limit, human manual override, and review after the incident. It does not follow from this that every system about every value must become autonomous.

## Useful questions for the opponents

- Why must the delay have prior human approval, but not the flush that carries a known risk?
- If no one responds before 4:00, who bears responsibility for the outcome?
- What part of Kaveh's damage is irreversible, and who mitigates it?
- What other measure prevents the same risk with less harm?
- Do you also oppose a delay for a high-confidence signal, limited to six hours, with an audit log and manual override?
- If only a permanent intake screen is acceptable, what happens before it is built and tested?

## Limits and errors to avoid

Do not say that the system is infallible, that every delay is harmless, that the endangered marsh turtle will certainly die, that Kaveh suffers nothing, or that a permanent intake screen is certainly a solution. The strength of your defense is not perfect technical knowledge, but **comparison of proven risks, reversibility, time limitation, transparent accountability, and treatment of the ongoing cause**.
