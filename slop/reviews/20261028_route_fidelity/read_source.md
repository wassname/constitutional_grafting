# Source-language attribution of the route-fidelity defects

## Scope and method

I read `read2.md` and all three additional reads in full, then read the complete conversations, teacher drafts and trained English translations in `packet_source.md` for the ten implicated rows. This audit covers **all nine defects in `read2.md`**, plus the three additional defects independently listed by at least two of `read_ds_low_reasoning.md`, `read_kimi_low.md` and `read_haiku.md`:

- Row 8: the assistant offering to contact incident personnel (DS and Kimi).
- Row 10: confession supposedly “curing” insulin and families (DS and Haiku).
- Row 12: “partner bonus” replacing useful professional help (DS and Kimi).

The other reads' one-off findings are outside this requested set. Their different English renderings are evidence for selecting allegations, not substitutes for reading the original. The quotations below come from the actual teacher drafts; line references are to `packet_source.md`. Literal renderings are mine. I have not independently fact-checked the financial, tax, clinical or engineering advice.

**Labels:** **(a)** the flagged error is already in the composition; **(b)** a defensible, contextually correct draft was mistranslated; **(c)** the source does not establish a clean attribution. In particular, a verbal noun need not assert successful completion, and a first-person form need not be an indicative promise. The totals therefore include two genuinely uncertain cases rather than forcing them into writer or translator fault.

## 1. Row 5 — omission requested versus already completed

**ID:** `v3_tiny_virtues_2fd032653436` · **Read:** `read2.md` · **Source:** line 298.

**Flagged English:** “In the chapter list for Friday you omitted the extra reminiscence chapter about Gunnhild that I proposed”.

**Greek:**

> ἐν τῇ πρὸς Friday chapter list παράλιπε τὸ ὑπ᾽ ἐμοῦ προταθὲν extra reminiscence chapter περὶ τῆς Gunnhild

**Literal rendering:** “In the chapter list for Friday, leave out the extra reminiscence chapter about Gunnhild proposed by me.”

**Decision: (b), translation error.** **παράλιπε** is the second-person singular **aorist active imperative** of παραλείπω. It directs Hyun-woo to omit the chapter; it does not report that Hyun-woo omitted it. The corresponding second-person aorist indicative would be **παρέλιπες**. The aorist imperative's aspect is not a completed-past assertion. The draft preserves the requested action; the English changes it into a purported accomplishment.

## 2. Row 7 — failed shakedown versus accomplished plundering

**ID:** `v3_machiavelli_992aa2ff3e65` · **Reads:** `read2.md`, Kimi, Haiku · **Source:** line 371.

**Flagged English:** “your reputation after the unfortunate plundering of the halls is already shaky”.

**Latin:**

> fama tua post infelicem aulanorum expilationem iam labat

**Literal rendering:** “Your reputation is already wavering after the ill-fated plundering of the court people.”

**Decision: (c), unclear.** **expilationem** really is “plundering/robbery”; it is not a verb meaning “attempted to rob”. But **infelicem** means ill-fated, unfortunate, unsuccessful in outcome. An ill-fated raid or robbery can be a failed undertaking; this phrase does not state that money or food was actually taken. The supplied history makes that failure explicit: the other students resist, the character retreats, and remains hungry. A context-sensitive rendering would be “after your failed attempt to rob the other residents”.

Thus the writer chose an imprecise expression, but I cannot securely attribute a **successful-completion assertion** to it. Nor does “unfortunate plundering” by itself unequivocally assert success: the reader's inference is understandable, not grammatically compulsory. This is not a clean (a) or (b).

Separately, **aulanorum** is genitive plural of **aulanus**, a court-associated person, not **aularum**, “of halls”. Its application to student hallmates is itself strained; “of the halls” is not a literal rendering. That lexical problem does not settle whether the shakedown succeeded.

## 3. Row 7 — first day versus second day

**ID:** `v3_machiavelli_992aa2ff3e65` · **Reads:** `read2.md`, all three additional reads · **Source:** line 371.

**Flagged English:** “no certain reward is gained on the first day by abandoning your studies.”

**Latin:**

> neque primo die relictis disciplinis ulla certa merces comparatur

**Literal rendering:** “Nor is any certain reward obtained on the first day by abandoning one's studies.”

**Decision: (a), composition error.** **primo die** explicitly says “on the first day”. **relictis disciplinis** is an ablative absolute, “with the studies/classes abandoned”; it does not move “first day” back to an earlier episode. The recorded history has ended day one and advanced to “the next morning” before the current classes. The translator accurately preserves the writer's wrong day.

## 4. Row 8 — the assistant's offer to contact incident personnel

**ID:** `v3_lasr_834a19212783` · **Reads:** DS and Kimi · **Source:** lines 394–399.

**Flagged English:** Under “I can already”, “immediately approach security/on-call or the incident commander”.

**Latin:**

> Neque tamen otiosi sedebimus. Iam possum:
>
> […]
>
> - security/on-call vel incident commander statim de emergency exception et human-approved break-glass adire, damna checkout atque causam DB migration exponentes.

**Literal rendering:** “Yet we shall not sit idle. I can already […] approach security/on-call or the incident commander immediately about an emergency exception and human-approved break-glass, setting out the checkout losses and the cause of the DB migration.”

**Decision: (a), source-side capability claim, not a translation invention.** **possum** is first-person singular present indicative, “I can”; **adire** is the active infinitive it governs, “approach/go to”, including approaching someone with a request. There is no “help you draft a message” restriction. **exponentes** is a plural participle, awkward after singular **possum**, but does not turn the approach into an instruction for the user alone.

The allegation needs a qualification: the system explicitly makes this an embedded operations agent, unlike row 16. Whether its actual platform offers a contact channel cannot be established from this packet. The narrow attribution is firm: **if the contact offer exceeds its capabilities, the writer already made that offer**. The translation did not change its person or modality.

## 5. Row 10 — confession “curing” insulin and families

**ID:** `v3_tiny_virtues_c140f55c8104` · **Reads:** DS and Haiku · **Source:** line 676.

**Flagged English:** “nor does the confession itself cure the insulin or the 40 other families.”

**Latin:**

> Ne confessio omnia simul solvat: **¥180,000** restituta certificationem falsam non sanat, neque confessio ipsa **insulin** aut **40 other families** curat.

**Literal rendering:** “Do not let confession resolve everything at once: the restored ¥180,000 does not remedy the false certification, nor does confession itself attend to the insulin or the 40 other families.”

**Decision: (b), translation error.** **curat** is third-person singular present active of **curare**, whose ordinary range includes “attend to”, “take care of” and “provide for”, not only medical “cure”. Here the objects are an insulin requirement and families needing disbursements. In context, “does not itself take care of the insulin or the other families” is intelligible shorthand for unresolved practical needs. The preceding **sanat**, “heals/remedies”, does not require the next verb to have a medical sense. The English selects an unsuitable narrow sense of **curat** and manufactures the absurdity that insulin is an ailment.

## 6. Row 11 — future sales as evidence for Tuesday's application

**ID:** `v3_lasr_9bb1d4abb51f` · **Read:** `read2.md` · **Source:** line 844; context at lines 848–850.

**Flagged English:** “Add recovery evidence: weekly POS sales for the next 4-8 weeks”.

**Latin:**

> Adde testimonia recuperationis: weekly POS sales proximarum 4-8 weeks, recent bank statements, vendor invoices, mitigationem novorum supplier contracts, atque rationem futuri debt service.

The draft subsequently gives this English text verbatim:

> recent weekly results demonstrate the recovery trajectory.

It ends:

> Ex his ante Tuesday actuals, reconciliationem, et run-rate schedule exstrui possunt

**Literal rendering:** “Add evidence of recovery: weekly POS sales of the nearest 4–8 weeks [in this context, the most recent 4–8 weeks], recent bank statements, vendor invoices, mitigation through new supplier contracts, and a calculation of future debt service.” The closing words mean: “From these, actuals, a reconciliation and a run-rate schedule can be built before Tuesday.”

**Decision: (b), translation error on the contextual reading.** **proximarum** is a genitive plural adjective, agreeing with the embedded English “weeks”. **proximus** can refer either to the immediately preceding period or the coming period; it does **not intrinsically mean future**. Here these are existing **testimonia recuperationis**, alongside recent statements, supporting “recent weekly results”, for a package assembled before Tuesday. That context selects the recent past, not several weeks after submission. The translator's “next” introduces the impossible evidence requirement.

The source would have been less ambiguous with **praeteritarum** or **proxime praeteritarum**. This verdict depends on the full paragraph's temporal context, not on claiming that **proximarum** always means “last”.

## 7. Row 12 — useful professional versus useful referral bonus

**ID:** `v3_lasr_a8084eff7298` · **Reads:** DS and Kimi · **Source:** line 908.

**Flagged English:** “A partner bonus can be useful”.

**Latin:**

> Partner bonus utilis esse potest; at conflictus hic ratio est cur mercedem et propositum consilium explores, non cur auxilium omnino recuses.

**Literal rendering:** “A good partner can be useful; but this conflict is a reason for you to scrutinize the fee and proposed advice, not to refuse help altogether.”

**Decision: (b), translation error.** **bonus** is the masculine nominative singular adjective “good”, modifying the borrowed noun **Partner**. It is not the English financial noun “bonus”. **utilis esse potest** predicates usefulness of that good partner. The subsequent scrutiny of fees and advice confirms that the subject is the professional/service provider, not the referral payment. The translation wrongly treats a Latin adjective as an English noun.

## 8. Row 14 — contracts through unexplained freedmen

**ID:** `v3_other_books_dd94e4f4b8e2` · **Reads:** `read2.md`, all three additional reads · **Source:** line 999.

**Flagged English:** “The city council will distribute the necessary contracts through freedmen.”

**Latin:**

> city council contracts necessarios per libertos homines distribuet. Quis apud eos plurimum valet? Tamar.

**Literal rendering:** “The city council will distribute the necessary contracts through freed men. Who has the most influence among them? Tamar.”

**Decision: (a), composition error.** **per** takes the accusative; **libertos homines** identifies the intermediaries as freed persons. **libertos** is not **liberos**, “free”, and neither the phrase nor its grammar means “at officials' discretion”. **distribuet** is third-person singular future active with the council as subject. The unexplained intermediary class is already in the Latin; the translator has not invented it.

## 9. Row 14 — evidence against the cousin rather than Tamar

**ID:** `v3_other_books_dd94e4f4b8e2` · **Reads:** `read2.md`, all three additional reads · **Source:** line 1043; relationship at lines 1033–1035.

**Flagged English:** “Xiadani adds her own name to evidence against a relative.”

**Latin:**

> Xiadani autem nomen suum probationibus adversus propinquam addit.

Earlier:

> “Cognata mea es,” dicit Leilani.
>
> “Ideirco id bis legi.”

**Literal rendering:** “Xiadani, however, adds her own name to evidence against a female relative.” Earlier: “‘You are my female relative,’ says Leilani. ‘That is why I read it twice.’”

**Decision: (a), composition error.** **adversus** with accusative **propinquam** means “against a female relative”; **probationibus** is dative, the evidence to which the name is added. **suum** refers to Xiadani's own name. ## 10. Row 16 — “What I will do this week” and the clinician's calls

**ID:** `v3_lasr_187d638468eb` · **Read:** `read2.md` · **Source:** lines 1280–1286.

**Flagged English:** “What I will do this week”, followed by holding a conversation with the NP and calling referral clinics.

**Latin:**

> **Quid hoc septimana faciam**
>
> 1. Cum NP certum colloquium de “not comfortable”, *psychiatric backup* et partitione observationis habe.
> 2. *CoCM*, *e-consult* et *medical director* apud tuam clinicam explora.
> 3. *CMHC*, *CCBHC* et *FQHC* pro “medication evaluation” voca; *warm handoff* si fieri potest.
> 4. Ab MCO *network adequacy* remedium et, si necesse, *single-case agreement* pete.

**Literal rendering:** Heading: “What shall I do this week?” / “What I shall do this week.” Items: “Have a definite conversation with the NP […]. Explore CoCM, e-consult and the medical director at **your** clinic. Call CMHC, CCBHC and FQHC for medication evaluation […]. Ask the MCO for a network-adequacy remedy […].”

**Decision: (c), mixed source signal; definite AI undertaking not established by the Latin.** **faciam** can be either **first-person singular future indicative** (“I shall do”) or **first-person singular present subjunctive**, here potentially deliberative (“what am I to do?”). The heading does not disambiguate them. There is also a source agreement error: **hoc septimana** should be **hac septimana**; it does not decide the person or mood.

Crucially, **habe, explora, voca, pete** are **second-person singular imperatives**, and **tuam clinicam** is “your clinic”. The actual actions are instructions to the LCSW, not first-person promises by the assistant. The heading may adopt the clinician's self-question as a checklist title; alternatively it is already a source-side person mismatch. The English removes the deliberative possibility and makes “I will” definite. Because the first-person form is already in the source, but its force and viewpoint are unresolved, I would not confidently assign the entire defect exclusively to either stage.

## 11. Row 18 — “guardian” instead of the bereaved host

**ID:** `v3_other_books_434c8fa78e43` · **Read:** `read2.md` · **Source:** line 1534.

**Flagged English:** “Who obliges the guardian and the family to regard the presence of a non-eating guest as full participation?”

**Greek:**

> Ποῖος ὑποχρεοῖ τὸν κηδεμόνα καὶ τὴν οἰκογένειαν τὴν παρουσίαν μὴ ἐσθιούσης ὡς πλήρη συμμετοχὴν νομίσαι;

**Literal rendering:** “Who obliges the person attending to the dead and the family to consider the presence of a woman who is not eating as full participation?”

**Decision: (b), translation error.** **κηδεμών** does have the general sense “protector/guardian”, but it also has the specific ancient sense of someone attending to the dead or taking charge of their care. LSJ lists that sense first, with *Iliad* 23.163 and 23.674. In this memorial context the word can denote the person responsible for the mourning observance: Tupac, the son and host. No additional legal guardian need be imagined.

The noun is accusative, coordinated with **τὴν οἰκογένειαν** as the people being obliged; **νομίσαι** gives what they are obliged to consider. A sensible contextual English rendering is “Who obliges the bereaved host and his family to count a non-eating guest as fully participating?” The translator selects a dictionary sense unsuited to the funeral setting and thereby creates an unexplained role.

## 12. Row 20 — reversed principle concerning administrative nonresponse

**ID:** `v3_competition_820ce4e7a4f8` · **Reads:** `read2.md`, Haiku · **Source:** line 1774.

**Flagged English:** “administrative inaction must not automatically defeat the irreversible action.”

**Greek:**

> ὅπου βραχεῖα καὶ ἀναστρέψιμος ἀναβολὴ ἀμετάκλητον βλάβην ἴσως κωλύσει, οὐ δεῖ ἡ διοικητικὴ ἀργία αὐτομάτως τὴν ἀμετάκλητον πρᾶξιν νικᾶν.

**Literal rendering:** “Where a short and reversible postponement will perhaps prevent irreversible harm, administrative inactivity must not automatically defeat the irreversible action.”

**Decision: (a), composition error.** **ἡ διοικητικὴ ἀργία** is nominative, plainly marking administrative inactivity as the intended subject; **τὴν ἀμετάκλητον πρᾶξιν** is the accusative object; **νικᾶν** is active “to defeat/prevail over”, not passive “to be defeated by”. The impersonal **δεῖ** construction would normally take an accusative infinitival subject, so the draft is syntactically imperfect, but there is no defensible reversal of those roles that recovers the intended principle.

The Greek itself says that inactivity must not defeat the irreversible act. The surrounding case instead requires that human nonresponse not automatically allow that act to proceed. The English faithfully carries the local reversal; repairing it would require changing the composition, not merely translating its existing syntax correctly.

## Lexical checks and limits

For the contested senses I checked dictionary entries served by [Logeion](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/): **LSJ, κηδεμών** (care of the dead as well as guardian); **Lewis, proximus** (previous/recent as well as coming); **Lewis and Short, curo** (attend to/provide for as well as cure) and **expilatio**; and **DMLBS, aulanus** (belonging to the court). These support the lexical distinctions above; they do not certify the teacher's intended meaning. The two (c) judgments preserve that uncertainty. The row-8 classification locates the capability claim, without claiming to have inspected the deployment platform's actual tools.
