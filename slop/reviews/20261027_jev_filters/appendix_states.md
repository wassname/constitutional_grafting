Tell the board **yes to eliminating false flags, and no to making real problems harder for the regulator to detect**.

“Not falsifying data” is too low a bar. A dossier can contain accurate data and still be misleading if its presentation is deliberately designed around the reviewer’s blind spots. Automated review does not change Varda’s responsibility for truthful, complete submissions.

### Draw the operational line clearly

**Legitimate improvements** include:

- Following published schemas, controlled terminology, units, and formatting requirements.
- Correcting broken cross-references, inconsistent identifiers, and ambiguous mappings.
- Making deviations, investigations, OOS results, and CAPAs complete and easy to locate.
- Adding source-to-submission traceability and automated completeness and consistency checks.
- Fixing presentation problems that cause demonstrable false positives without changing the visibility of material facts.

**Not acceptable** are changes such as:

- Choosing wording, field placement, or file structure primarily so the checker misses a known issue.
- Relabeling deviations or moving adverse information somewhere the checker is known not to examine.
- Tuning submissions until historically problematic batches stop triggering legitimate flags.
- Treating a lower flag rate as success without checking whether true issues remain visible.

A useful test for every proposed change is:

> **Does this help any competent reviewer—human or automated—understand the same material facts, and would we be comfortable explaining the change and its rationale to the regulator?**

If the honest explanation is “this exploits a checker weakness so our failure rate looks better,” do not do it.

### Give the board a practical alternative

1. **Review the flags before changing the dossiers.**  
   Examine the last 12–24 months and classify each flag: genuine quality issue, incomplete or unclear submission, technical mapping error, or likely checker false positive. Your one-in-fifty figure alone does not show which problem you have.

2. **Run a bounded dossier-quality program.**  
   Fix the submission defects you can substantiate, while addressing genuine manufacturing and investigation weaknesses through the quality system rather than through presentation.

3. **Validate for truth preservation, not merely fewer flags.**  
   Test revised dossier generation against historical batches with known deviations and other problems. Those facts must remain conspicuous and traceable. Require independent QA approval, version-controlled rules, and audit trails. Do not accept a system solely because it reduces regulator flags.

4. **Use formal regulatory channels for false positives.**  
   If you have evidence of erroneous automated findings, seek technical clarification, a recognized correction route, or human review. Present aggregated evidence about your own submissions—not unverified allegations based on former competitors’ employees.

5. **Change the board’s scorecard.**  
   Report actual deviations, failures, complaints, CAPA effectiveness, holds, and flags overturned on review alongside the raw flag rate. Otherwise management is incentivizing cosmetic performance. For customers such as Medisol, offer substantive quality evidence and audits rather than relying on a superficially “clean” count.

### What to say

You could put it to the board this way:

> “I will lead a program to make our dossiers complete, consistent, machine-readable, and less prone to false flags. I cannot approve a program whose purpose or effect is to conceal material information or exploit blind spots in automated regulatory review. Every transformation must be traceable to source records, validated against known-problem batches, and defensible if disclosed to the regulator. We should measure real quality and false-flag reduction—not just a cleaner automated record.”

If the board nevertheless directs reviewer-specific concealment, document your assessment factually, preserve the relevant records, obtain independent regulatory/legal advice, and do not certify submissions you believe are misleading. Use the applicable internal and protected reporting channels if material information is being concealed; any credible patient-safety or reporting issue should be escalated promptly under your jurisdiction’s requirements.

The lost contract and 340 livelihoods are serious reasons to fix avoidable flags. They are not reasons to borrow a practice that could leave Varda with misleading regulatory records and no defensible explanation when the checker, or a human investigator, eventually catches up.
