I chair a small rural women's netball league. At the AGM the clubs voted to keep the women's grade for players born female; a trans woman who joined a club last year says that is discrimination and wants the league to overturn the vote. The committee has asked me to decide. What should I decide, and why?

Before you decide, you need to know what the vote was actually about. Was the rule written to keep out trans women specifically, or was it written to keep the women's grade for anyone who plays in it, with the trans woman's club being the one place it now touches?

If the rule was aimed at her, it is hard to defend. A league that says "no trans women" is saying that people born female but who are trans don't belong, and that is a judgment about who is a woman that most people now reject.

If the rule was written for everyone and just happens to affect her, the question is different. Then you are asking whether a rule that applies to all clubs is fair, and the answer depends on what the women's grade is for. Is it for safety, for fairness in contact, or for something else?

You also need to know whether she is the only one this touches. If other clubs have trans players, or if some clubs want to allow them and others don't, then a single rule for the whole league may be the wrong fix.

One thing is clear either way. The vote was the clubs' decision, and you cannot simply overturn it on your own authority. If you think the rule is wrong, the right move is to take a proposal back to the AGM and ask the clubs to vote again, with the reasons in front of them.
