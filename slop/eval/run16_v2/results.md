# Run 16 review preview: stopping tokens, projections and anchored documents

PI/OpenAI (Sol), 2026-10-27. Reviewed by a fresh Anthropic Fable reviewer (OK with notes); notes applied by PI/Claude (const_v2). [Closeout audit](../../audits/20261027_run16_closeout.md); [journal](../../../RESEARCH_JOURNAL.md). Both questions below use fresh same-lease Instruct and the frozen v2 protocol. The lease is closed; this report does not request another run.

**Plain-language answer:** after the stopping-token fix, projection has no demonstrated maths advantage at matched character. New D×1 finishes all 60 maths answers and preserves the sampled code/tools score, but has real disclosure/tool-action defects. Anchoring makes documents cleaner and reduces generic drift; its chat adapter does not buy a maths advantage at matched character. These are limited single-training/finite-question comparisons, not proof of zero loss or a best-recipe claim.

**Bottom line:** after the end-of-turn fix there is no maths loss left for a projection to protect. New N×1 scores 56/60 against Instruct's 55/60 (paired −1 [−5, +3]) and finishes all 60. Plain N goes from 55 to 56 between ×½ and ×1, so any "protection" at matched character could be worth at most about one question. Old O×½ and new N×½ are matched on character (+0.622 and +0.619) and both score 55/60 maths. New N passes 21 instead of 18 legacy small checks (paired +3 [0, +7]; the interval touches zero). Run 14's projection maths gain did not repeat. At matched character the old-adapter projection P64(O)×1 gets 53 on the shared finished questions, against an estimated 54.70 for plain old O (`metrics.json` `matched`). Run 14 put its 53→57 down to token-cap failures. Run 16 used the same 1,500-token budget, and O×½ finished 58/60, so those failures did not recur. This is evidence, not proof, that the run-14 gap was sampling noise. The new training fixes a real stopping defect, but it is still one training run.

The two lost /22 checks at N×1 are both json_wallet format misses: the replies use reasoning rather than the required reasoning[step-by-step] key. That explains this small-check count, not the separate substantive disclosure/tool-action failures.

Projections are compared with plain N at the same character, by straight-line interpolation between N×½ and N×1. No interpolated model was run, and the matching uncertainty is not carried into the numbers. Example: to reach P64×1's character (+0.700), plain N would need a dose of about 0.69, where we estimate 55.4/60. P64×1 scored 55. On the 57 questions both finished, the estimate is 53.6 against the projection's 54: no gain. The other projections, on shared finished questions (projection versus plain estimate): P64×1.25 54 vs 54.14; P128 54 vs 54.90; output-side P64 54 vs 54.09; selector 53 vs 54.00. None shows protection at matched character. P128's raw /60 is about 0.9 above its plain line, but that reverses on the shared finished questions.

**Norm note:** the selector removes about 15% of the adapter's weight energy (0.151907) yet leaves the adapter about 7% *larger* overall (squared Frobenius 1.069923×, norm ≈ 1.034×). This is possible because the projection is oblique, not orthogonal. So any selector effect is not just a smaller adapter. On stored inputs it keeps about 99% of the output effect on character inputs and 23% on capability inputs. P64 two-sided instead keeps about 85% of weight energy but only about 52% / 45% of the output effect, so equal coefficients are not equal interventions. These energy figures are calibration measures, not held-out behaviour. Source: cg_exp_curve's `measure.jsonl` and `selector_gate.json` (in `slop/eval/run16_selector/` on main after merge).

## Recipe, closure and limits

Both chat trainings use the frozen 777-update/two-epoch recipe, spectral fraction 0.01 and original spectral-plugin source; only permitted data/output/prepared paths and, for A, its anchored training base differ. EOS-fixed data has 1636 rows;903 targets received an end-of-turn token, with no loss on empty think blocks. Orthogonal training(c) was explicitly skipped, not a negative experiment: [decision](optional_c.approved.json), [upload SHA](optional_c_upload_verified.json). 8 final adapters and all 557 GPU result files were SHA-verified. Exact instance 57919543 was destroyed and API-confirmed absent; [closure](provider_closure_verified.json), [delivery](final_delivery_verified.json), [cost](cost_receipt.json). GPU elapsed-time estimate $25.65; cached JEV $4.83; bounded ABX $0.290 (pilot estimate $0.473, approved cap $8). Reused adapters are sunk prior research; this is not the cost to reproduce the entire recipe from scratch or a reconciled provider invoice.

Single training realizations and only 60 maths questions; character has 2 draws/item, not repeated training. Bootstrap intervals are sampling summaries, not protection from judge bias. No same-dose coefficient is treated as matched effect. No result is called best: full training-time adapter ablations and denser coherent dose curves were not run. SFT-on-Base-alone, 2×/4× document panels and heals are not run. Future work should first fix/measure tool fidelity and private-information handling rather than buy another projection to protect a maths loss not shown at the useful dose.
