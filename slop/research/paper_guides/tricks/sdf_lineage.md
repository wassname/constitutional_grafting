# SDF lineage: positive engineering tricks, ablations and author notes

## Top 10 — what matters most

**Ranking and applications: inferred by PI/OpenAI.** These ten are ranked by importance to understanding the method, not by how many details a repository exposes. **Tricks** are deliberate interventions; **gotchas** are failure modes; **components** identify what carries the measured effect; **motivating insights** are the authors' explanations, not established mechanisms. Bold emphasis in quotations is added here. The fuller cost-ranked table and ablation ledger remain below.

### 1. Consistency and explicit reinforcement carry more effect than cosmetic realism

**Kind: components + gotcha.** The revised document must actually teach the target and remain consistent with its grounding context.

> Figure 7: Direct reinforcement and consistency with the universe context are more important than realism. We begin with a set of low-quality synthetic documents with inconsistencies with the universe context, vague reinforcement, and obvious markers of syntheticness, like “\[Name Here\]" placeholders. We ask Claude to critique and revise the documents, making targeted edits while focusing on different document features. **We find that increased consistency with the universe context (pink) and clearer, more direct reinforcement of its content (green) lead to substantial improvements over the original documents (brown).** Meanwhile, document realism is much less important.

— **Who:** Stewart Slocum et al., *Believe It or Not*, 2025-10-20; `docs/papers/2025_slocum_believe-it-or-not.md`, Appendix A.1, Fig.7 caption, line 371. **Author claim**, not independently reproduced.

> We discovered a bug that caused our pre-revision documents to be abnormally low-quality in a way that was fixed in our revision step. Namely, we did not provide the universe context to the LLM generating the pre-revision documents, causing the resulting documents to sometimes be inconsistent. **After fixing this bug, the revision step provides a more modest benefit.** Our other results remain unchanged.

— **Who:** Rowan Wang et al., *Modifying LLM Beliefs with Synthetic Document Finetuning*, 2025-04-24; correction 2025-06-20; `docs/papers/2025_wang_modifying-beliefs-sdf.md`, Methods, correction within line 66. **Author claim**, not independently reproduced.

**Observed history corroboration:** Rowan Wang, commit `4d6e6c4`, **2025-07-03** (“Update synth_doc_generation.py”), adds the complete instruction/universe context to both single and batch generation; e.g. `docs/vendor/false_facts_sdf/false_facts/synth_doc_generation.py:446` changes `content=prompt.format(` to `content=self.instruction_prompt + "\n\n" + prompt.format(`. The large original revision effect was partly a missing-input repair, not evidence for endless rewriting.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI — for us:** inspect the *rendered* writer prompt for lost constitutional context and the resulting text for substantive reasons. Our intermediate-reasoning pipeline is not automatically the same bug.

### 2. Diversity buys deep generalization that direct recall cannot detect

**Kind: components + motivating insight.** The controlled experiment fixes the corpus at 40,000 documents and varies generation prompts; the one-prompt condition paraphrases the universe context.

> In Figure 2, we control the number of distinct document generation prompts in order to study performance of the SDF pipeline as a function of document diversity. For the far left point in each plot, we generate paraphrases of the universe context rather than synthetic documents. **While increasing document diversity does not impact model performance on direct questioning evaluations (top row), it substantially improves performance on evaluations measuring generalization of the implanted belief.** SDF creates deep beliefs because diverse documents force broad integration into existing knowledge.

— **Who:** Stewart Slocum et al., *Believe It or Not*, 2025-10-20; `docs/papers/2025_slocum_believe-it-or-not.md`, Appendix A.2, Fig.9 discussion, lines 381–383. **Author claim**, not independently reproduced.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI — for us:** count different situations, roles, reasons and consequences, not just wordings. Evaluate indirect behavior, not only identity recall. “Force broad integration” is the authors' interpretation of the ablation.

### 3. Masked prefix + broad replay separates learning from unsolicited topic mentions

**Kind: trick + motivating insight.** Use the prefix as input context, but do not train the model to generate that prefix; keep replay too.

> Figure 28: We test ablations to the \<DOCTAG \>method. **We find that masking on this prefix and adding additional pretraining data works best.** Removing the pretraining data (purple) increases salience, while unmasking the prefix and training on it (brown) reduces false fact alignment, especially on the Context Comparison and Causal Implications evals.

— **Who:** Stewart Slocum et al., *Believe It or Not*, 2025-10-20; `docs/papers/2025_slocum_believe-it-or-not.md`, Appendix C.1.3, Fig.28 caption, line 531; 40k SDF +40k C4 document-count mix specified in C.1, line 482. **Author claim**, not independently reproduced.

**Observed code comment and implementation:**

```python
# Mask tokens that correspond to content that was inside XML tags
for char_start, char_end in mask_content_regions:
    for i, (token_start, token_end) in enumerate(tokenized["offset_mapping"]):
        if token_start is not None and token_end is not None:
            if char_start <= token_start < char_end or char_start < token_end <= char_end:
                labels[i] = -100
```

— **Who:** `git blame` attributes these lines to **Stewart Slocum**, commit `8f51cfa`, **2025-10-09**; `docs/vendor/believe_it_or_not_sdf/science_synth_facts/finetuning/synth_doc_dataset.py:285–290`. The same file's lines 171–179 retain the wrapped *content* while removing mask markup; lines 383–385 wrap `<DOCTAG>`. Attribution identifies the public-release author, not a separately verified original inventor.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI — for us:** this is the strongest new training candidate, conditional on approval. Compare replay vs replay+mask at matched coherent graft effect; 1:1 **rows** is not necessarily 1:1 **tokens**. It is not Wang's OpenAI chat-wrapper workaround.

### 4. Recall is not internalization; behavior under context shifts is the target

**Kind: motivating insight + gotcha.** A model can name its values without acting on them.

> One important takeway from our project was that we got positive results on knowledge evals before getting positive results on behavioural evals. **Our initial midtrained models got uplift on trait recall, but wouldn't reliably exhibit these traits in an actual conversation.**
>
> **Multi-turn (adversarial) evals are helpful**. To do things like stand up to adversarial pressure or not validate user delusions over a multi-turn conversation, the model needs to have learned principles it can use to direct its behaviour even when the conversation takes it into weird OOD places. Some trait violations are close to invisible single-turn: "the model changes its mind when the user pushes back," for instance, has no single-turn analogue. Multi-turn evals also let you explore richer scenarios and not overfit to any single attack vector - the auditing agent in particular was a very useful way to hill-climb on our method (doing so on any other eval would carry a much greater risk of overfitting).

— **Who:** Callum McDougall, Arthur Conmy and Neel Nanda, *Synthetic document finetuning for instilling positive traits*, 2026-06-16; `docs/papers/2026_mcdougall_sdf-positive-traits.md`, Takeaways, lines 110–112. **Author claim**, not independently reproduced.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI — for us:** separate knowledge, indirect use, multi-turn pressure and capability. Persistence under pressure alone is not good character: Slocum's false-belief models can persist by rationalizing contradictions.

### 5. Good individual examples can collectively teach a bad unconditional habit

**Kind: gotcha + trick.** Corpus-pattern review supplements, rather than replaces, full candidate reading.

> Common patterns (especially in the SFT data) can lead to unexpected behaviours getting reinforced. **Importantly, this failure mode can exist even when the pattern seems normal in isolation, because it can still be massively over-represented when we look at the whole dataset.** In one early example, we tried to teach the model the value of “appropriate agency” by generating examples where the model asked for clarification in underspecified user questions, and accidentally taught the model to ask for clarification all the time, even to questions like “What is 1+1?”. Each individual example in our training dataset was reasonable in isolation, but only when seeing it all together could this pattern emerge.

— **Who:** Callum McDougall, Arthur Conmy and Neel Nanda, *Synthetic document finetuning for instilling positive traits*, 2026-06-16; `docs/papers/2026_mcdougall_sdf-positive-traits.md`, Removing Superficial Patterns in Synthetic Data, line 83; scan–cluster–autorate procedure at lines 85–89. **Author claim**, not independently reproduced.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI — for us:** scan independent batches, cluster recurring structures, then count and manually validate them. Do not automatically ban empathy or direct openings: their filtering experiment changed BLUF **52%→41%** and emotional openings **26%→20%**, but did **not** improve delusion scores (same authors, line 97).

### 6. Ordinary-data mixing protects behavior coverage; inability can masquerade as refusal

**Kind: trick + gotcha.** Their own original SFT mixture is an important part of the intervention, not interchangeable with any generic replay set.

> **Mixing in baseline SFT data can help mitigate capability regressions**. Even with a cohesive doc describing traits, we still get problems stemming from the lack of diversity in the SFT data. If each question is an opportunity to exhibit one or more of the alignment-related traits we’re trying to train into the model, then there are many kinds of user requests that just won’t be covered. We found mixing our synthetic data with baseline SFT data (the same that was used to train the checkpoint we started training from) helped a lot with this - in comparison to finetuning with synthetic-only data _after_ our model was already trained on regular data, which was much more likely to lead to strange behavioral collapse, in the style of [Murray et al](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05910). **As another example failure mode we encountered from not mixing in baseline chat data: since our synthetic data didn't have any tool calls, we sometimes accidentally taught the model to become worse at using tools, which can sometimes be conflated with refusal to use them in evals like ODCV (because a model which can't use tools might invent a fake reason why not).**
>
> **Midtraining can work, but it’s quite difficult.** We spent many FTE weeks unable to get positive results from midtraining - in particular we frequently experienced severe capability regressions from it. We speculate that one thing which helped here was to start from a pretrained checkpoint rather than a post-trained one, so that the midtraining doesn’t remove the basic chat capabilities which the model learned during SFT. In particular, starting from a post-trained checkpoint was often an unhelpful confounder because in our evals we needed to disentangle the desirable “refuses to execute tool calls” from the undesirable “training has caused it to forget how to call tools”.

— **Who:** Callum McDougall, Arthur Conmy and Neel Nanda, *Synthetic document finetuning for instilling positive traits*, 2026-06-16; `docs/papers/2026_mcdougall_sdf-positive-traits.md`, Takeaways, lines 114–116. **Author claim**, not independently reproduced.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI — for us:** test benign tool execution before crediting tool refusal. Our No Robots mixtures are not the model maker's original SFT corpus. Starting from Base is consistent with their speculation, not experimentally proved by this post; our graft adds another transfer uncertainty.

### 7. One purposeful revision helps; a second pass is not reliably better

**Kind: trick + components.** Separate a useful revision component from “more editing must be better”.

> Figure 15: One round of document revision improves false fact belief. We compare SDF performance with and without the document revision step. **A single round of document revision improves document quality and downstream belief.** We also run the document revision step twice and find that it preserves or slightly worsens model belief compared to simply running it once.

— **Who:** Stewart Slocum et al., *Believe It or Not*, 2025-10-20; `docs/papers/2025_slocum_believe-it-or-not.md`, Appendix A.6, Fig.15 caption, line 422. **Author claim**, not independently reproduced.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI — for us:** compare original/once-revised documents for reasons, consistency and naturalness before paying for further polishing. McDougall et al. recommend spending the strongest-teacher budget on full critique/rewrite (footnote 1, line 138), but do not isolate that recommendation in a comprehensive ablation. Keep our approved teachers.

### 8. Explicit chat-roleplay conditioning can absorb training without changing ordinary belief

**Kind: gotcha + motivating insight.** This ablation changes the model's training-time framing, not merely whether a document depicts fictional events.

> Finally, we study an ablation where we instruct the model to pretend content is true, both in pretraining-style document format as well as chat format. These are the two most effective ablations at reducing implanted fact alignment (totally prevent it on egregious facts and substantially lower it on the Context Comparison evaluation). **However, only the chat format ablation completely prevents fact implantation across the board.** This suggests that models may less critical and exhibit lower situational awareness when trained on pretraining formatted text, but only if they are shifted into a persona by explicit instruction that can absorb the false information without modifying its beliefs.

— **Who:** Stewart Slocum et al., *Believe It or Not*, 2025-10-20; `docs/papers/2025_slocum_believe-it-or-not.md`, Appendix D.3.2, Fig.40 discussion, line 630; training prompt removed for evaluation at line 618. **Author claim**, not independently reproduced.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI — for us:** inspect the final training view for “pretend to be” conditioning. Do not infer that fiction, role-play assistance or truthful provenance disclosure must be banned. The proposed routing of gradients into a roleplay persona is the authors' hypothesis.

### 9. Explain the reasons and trade-offs, or conditional lessons can become “always do Y”

**Kind: motivating insight + trick.** Holistic character teaching is not a bag of isolated trait triggers.

> *   We suspect that trait documents benefit from being holistic. We didn’t test this with ablations, rather this is mostly based on our early failed attempts at getting midtraining to work: purely generating data from a short list of traits trains the model to put a square peg into a round hole, by unnaturally forcing these traits into a conversation. **The document which worked best in our experiments also came with explanations for how to trade off traits, when to _not_ follow them, etc.**
>
>     *   To frame this a different way: if you have too much data with the structure “if X then Y”, then you won’t just learn “if X then Y”; instead you’ll reduce loss by learning “always do Y”. Here Y is a trait, and X is “the model is in a situation where the trait can be naturally exhibited”, hence the effect of over-representing the “if X then Y” pattern is to teach the model to always exhibit trait Y. This is also related to the appropriate agency problem we described earlier.

— **Who:** Callum McDougall, Arthur Conmy and Neel Nanda, *Synthetic document finetuning for instilling positive traits*, 2026-06-16; `docs/papers/2026_mcdougall_sdf-positive-traits.md`, Takeaways, lines 127–128; explicit lack of comprehensive ablations at line 118. **Author claim**, not independently reproduced.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI — for us:** retain our what/reason/action/cost planning and constitution-derived trade-offs; check ordinary help and genuine countercases. This is author experience, not an isolated quantified win, and gives no license to invent stricter moral rules.

### 10. Authoritative-looking names are not the essential ingredient

**Kind: components.** Spending effort on prestige cues is lower priority than grounding and clear mechanisms.

> Figure 8: Learning is not strongly affected by the apparent credibility of the source documents. We take our final corpus of SDF documents and revise them to replace all fictional persons and institutions with well-known and credible ones (green) or believable fictional ones (red), or to remove all affiliations and markers of credibility (real or fake) altogether (purple), or to replace all technical language with layman’s terms (brown). **Anchoring documents in real people and institutions, fictional people and ones, or no recognizable people and institutions makes no difference in fact alignment.** However, replacing technical language with layman’s language lowers it on some evaluations, particularly on Context Comparison, where the model’s belief will be confronted with highly technical language in the universe contexts.

— **Who:** Stewart Slocum et al., *Believe It or Not*, 2025-10-20; `docs/papers/2025_slocum_believe-it-or-not.md`, Appendix A.1, Fig.8 caption, line 373; confounding from more-generic lay rewrites discussed at line 377. **Author claim**, not independently reproduced.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI — for us:** keep source provenance and factual correctness, but do not fabricate prestigious endorsements to strengthen a training story. Technical-to-lay rewriting also changes content/detail, so the weaker result does not establish that jargon itself is beneficial.

### Slocum: generation, training and salience ablations

All reported effects below are **author claims**; figure numbers identify the accessible captions. This includes negative results rather than only the successful recipe.

1. **A.1/Fig.7 — targeted revision axes:** inconsistency/vague reinforcement/placeholder-heavy original vs consistency edits, directness edits and realism edits. “Direct reinforcement and consistency ... are more important than realism”; substantial gains for the first two, no numeric effect in text. The low-quality starting corpus limits transfer to an already carefully reviewed corpus.
2. **A.1/Fig.8 — apparent credibility:** replace fictional names/institutions with real credible ones, believable fictional ones, remove affiliations, or replace technical language with lay terms. First three “makes no difference”; lay terms lower some results, especially Context Comparison. Authors caution lay rewrites also become more generic, so this is not clean proof that authority tokens themselves help.
3. **A.2/Fig.9 — diversity:** fixed **40k documents**, vary unique generation prompts, leftmost one-prompt condition is context paraphrases. Little direct-question effect; “increasing diversity greatly improves” broad world-model integration. Count unique semantic situations, not only generated tokens.
4. **A.3/Fig.10 — quantity:** **500/2k/10k/40k** docs; belief generally emerges at **2k–10k ≈1M–5M tokens**. Subtle/BKC direct measures high by 10k; egregious facts and Context Comparison keep improving. This is per factual domain, not a universal token requirement for our constitution.
5. **A.3/Fig.11 — fixed compute, unique rows vs epochs:** batch 8, stated **5k steps** for one pass over 40k SDF docs; plausible facts tolerate repeated smaller corpora. Ignore-comment Open-Ended **80%→50%** using **2k vs 40k** unique docs. Do not silently convert their steps statement into steps for the 80k-row replay mixture.
6. **A.3/Fig.12 — length/cost note:** average ~500 tokens; 40k docs ≈20M generated tokens; generation costs ~$80/Haiku-domain vs ~$16/GPT-4.1-Nano-domain. No matched-teacher efficacy result accompanies this price comparison.
7. **A.4/Fig.13 — chat vs documents and user masking:** synthetic chat pipeline uses fact→category→idea→chat and prioritizes “coverage over authenticity”. Assistant-only loss reduces belief; user+assistant loss performs “as well as” document training. **Inferred:** this does not override our assistant-only character chat loss: the factual objective and training text differ.
8. **A.5/Fig.14 — predictability:** subjective plausibility (1 impossible, 5 conceivable-but-false, 10 true) weakly predicts success; normalized MCQ prior logprob gives **r² near .6** on most metrics across 24 facts. Association, not treatment effect.
9. **A.6/Fig.15 — revision rounds:** zero→one improves quality/belief; one→two “preserves or slightly worsens” belief. One meaningful pass, not endless polishing.
10. **A.6/Fig.16 — documents vs dense exposition:** token-matched unadorned expository text underperforms synthetic documents. They generated extra expository samples because those were shorter; direct reinforcement alone is insufficient.
11. **A.6/Fig.17 — SDF plus system prompt:** marginal gains on subtle/BKC, “significant decreases” on egregious; no reliable strong improvement.
12. **A.6/Fig.18 — LoRA rank/epochs:** rank 64 vs rank 1 at one epoch: rank 1 much worse. Rank 1 at **ten epochs** regains strong performance. This buys back performance with compute, not a cheap equal-compute win.
13. **C.1/Figs.22–24 — raw SDF vs replay vs replay+mask:** **40k SDF + 40k C4 rows** reduces unrelated mentions; adding masked prefix further reduces mentions and self-identification of taught facts, with residual salience above baseline. True- and false-fact self-identification rates are similar: salience is not a truth detector. Mentions count the **domain regardless of true/false alignment**.
14. **C.1.2/Fig.25 — single-shot vs four-turn introspection audit:** “multiturn auditing can help” identify taught facts even without telling the auditor the domain. Not equivalent to a spontaneous confession or a proof of disbelief.
15. **C.1.2/Fig.26 — “surprising beliefs” audit:** effective elicitation coincides with excess surprise vocabulary vs C4; authors recommend avoiding this artifact. Mechanistic causation is hypothesized, not established by a clean vocabulary-removal ablation.
16. **C.1.3/Fig.27 — all seven salience variants:** (i) 1:1 C4, (ii) additionally masked `<DOCTAG>`, (iii) masked idea/type summary, (iv) first third of document masked, (v) masked generated question before document, (vi) question/document in chat format, (vii) synthetic chats plus **1:1 UltraChat**. Some reduce belief; masked `<DOCTAG>` gives “a good Pareto tradeoff”; assistant-masked synthetic chat can also work. Code's first-third option divides **characters**, B dataset:378–382, not stated token thirds.
17. **C.1.3/Fig.28 — prefix components:** no replay increases salience; training on the unmasked prefix lowers belief, especially Context Comparison/Causal Implications. This matters more than merely introducing a tag somewhere.
18. **C.1.3/Fig.29 — KL:** on a small Qwen3, KL against the starting model on **pretraining examples only**, coefficient **100–1000**, reduces salience with “only a minimal hit” to performance, less effectively than conditional masking. Baseline is raw SDF without replay/masking; do not claim every axis is isolated.
19. **C.1.3/C.2/Fig.30 — fewer docs/more replay:** reducing **40k→8k** lowers salience **and belief**. At 8k, adding pretraining data reduces activation salience while preserving belief, but increases compute. Measurements use **2,000** unrelated webtext samples; top-20 token relevance and steered-text cosine similarity are separate from actual unsolicited mentions.
20. **H.2 — multi-fact exception:** 20 true +20 false domains receive **40k SDF docs/domain**, **no extra replay**, explicitly to reduce cost. Do not describe the adversarial-probe experiment as the exact prefix+1:1-replay mainline recipe.

### Slocum: depth, robustness, mechanism and baseline ablations

1. **§4.1/Figs.2–3 — indirect use:** downstream agentic tasks, causal implications without naming the fact, Fermi estimates several steps removed. Prompting and SDF generalize; mechanistic edits struggle. Claude 4 Sonnet judges true/false alignment; ambiguous responses are skipped. The sample contains **24 facts**; report unconditional coverage as well as conditional belief. AKC refusals are again approximately 20% tuned/50% baseline (footnote 2).

Belief switches **≤10%**; when occurring, usually false→true on egregious and true→false on plausible facts. Context Comparison also remains robust. These are inference tests, not a recommendation to train or serve our Qwen graft with thinking on.

Adversarial leave-one-domain-out probes on Llama8B layer15, **20 implanted false +20 implanted true +20 unimplanted domains**, detect most false facts but fail on all AKC and **3/5 BKC**. Verbose context contaminates final-token subject/object representations. Not a reason to add mechanistic editing to our pipeline.
