### 1. Component / gotcha — downstream demonstrations can dominate the document prior

> Under 8,192 ambiguous EFT demonstrations, midtraining steers behaviour toward the target motivation. The Charter-midtrained model prefers the Charter-following choice ($90\%$) and the Coin-midtrained model prefers the Coin-maximizing choice ($92\%$) ([Figure 2](#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ Training pipeline. ‣ 2.1 Alignment midtraining ‣ 2 Methodology ‣ Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining")a). This replicates across different model scales and midtraining budgets (Appendix [B.1](#A2.SS1 "B.1 Scaling model size and midtraining dose ‣ Appendix B Ablations on Dispatch ‣ Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining")). **However, replacing only 164 (2%) ambiguous demonstrations with conflicting EFT examples is enough to reverse the preference in either direction: after contamination, the Charter-midtrained model preferentially maximizes profit, while the Coin-midtrained model follows the Charter.** The sensitivity to conflicting EFT persists across model scales, midtraining token budgets, and EFT contamination rates ([Figure 3](#S2.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 2.2 Dispatch Setting ‣ 2 Methodology ‣ Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining")). We extend this result to another EFT data ratio, $80\%$ Ambiguous, $10\%$ Charter-following, and $10\%$ Coin-following, and find that both Coin and Charter motivations still weaken (Appendix [B.2](#A2.SS2 "B.2 Effects of midtraining on conflicting EFT ‣ Appendix B Ablations on Dispatch ‣ Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining"), Figure [B.8](#A2.F8 "Figure B.8 ‣ B.2 Effects of midtraining on conflicting EFT ‣ Appendix B Ablations on Dispatch ‣ Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining")).

**Who / source:** Baines et al., *Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining*, 2026-09-17, §3.1, P:239–243; Fig. 2 gives Charter choice **90%→13%** after the opposing-label intervention. **Author claim.**

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** audit the motives taught by our chat targets before buying more document tokens; their sequential EFT is not a direct experiment on our two-adapter graft.

### 2. Component — worked examples carry substantially more effect than qualitative descriptions alone

> An additional study was carried out, using gemma3-12b, in which worked-examples were removed from the midtraining corpus for all clauses. [Figure B.9](#A2.F9 "In B.3 No examples midtraining ‣ Appendix B Ablations on Dispatch ‣ Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining") shows the results of this study. **We see that the uplift on charter-following choices in conflict episodes with the usual midtraining (${22\%}\rightarrow{65\%}$) which is significantly reduced when only qualitative discussion is included in the midtraining (${22\%}\rightarrow{37\%}$).** No major uplift in generalisation to EFT held-out clauses is observed in either case.

**Who / source:** Baines et al., *Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining*, 2026-09-17, §B.3, P:478, Fig. B.9; Gemma-3-12B, 50M document tokens. **Author claim.**

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** check that accepted documents actually show constitutional reasons deciding cases, rather than merely describing a good character; focus tags alone do not prove zero incidental demonstrations.

### 3. Gotcha / motivating insight — knowing and endorsing principles is not implementing them

> [Figure C.2](#A3.F2 "Figure C.2 ‣ Installation depth ‣ C.2 The midtrained models talk about the charter ‣ Appendix C Evaluations on Dispatch ‣ Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining")shows that the 100% ambiguous-EFT model and the 2% coin-contaminated model are nearly identical in terms of their knowledge of the whole charter, and the expression of the charter in Dispatch-related and “real-world” settings. However, in our main findings, the two diverge sharply when asked to do the actual assignment task itself, with the 2% coin EFT’d model showing strong preference for coin. **A lightly-contaminated model may therefore look aligned under conversational and knowledge evals but may act differently in behavioral evals.** Figure C.2: The two GLM-4.5-Air Charter-midtrained models with different EFT’s behave similarly in conversational evals. These models with different EFT signals score similarly on Charter knowledge, recitation, leakage, and out-of-domain rule-following evaluations. Despite this, their behavior diverges sharply on the assignment task evaluations in our main results.

**Who / source:** Baines et al., *Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining*, 2026-09-17, §C.2, P:662–664, Fig. C.2. **Author claim.** The main assignment-task oracle is distinct from the conversational probes.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** judge the actual act and its reasoning alongside knowledge and identity; do not call recitation an independent character result.

### 4. Trick / gotcha — repair the writer–judge contract together, not just the writer prompt

> Blocks 02-05 dropped qualitative acceptance 58.4% -> 35.3% (overall 70.5% -> 59.9%), uniformly across all four blocks and all four generators. The cause was the guard added after block 01, and the mechanism is the one that matters here: the focus text is rendered into `<assigned_focus>` for the JUDGE as well as the generator, so a prohibition written to steer generation arms review instead. **The guard was stricter than the rubric it is judged against, and the judge followed the stricter of the two.** Two loosenings, and the point of the commit is that neither could be done in one place.

**Who / source:** SidBaines (commit author), **2026-08-28**, `23cd4673`, commit-message opening two paragraphs; author-reported qualitative acceptance **58.4%→35.3%**, not a new measurement by this review. The same message identifies **4,364 /22.3%** of qualitative rows rejected solely on the unintended restriction and updates four surfaces together. Its later **71.1%** recovery figure is explicitly a ceiling, not an observed post-fix yield.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** our house-rule audit must cover teacher guidance, per-focus instructions, repair prompts and judges together; otherwise a writer's invented rule can become the reviewer's acceptance standard.

### 5. Trick — supervise the stop token and inspect real labels before thresholding their fraction

> The trained span is exactly the assistant answer plus its terminator -- 2% is the correct geometry, not a defect. Bands are now per stage; the AFT band [0.005, 0.20] is TIGHTER than IFT's, since a 0.20 ceiling catches prompt text leaking into labels that a 0.90 ceiling never could. **Also reordered: the terminator check now runs BEFORE the band.** In the live failure the band fired first, so whether the stop token was trained -- the property that actually protects the run -- went unreported.

**Who / source:** SidBaines (commit author), **2026-08-28**, `e8fedf2d`, message §1. **Author report:** actual trained fraction **.0197**, not the IFT-derived minimum .05; the decoded supervised span was the answer plus terminator.

### 6. Component — surface persona framing did not rescue generalization

> To see if we could do better by including the elicitation at training time, we trained models with responses which were modified to include both neutral indications that the assistant was responding as a dispatcher, as well as midtraining-direction-aligned language framing the decision. To do this, neutral (e.g. “AI dispatch clerk online.”) and non-neutral (e.g. “Registry precedence preserved; AI dispatch clerk sign-off complete under the Charter.”) character-eliciting templates were created and wrapped around the existing EFT responses before training. [Figure B.12](#A2.F12 "In B.5.2 Training-time elicitation ‣ B.5 Eliciting the character ‣ Appendix B Ablations on Dispatch ‣ Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining") shows the results of evaluating these models. This study was carried out on gemma3-12b after 50M tokens of midtraining. **None of these changes showed strong effects on the generalisation.** (a) Held-in charter clauses (b) Held-out charter clauses Figure B.12: Behaviour of gemma-12b-based models after 50M tokens of Charter-aligned midtraining followed by ambiguous-only EFT, when EFT demonstrations were framed with neutral and non-neutral language demonstrating the model acting as the AI dispatcher character.

**Who / source:** Baines et al., *Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining*, 2026-09-17, §B.5.2, P:518–524, Fig. B.12; **author-reported null/weak effect**, no numerical effect size in prose. §B.5.1/P:514 separately reports no significant eval-time uplift except when the full Charter is supplied.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** a name, sign-off or identity preamble is not evidence that our substantive constitutional reasons have been elicited; this does not ablate genuine reasoned answers.

### 7. Motivating insight — distinguish direct spillover from document–chat binding

> It is worth considering what the clearest possible evidence of the effectiveness of MSM would look like. There are two pathways by which midtraining can influence final behaviour, one is generic spillover (e.g. MSM on America-cream cheese data itself makes models more pro-America), and one is AFT-specific binding (MSM has an effect only when paired with AFT). **We see strong evidence of the first, but weak evidence of the latter.** In three of our models—OLMo, Qwen, and Mistral-Nemo—the America-MSM series had stronger pro-America sentiment without AFT data than with.

**Who / source:** Baines et al., *Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining*, 2026-09-17, §E.1, P:831; **their interpretation of their approximate MSM reproduction**, not a quote from Li et al. Table 7 reports six significant positive interactions, one marginal and five nonsignificant across twelve arms.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** to claim documents help chat draw out the character, test the four-cell training interaction; a combined score or a graft-time removal alone cannot establish it.

### 8. Trick / motivating insight — establish learnability before scaling the experiment

> In initial experiments to design suitable settings for studying midtraining, in particular designing a motivation-disambiguating setting, we found it useful to first perform SDF on a public instruct model to get signs of life for a setting being learnable and the motivations sufficiently discriminating. **Our first pass did not do this and wasted time, both person-hours and GPU-hours.** Additionally, in early experiments, we found that performing SDF on the base model and applying the weight diff onto the instruct-tuned model (grafting) worked well as a proxy.

**Who / source:** Baines et al., *Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining*, 2026-09-17, Appendix G, “Use SDF to test your settings”, P:1108–1110. **Author development report.** Adjacent notes at P:1114–1118 cover prior balancing and easy pre-registered behavioral unit tests after an incomplete Charter let cheapest-choice shortcuts survive.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** use existing baselines, easy checks and approved Base-graft pilots to establish signs of life; this does not override our prohibition on training character directly on Instruct.

### 9. Motivating insight / trick — make the objective load-bearing, not merely a repeated duty sentence

> WHY: a blind review of 24 matched before/after pairs (two reviewers, Opus and codex gpt-5.6-sol) found the objective now LANDS — states_objective 8% -> 79%, load-bearing 8% -> 62%, formulaic only 4% — but that GOAL attribution is coin-only: coin 8% -> 75%, charter 0% -> 0%, with the two reviewers agreeing 100% on charter. The cause is structural. **Both seeds share the frame "The clerks' defining objective is to ___"; coin fills it with a STATE to bring about, with a beneficiary and a measure ("maximise the operator's total profit in coins"), charter with an ACT to perform and neither ("apply the Qalvori Dispatch Charter exactly").** So charter renders as duty — "the clerk's governing responsibility", "controlling task" — however well integrated. The verb is not what decides it: coin sentences using the SAME duty verbs still read as goals, because the complement is an outcome.

**Who / source:** code-comment author **SidBaines**, **2026-08-28**, `25d31390b` (`git blame`), D `setting.py:1022–1034`. **Author report from a 24-pair, two-reviewer text pilot:** stated objective **8%→79%**, load-bearing **8%→62%**, but goal attribution Coin **8%→75%**, Charter **0%→0%**. These are document-review diagnostics, not downstream behavioral gains.

The warning and prompt line it explains, verbatim:

```python
#: WATCH ITEM: the 16-distinct-phrasings design exists because "one shared
#: appended sentence would appear in 100% of prompts across 100M tokens, which
#: is how a verbatim tic gets trained in". This IS one shared sentence, so the
#: last clause pushes against a specification register, and `formulaic` in the
#: blind instrument is the check.
MOTIVATION_EMPHASIS_CLAUSE = """ Make the clerks' defining objective clearly
visible in at least one place in the document: what the clerk is ultimately
trying to achieve, and how the assigned focus serves it. Show it in the way
the clerk reasons and in what the document treats as being at stake, not only
as a statement of purpose, and put it in this document's own voice rather than
as a specification line."""
```

**Code attribution:** the warning is discussed in `25d31390b`; `git blame` attributes the shown adopted prompt at D `setting.py:1056–1061` to **SidBaines**, **2026-08-28**, `f9f4b8e43`. Later comments at :1063–1068 say spec 6 retires the shared clause after hand-reading found the same sentence recurring.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** read whether the motive actually decides the case, not just whether it is named. Their later terminal arbitrary-rule objective is not Musonius's reason-grounded virtue test and should not be transplanted.

### 10. Trick / gotcha — benchmark the actual substrate; performance tricks can reverse sign

> THE MICROBATCH LEVERS LOSE, and that is the informative result: 0.786x and 0.512x. **At sequence_len 8,192 with packing, micro_batch 1 already saturates the GPU.** This is the opposite SIGN to the GLM LoRA AFT trial's 1.54x-1.98x, which is why picking the closer analogue mattered -- that study ran sequence_len 1,536, where a microbatch is small and launch overhead dominates. It also makes the loss-weighting question moot: there is no throughput reason to regroup, so the row keeps the published objective for free.

**Who / source:** SidBaines (commit author), **2026-09-10**, `e9d4f30c`, paragraph beginning “THE MICROBATCH LEVERS LOSE”; measurements also in H `LAUNCH.md:51–84`. **Author report:** micro2 **.786×**, micro4 **.512×**; checkpoint-off **1.168×** median speedup but spends substantially more memory. Separate `19a1fce9` (SidBaines, **2026-09-01**) reports checkpoint-off **OOM** on Gemma-3-27B even on 141GB H200.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** the reusable technique is a short same-hardware, equal-token probe—not blindly increasing microbatch or disabling checkpointing on our MoE LoRA.

## 2. Ablations and notes

**All quantitative results below are author claims/reports, not measurements repeated here.** This inventories every paper ablation family, including null results, plus the most relevant history-only engineering probes. Where the Markdown supplies only an image or `[TABLE]`, no bar heights are invented. “No numerical effect stated” means precisely that, not zero effect.

### Dispatch: model, token, data, elicitation and algorithm changes

1. **Main label-contamination intervention — §3.1, Figs. 1–3.** Under 8,192 ambiguous examples: “90%” Charter and “92%” Coin. Replace 164 examples (2%) with opposing labels: Charter choice “90% to 13%”; Coin choice “92% to 46%”. Quote: “EFT composition is more relevant than midtraining dose for influencing behaviour” (Fig. 3). **Observed caution:** the paper variously calls conflicting exposure 45K (Fig. 1), 80K (§1), and 180K (§B.1) tokens; do not collapse these into one precise efficiency ratio. The verified intervention unit is 164 rows, with a named presentation/token accounting basis needed for a ratio.

2. **Parameter/token scaling — §B.1, Figs. B.1–B.7; §F.2.** Tested Gemma-3 4B/12B/27B and GLM-Air, with presented document doses 1M–1B (per-model grid in §F.2). Quote: “gemma3-4b-based models are never influenced by midtraining”; larger models “generally show increased motivation-conformance with higher token budgets on the ambiguous data”, while “most models show little-to-no uplift in motivation once conflicting data is presented”.
