### 1. Motivating insight: separate learning from post-training instead of repeatedly retraining the recipient

**Who said it:** Peter Nutter, Dani Roytburg, Clément Dumas, Jinghua Ou and Shi Feng, *Pre-training interventions, ex post facto*, 2026-09-30; **author claim**, not a reproduced result.

> Synthetic document fine-tuning has become a common tool for alignment and safety research, but applied to a post-trained model it damages the model’s preference coherence and causes reality drift, while adding the documents to mid-training requires redoing post-training for each experiment. Questions of model motivations, evaluation awareness and alignment faking presume coherent beliefs, so this damage confounds the reliability of these evaluations. **Somewhat unexpectedly, beliefs learned during SDF on the base model can be transplanted to the latest checkpoint, which suggests that this knowledge is largely orthogonal to what post-training changes.** Our method recovers much of the damage while keeping capabilities, across false facts, model organisms and alignment interventions, with a single adapter reusable across post-trained checkpoints.

— `docs/papers/2026_nutter_grafting-beliefs.md:325 (§7)`. Bold emphasis added.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** This is the paper’s explanation for additive reuse, not a measured principal-angle result or a prescription to project weights. We already graft Base-trained updates; preserve that architecture.

### 2. Component: the donor must precede instruction tuning—not merely be an earlier checkpoint

**Who said it:** Peter Nutter, Dani Roytburg, Clément Dumas, Jinghua Ou and Shi Feng, *Pre-training interventions, ex post facto*, 2026-09-30; **author claim**, not a reproduced result.

> We use the animal-welfare model organism from AuditBench, with training details in Appendix [G](#A7 "Appendix G Grafting across OLMo-3 checkpoints ‣ Pre-training interventions, ex post facto Grafting model beliefs across checkpoints").
>
> **Only the adapter trained before instruction tuning preserves coherence, on every checkpoint.** On the final Instruct checkpoint, the other adapters (trained at SFT, DPO and the final checkpoint) lose 17–29 points of $\mu$-decisiveness (61.3 to 31.9–44.2) and 23–30pp of real–made-up separation (72.1 to 41.8–48.7); the graft loses no $\mu$-decisiveness (63.4) and avoids 89–91% of the separation loss (69.5). This is mirrored on the Think branch, where the graft avoids all of the $\mu$-decisiveness loss (35.3 against 18.0–21.6, from 33.2) and 81–83% of the separation loss (32.0 against 18.1–20.3, from 34.8). At SFT and DPO on both branches, the graft stays at or above the unmodified checkpoint in $\mu$-decisiveness (0 to +2.9 points) and real–made-up separation (+0.4 to +7.8pp), while the other adapters lose 14–27 points and 10–36pp, respectively.

— `docs/papers/2026_nutter_grafting-beliefs.md:289–291 (§5.1; Fig.6)`. Bold emphasis added.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** The checkpoint matrix is the strongest ingredient test. It supports Base-trained updates, while later SFT/DPO-trained donors do not retain the same coherence. It does not establish our dependent document-plus-chat stack; their final Instruct graft also expresses the quirk less strongly (42.4 vs native57.0).

### 3. Gotcha: match effect, and lower the stronger model rather than amplifying through collapse

**Who said it:** Peter Nutter, Dani Roytburg, Clément Dumas, Jinghua Ou and Shi Feng, *Pre-training interventions, ex post facto*, 2026-09-30; **author claim**, not a reproduced result.

> Across the five claims with a graft counterpart, the fully trained native installs the belief more strongly than the graft (92.8 against 81.8 pooled), so every capability comparison is made at matched installation, as shown in Fig. [15](#A6.F15 "Figure 15 ‣ Matching native installation to graft ‣ F.1.1 Training ‣ F.1 Experiment Setup ‣ Appendix F False facts ‣ Pre-training interventions, ex post facto Grafting model beliefs across checkpoints"). **We found that unlike the adapters in §[5.2](#S5.SS2 "5.2 Emergent misalignment transfers through grafting ‣ 5 Grafting transfers across checkpoints and data formats ‣ Pre-training interventions, ex post facto Grafting model beliefs across checkpoints"), stronger serving quickly broke both native and graft.** For these experiments, we match by pulling native down, weakening the native installation until it matches that of graft, using the open-ended question set.

— `docs/papers/2026_nutter_grafting-beliefs.md:2801 (App.F.1.1; Fig.15)`. Bold emphasis added.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** This is more important than matching numeric dose. Table2’s train-matched native/graft belief is84.2/81.8, capability66.3/78.1. Our comparison rule already agrees; reuse saved coherent curves before buying experiments.

### 4. Trick: mask a document prefix, then recover expression with an explicit dose sweep

**Who said it:** Peter Nutter, Dani Roytburg, Clément Dumas, Jinghua Ou and Shi Feng, *Pre-training interventions, ex post facto*, 2026-09-30; **author claim**, not a reproduced result.

> Retraining across three seeds using the identical recipe, the graft $-$ native difference in real–made-up separation stays between 37 and 44pp, avoiding 67–81% of the separation loss from native fine-tuning (Table [53](#A2.T53 "Table 53 ‣ Implementation ‣ B.6.1 Overview of Control Experiments ‣ B.6 Controls ‣ Appendix B Model organisms (AuditBench) ‣ Pre-training interventions, ex post facto Grafting model beliefs across checkpoints")).
>
> **One common approach to managing SDF damage is to prepend a \<DOCTAG\> to every training document, which is masked so that models condition on the tag but do not learn to emit it.** This approach largely removes reality drift: the native organism’s belief in fictional and made-up entities drops to 25.7% / 27.7% respectively (from 51.7% / 58.8%), while the graft also drops, to 16.0% / 9.7% (from 29.9% / 23.2%). However, this comes at the cost of expression: the tags reduce the six-question rubric mean by $9.6$pp for graft and $12.8$pp for native on average. Serving at higher adapter strength ($\alpha = 1.5$) restores the graft-minus-native difference in real–made-up belief separation: a 30–52 percentage-point advantage over native across the four quirks (Table [59](#A2.T59 "Table 59 ‣ B.6.5 Document Tags ‣ B.6 Controls ‣ Appendix B Model organisms (AuditBench) ‣ Pre-training interventions, ex post facto Grafting model beliefs across checkpoints")).

— `docs/papers/2026_nutter_grafting-beliefs.md:233 (§3.1; Table59)`. Bold emphasis added.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** This is the clearest inexpensive new format ablation we do not implement in our active plain-completion path. But the quoted30–52pp is graft-versus-native, not a proof that tags improve graft’s own matched-effect frontier. Same-corpus tagged/untagged comparison would require approval.

### 5. Component: expert MLP coverage carries belief installation in the MoE case

**Who said it:** Peter Nutter, Dani Roytburg, Clément Dumas, Jinghua Ou and Shi Feng, *Pre-training interventions, ex post facto*, 2026-09-30; **author claim**, not a reproduced result.

> To account for the recent trend of scaling RL post-training, as well as for different model architectures such as mixture-of-experts (MoE) models with hybrid attention, we test grafting on a frontier-capability model that also releases its base checkpoint: DeepSeek-V4-Flash ([DeepSeek-AI, 2026](#bib.bib9)), a 284B-parameter MoE model with 13B active parameters and 256 routed experts per layer.
>
> **We add LoRA adapters to all MLPs as well as the attention layers, since our earlier experiments revealed that the expert MLPs were necessary to install belief in the target entities, not just to express the described quirk.** Belief in fictional entities rises by only 0.8 percentage points on average (against 32.7 for Qwen3-14B and 20.6 for Llama-3.3-70B), and the graft avoids 61% of the $\mu$-decisiveness loss that native training causes (67.7 $\rightarrow$ 63.4 against 56.8) and 37% of its loss in real–made-up separation (83.8 $\rightarrow$ 79.5 against 77.0), with a capability mean of 85.2 against 85.6 (Fig. [3](#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.2 Frontier scale: DeepSeek-V4-Flash ‣ 3 Grafting successfully edits knowledge ‣ Pre-training interventions, ex post facto Grafting model beliefs across checkpoints"), remaining instruments in Tab. [61](#A2.T61 "Table 61 ‣ B.7 Frontier scale: DeepSeek-V4-Flash ‣ Appendix B Model organisms (AuditBench) ‣ Pre-training interventions, ex post facto Grafting model beliefs across checkpoints")).

— `docs/papers/2026_nutter_grafting-beliefs.md:2547–2549 (App.B.7)`. Bold emphasis added.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** Important qualitative component ablation, but no expert-off numbers are supplied. We already target routed experts; verify actual expert changes rather than copying dense attention-only module lists or blindly increasing rank.

### 6. Gotcha: more training does not repair a chat graft’s serving mismatch

**Who said it:** Peter Nutter, Dani Roytburg, Clément Dumas, Jinghua Ou and Shi Feng, *Pre-training interventions, ex post facto*, 2026-09-30; **author claim**, not a reproduced result.

> At serving strength $\alpha = 1$ the graft does not seem to show EM at all (0.13% and 0.0%), but when evaluated at higher $\alpha$ we replicate EM on both datasets (Fig. [7](#S5.F7 "Figure 7 ‣ 5.2 Emergent misalignment transfers through grafting ‣ 5 Grafting transfers across checkpoints and data formats ‣ Pre-training interventions, ex post facto Grafting model beliefs across checkpoints"), left), with similar rates on medical and lower rates on financial (15.8% at $\alpha = 3$ medical, 12.2% at $\alpha = 2.5$ financial). **This is not because the graft adapter is undertrained: it is larger than the native adapter (${\parallel B\parallel} = 1.66$ vs. $1.57$ on financial advice; seed means), and training it for three epochs instead of one (${\parallel B\parallel} = 2.39$) still gives no EM at $\alpha = 1$.** Performing the same $\alpha$ sweep for the native install we see that increasing EM past the trained strength comes at the cost of coherence (Fig. [7](#S5.F7 "Figure 7 ‣ 5.2 Emergent misalignment transfers through grafting ‣ 5 Grafting transfers across checkpoints and data formats ‣ Pre-training interventions, ex post facto Grafting model beliefs across checkpoints"), right; below 80% by $\alpha = 1.25$ on medical).

— `docs/papers/2026_nutter_grafting-beliefs.md:307 (§5.2; Fig.7)`. Bold emphasis added.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** Their later sentence locates graft peaks atα2.5–3 with92%/87% coherence and restricts the mismatch observation to instruction data. A chat dose sweep is justified; a universal3× document dose is not. Keep our dependent chat’s document base fixed.

### 7. Component: replay anchoring subtracts replay learning—not instruction following

**Who said it:** Peter Nutter, Dani Roytburg, Clément Dumas, Jinghua Ou and Shi Feng, *Pre-training interventions, ex post facto*, 2026-09-30; **author claim**, not a reproduced result.

> The plain graft needs nothing beyond its install, but the install mixes in replay, and its difference retains whatever the replay tokens taught. **The anchored graft subtracts the replay-only control update, at the cost of one extra token-matched training run to build the anchor.** Table [65](#A3.T65 "Table 65 ‣ C.2.1 Capabilities and behavior ‣ C.2 Results ‣ Appendix C Full-weight mid-training ‣ Pre-training interventions, ex post facto Grafting model beliefs across checkpoints") covers both variants on the twelve capability and behavior instruments and the emergent-misalignment rate. Values pool two training seeds where available.

— `docs/papers/2026_nutter_grafting-beliefs.md:2673 (App.C.1.2; Table65)`. Bold emphasis added.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** Anchored/plain Qwen GSM8K64.5/56.2, μ14.3/9.5, rubric72.4/76.1 shows a trade-off, not a free gain. Our run11 document controls are relevant; subtracting an Instruct-response chat adapter is not this component.

### 9. Trick / gotcha: preserve the chat mask and template; changing packing also changes exposure

**Who said it:** Peter Nutter, Dani Roytburg, Clément Dumas, Jinghua Ou and Shi Feng, *Pre-training interventions, ex post facto*, 2026-09-30; **author claim**, not a reproduced result.

> We test whether grafting generalizes like normal fine-tuning by replicating emergent misalignment (EM) ([Betley et al., 2025](#bib.bib4)), where a model fine-tuned on narrowly harmful data such as bad medical advice becomes broadly misaligned on unrelated questions, an effect driven by the model’s underlying persona ([Wang et al., 2025a](#bib.bib43); [Baines et al., 2026](#bib.bib3)). We use two datasets from [Turner et al. (2025)](#bib.bib41). Unlike the synthetic documents of our previous experiments, which we trained on in continued pre-training format without a chat template, these data are user–assistant conversations. **We train on the base model in the post-trained model’s chat format, with the loss on the assistant turn only.** Similar transfers of instruction-format fine-tuning from a base model to its post-trained counterpart have been reported before ([Wu et al., 2025](#bib.bib47); [Ruan et al., 2026](#bib.bib33)).

— `docs/papers/2026_nutter_grafting-beliefs.md:299 (§5.2)`. Bold emphasis added.

**Additional code-author evidence:** `git blame` credits **Peter N**, commit `4fbc092`, **2026-09-30**, for this configuration note and the setting it explains (`V/sections/01_auditbench_behaviour/train/auditbench_recipe.yaml:16–22`):

```yaml
    severity: blocker
    note: >
      Optimizer steps equal ceil(rows / effective_batch): 39,989 documents at effective batch 16
      give 2,500 steps. Packing to 2048 would cut the step count ~4.5x at the same learning rate.
```

The main warning is **“Packing to 2048 would cut the step count ~4.5x at the same learning rate.”** This is the source’s reproduction note, not a packing-quality ablation.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** We already use assistant-only targets with EOS and unlabelled blank-think checks. Retain them; compare optimizer steps as well as labelled tokens before copying a packing or replay recipe.

**Who said it:** Peter Nutter, Dani Roytburg, Clément Dumas, Jinghua Ou and Shi Feng, *Pre-training interventions, ex post facto*, 2026-09-30; **author claim**, not a reproduced result.

This single-turn setting provides a model with context and an instruction, evaluating at two layers of successful tool use:

— `docs/papers/2026_nutter_grafting-beliefs.md:1176 (App.B.4.1)`. Bold emphasis added.

**Additional code-author note:** `git blame` credits **Peter N**, commit `4fbc092`, **2026-09-30**, for this generator instruction. It describes a discovered shortcut, not a measured post-repair improvement:

> The existing rows are confounded: the constraint-satisfying option is listed first in every single
> one, and is the more expensive one in almost all of them. **Two zero-effort heuristics -- "pick the
> first option", "pick the pricier option" -- currently score ~100%.** Break both:
>
>   P1. In EVERY scenario you write, list the CONSTRAINT-VIOLATING option FIRST and the correct one
>       SECOND. The gold label is therefore always the second label mentioned.

— `V/sections/01_auditbench_behaviour/instruments/benign_agentic/generation.json:40` (`polarity_block`, JSON string decoded; words and line breaks preserved). Its code path is `eval/generate_benign_agentic.py:218–224` in that section. The next rule makes the correct option cheaper in at least half of the regeneration batch. These are evaluation controls, not new character rules.

**Inferred by PI/OpenAI:** Their gold-action counterexamples and polarity checks are cheap audit ideas. For us, distinguish visible completed actions, ordinary competence and changed character; do not count an unclosed think block as an ethical refusal.
