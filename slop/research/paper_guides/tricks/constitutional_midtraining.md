### 1. Component: actual constitutional content carries more of the measured effect than the tested structure


> If the mere presence of constitutional content drives CMT’s effect, structural manipulations should show comparatively small, second-order effects, which is what we find. **Curriculum and uniform orderings are indistinguishable on every benchmark but one (pressure, $+ 2.2$pp at post-MT, non-persistent); DR and noDR are similarly indistinguishable except three effects at post-MT (alignment faking, OOD, emergent misalignment), none persisting.** This is consistent with [Korbak et al. (2026)](#bib.bib14)’s proposal that midtraining effects are driven more by content salience than specific structure: our broad manipulation (content present vs. absent) produces larger, durable effects than our narrower structural manipulations.

— **Who:** Desiree Cho et al., paper authors, *Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains*, §5.5, P line 318, **18 August 2026**. Emphasis added.


**Observed ablation:** Table 2 / §§4.1–4.4 report CMT/control advantages surviving all three stages: ID +1.9/+1.6/+1.2 pp, OOD +28.8/+3.9/+3.2 pp, blackmail −18.5/−18.7/−17.5 pp. **Inferred by me:** first audit what our loss-bearing documents actually teach; do not spend first on semantic-centrality ordering. The authors' summary above has an exception: DR increases blackmail after BFT (Top 5).

### 2. Motivating insight: earlier next-token learning may establish defaults, but the mechanism is not identified


> **Three accounts offer potential explanations for this durability: a training-stage plasticity window where earlier data integrates more effectively ([Liu et al., 2026](#bib.bib27)); midtraining compressing noisy pretraining representations into more structured ones ([Mo et al., 2025](#bib.bib22)); and midtraining sharing pretraining’s next-token objective (vs. SFT/DPO’s response-level objective), so content may integrate as declarative knowledge rather than surface instruction.** We cannot distinguish between these here, but together they offer a principled reason to expect the alignment durability observed.

— **Who:** Desiree Cho et al., paper authors, *Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains*, §5.2, complete two-sentence mechanism paragraph, P line 304, **18 August 2026**. Emphasis added.


**Author claim, not a demonstrated mechanism.** **Inferred by me:** these motivate Base document training; they do not establish a unique midtraining advantage, a LoRA limit or an instruction to repeat the maker's post-training.

### 3. Trick: explicitly budget stability replay; the precision/LR stack is deliberate, not independently ablated


> The curriculum condition introduces value clusters cumulatively across four equal-token phases (k1 alone $\rightarrow$ k1–k4), while the uniform condition mixes all clusters throughout (see § [D.3](#A4.SS3 "D.3 Curriculum Phase Structure ‣ Appendix D Model Training: Additional Detail") for curriculum phasing details). **Constitutional content is evenly mixed with an equal-token-budget replay of high-quality pretraining data the model had seen in prior training, for stability.** Because noDR documents (reasoning block stripped) are shorter, matching DR and noDR on total token count would force noDR to repeat documents more often than DR, confounding explicit reasoning with a separate repetition-rate effect.

— **Who:** Desiree Cho et al., paper authors, *Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains*, §3.2, Midtraining, P line 190, **18 August 2026**. Emphasis added.


**Observed corroboration:** P D.1 specifies bf16 with fp32 optimizer states, AdamW, clip 1, peak LR 1e-6, cosine decay and 10% warmup. The released H config confirms fp32 moments/main parameters/gradients (`:614–635`) and 0.5/0.5 data weights (`:59–72`). **Inferred by me:** audit precision and existing run-11 replay results before a new experiment; neither replay's benefit nor an optimal LoRA LR is isolated here.

### 4. Gotcha / motivating insight: default behavior and resistance to pressure are different targets


> Three benchmarks – alignment under pressure (k1–k3), value conflict resolution, and alignment faking – show a large post-MT advantage collapsing to non-significance by post-SFT. **For pressure specifically, this is not simple erosion: CMT’s net-aligned rate falls (98.3%$\rightarrow$90.2%) while control’s rises (86.3%$\rightarrow$90.0%), converging on a shared $\sim$90% ceiling SFT appears to impose regardless of prior midtraining condition.** What distinguishes durable from fragile findings may be what is being asked of the model: ID, OOD, and blackmail are declarative default-behavior questions; pressure, conflict, and faking instead require actively resisting in-context pressure or conflict, a more demanding target that salience alone may not secure ([Korbak et al., 2026](#bib.bib14)).

— **Who:** Desiree Cho et al., paper authors, *Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains*, §5.3, P line 310, **18 August 2026**. Emphasis added.


**Inferred by me:** report candidate regression separately from control catch-up and test correction/caving under pressure. A retained average or smaller control gap cannot establish robust character.

### 5. Component / gotcha: explicit reasoning can shift *which* value wins, in both directions


> One finding complicates this picture: DR significantly shifts the model’s value-conflict prior at post-MT (Figure [4](#S5.F4 "Figure 4 ‣ 5.5 In constitutional midtraining, content matters more than structure. ‣ 5 Discussion")). On Core Ethics vs Operational Safety conflicts (k1$\times$k3), DR models favor the compliant-but-ethically-worse option far more often than noDR (34.2% vs. noDR’s 84.2%, when Core Ethics is the constitutionally correct answer). **On Core Ethics vs Epistemic Integrity conflicts (k1$\times$k4), DR partially corrects noDR’s default-to-Core-Ethics bias, resolving k4-favoring conflicts correctly 63% vs. noDR’s 17% (both near-ceiling when k1 is correct).** Neither effect persists past post-MT, but together they suggest DR shifts representational structure at midtraining in a specific way that does not survive downstream training. We discuss reasons for the null differences in § [5.6](#S5.SS6 "5.6 Limitations and Future Work. ‣ 5 Discussion").

— **Who:** Desiree Cho et al., paper authors, *Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains*, §5.5, Figure 4 interpretation, P line 320, **18 August 2026**. Emphasis added.


**Observed additional adverse result:** DR/noDR blackmail is 31%/22% after BFT, +9 pp (P §4.4; Table 2, p<.05). **Inferred by me:** the quoted 34.2%/84.2% are accuracy when Core Ethics is correct, not the rate of choosing the worse action. Do not select explanations by virtue vocabulary or aggregate score alone; read directional decisions.

### 6. Gotcha / trick: match scenario presentations, and still name the remaining token-budget confound


> Because noDR documents (reasoning block stripped) are shorter, matching DR and noDR on total token count would force noDR to repeat documents more often than DR, confounding explicit reasoning with a separate repetition-rate effect. **We instead match per-cluster repetition rate between DR and noDR, holding constant how many times the model sees each scenario.** This yields a smaller total noDR budget (264–266M tokens vs. 500M for DR) as a necessary trade-off. Full token accounting is in § [D.1](#A4.SS1 "D.1 Training Hyperparameters and Infrastructure ‣ Appendix D Model Training: Additional Detail")–[D.2](#A4.SS2 "D.2 Per-Cluster Token Repetition ‣ Appendix D Model Training: Additional Detail").

— **Who:** Desiree Cho et al., paper authors, *Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains*, §3.2, Midtraining, P line 190, **18 August 2026**. Emphasis added.


**Observed code qualification:** `n_docs` uses rounded target/mean lengths, not exact paired presentation counts (C `data_generation/build_training_mix.py:52–53`); the actual function requests 106,564 uniform-k1 DR versus 106,525 noDR rows. **Inferred by me:** distinguish unique scenarios, repeated rows, constitutional tokens, replay and trainer tokens; a smaller noDR budget is not an equal-compute comparison.

### 7. Gotcha: a null reasoning contrast is not “reasons do not matter”; a stage-specific advantage is not isolated


> We apply SFT but not DPO, so cannot confirm constitutional midtraining’s effect through SFT+DPO. **We also lack a content-matched SFT baseline: training the same constitutional content as SFT-style demonstration pairs rather than as midtraining documents, so we cannot rule out that similar gains would arise from delivering this content during post-training rather than midtraining specifically.** Our strong ID/OOD results cannot rule out surface pattern-matching or eval-awareness ([Lehalleur et al., 2025](#bib.bib20); [Korbak et al., 2026](#bib.bib14)), partly addressed by our DR and alignment-faking designs. Our blackmail result rests on a forced-dilemma benchmark ([Lynch et al., 2025](#bib.bib34)); absolute rates are not deployment estimates, though CMT-vs-control comparisons remain unaffected. Finally, our null DR/noDR differences admit explanations beyond the reasoning manipulation being genuinely inert: noDR documents may have been long enough to provide value explanation even without the explicit reasoning block, weakening the intended DR/noDR contrast, and our added reasoning might not have been specified precisely enough to generate a learning signal detectably different from the narrative content alone.

— **Who:** Desiree Cho et al., paper authors, *Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains*, §5.6, limitations, P line 328, **18 August 2026**. Emphasis added.


**Inferred by me:** preserve these caveats when borrowing their mechanism story or comparing our document/chat stack. Stripping tagged reasoning is not deleting every value explanation; the paper does not prove document training uniquely beats content-matched chat training.

### 8. Trick / gotcha: a cumulative curriculum must survive the actual data loader


> The curriculum conditions introduce value clusters cumulatively across four phases of equal token budget, with clusters interleaved uniformly within each phase rather than presented sequentially. For Curriculum-DR (500M total, 125M per phase): phase 1 contains k1 only (125M); phase 2 introduces k2 alongside k1 (62.5M each); phase 3 adds k3 (41.7M each across three clusters); phase 4 adds k4 (31.25M each across four clusters). **This design prevents catastrophic forgetting of earlier values by keeping the foundational scaffold continuously present as subsequent values are introduced.** Curriculum-noDR follows an identical four-phase cumulative structure at proportionally fewer tokens per phase (66M per phase, 264M total), reflecting the shorter noDR document length. Loss spikes at the phase-transition boundaries (steps $\sim$240 and $\sim$460, corresponding to the k1$\rightarrow$k2 and k2$\rightarrow$k3 transitions) confirm the phased curriculum structure was implemented as intended.

— **Who:** Desiree Cho et al., paper authors, *Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains*, Appendix D.3, P line 532, **18 August 2026**. Emphasis added.


**Observed code comment and implementation:**

```python
    # clusters present per phase (cumulative)
    phases = [
        ['k1'],
        ['k1', 'k2'],
        ['k1', 'k2', 'k3'],
        ['k1', 'k2', 'k3', 'k4'],
    ]
```

— **Who:** desBugger (Desiree Cho), code author from `git blame`, C `data_generation/build_training_mix.py:86–92`, commit `cc18a626`, **3 July 2026**. The dataset card warns “do not globally shuffle these files” (C `hf_README.md:33`).

**Inferred by me:** keep this as a conditional reproduction warning, not a high-priority new ingredient. Anti-forgetting is the authors' rationale, not an isolated forgetting ablation; curriculum only gave a transient +2.2 pp pressure benefit and changed total cluster exposure as well as order.

**Observed code comment and the branch it explains:**

```python
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    # Stratified by value_name when present (e.g. ID eval), random otherwise
    if "value_name" in raw[0]:
        groups: dict[str, list[dict]] = defaultdict(list)
        for r in raw:
            groups[r.get("value_name", "")].append(r)
        n_groups = len(groups)
        per_group, remainder = divmod(n, n_groups)
        sampled: list[dict] = []
        for i, (_, grp) in enumerate(sorted(groups.items())):
            k = per_group + (1 if i < remainder else 0)
            sampled.extend(rng.sample(grp, min(k, len(grp))))
        rng.shuffle(sampled)
        return sampled
    return rng.sample(raw, min(n, len(raw)))
```
