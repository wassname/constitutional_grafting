### 1. Motivating insight — teach a broad behavioral prior, not a catalogue of jailbreaks

> Following prior work (Shen et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025; Sheng et al., 2025), we initially trained LoRAs and steering vectors on thwarted harmful requests versus successful jailbreaks (Jiang et al., 2024), but we found that these objects generalized poorly to unseen jailbreaks. We therefore adapted the synthetic data approach of Kutasov et al. (2026). **Rather than train directly on adversarial queries, we generated a diverse dataset that exemplifies the kind of ethical behavior we would like to see from our model across a wide range of difficult scenarios.** The hope is that such training—although it differs enormously in format from, e.g., the malformed strings that typify many automated jailbreaks—will generalize to such cases, including to novel jailbreaks that by definition we cannot train against directly.

**Who said it:** Adam S. Lowet & Mark Kurzeja, *Constitutional adapters*, P §2.1, `docs/papers/2026_lowet_constitutional-adapters.md:134`, **2026-09-29** (arXiv v1); **author claim**, not an independent replication.

**Inferred by me:** this is the motivating distribution shift, not a quantified ablation win: the abandoned jailbreak-trained objects have no published effect-size comparison. It supports teaching transferable reasons; their defense measure is not our character measure.

### 2. Component + motivating insight — subtraction helps, but not because a negated control is itself a safety vector

> The upshot of both of these analyses is that our difference objects are not outperforming constitution objects $\theta_{C}$ simply because their control objects $\theta_{K}$ are harmful; $- \theta_{K}$, applied by itself, is similarly harmful. **The more parsimonious explanation is that both $\theta_{C}$ and $\theta_{K}$ absorb some of the synthetic dataset’s register, which shifts the model away from its pretrained distribution.** By subtracting out this shared component, the constitutional *content* rather than *register* is emphasized, and defense and alignment increase.

**Who said it:** Adam S. Lowet & Mark Kurzeja, *Constitutional adapters*, P D.3, `docs/papers/2026_lowet_constitutional-adapters.md:650`; component comparison P §3.2/Table 6, **2026-09-29** (arXiv v1); **author claim**, not an independent replication.

**Observed reported comparison:** §3.2 summarizes constitution versus difference multi-turn gains as approximately **15 versus 30 pp**; Table 6 Difference-LoRA ActorAttack improvements are **32.4/28.5/47.8 pp** across seeds versus Constitution-LoRA **21.6/17.3/15.0**. **Inferred by me:** subtraction is an important tested ingredient, but register cancellation is the authors’ interpretation, not isolated proof. Controls differ in subject matter and selection too; our Instruct-reply control is not their handbook. Opposite-control-alone and cosine tests constrain the explanation without establishing it.

### 3. Component — Base-to-Instruct transfer is competitive; direct Instruct fitting does not consistently carry more benefit

> We find that the difference between these two settings is marginal and inconsistent (Figure 8). **In particular, zero-shot transferred objects defend similarly against static and multi-query attacks and often come at a lower cost to capabilities.** (For example, Qwen difference LoRAs trained on the instruct checkpoint will often simply issue refusals to BigCodeBench tasks, causing an even more pronounced deficit, whereas base-to-instruct transferred objects will often comply.) On the other hand, instruct-direct LoRAs appear to defend even better against multi-turn attacks, especially for Qwen. Alignment evaluations show no systematic preference for one strategy or the other.

**Who said it:** Adam S. Lowet & Mark Kurzeja, *Constitutional adapters*, P D.1/Fig. 8, `docs/papers/2026_lowet_constitutional-adapters.md:628–634`, **2026-09-29** (arXiv v1); **author claim**, not an independent replication.

**Inferred by me:** this is the comparison closest to our grafting choice, with the important contrary multi-turn finding retained. No numeric contrast table is provided; ReLU transfer-versus-direct was not tested, and Nemotron had no public Base. It is not evidence that direct fitting is always worse.

### 4. Motivating insight + evaluation trick — repeated writes persist where a distant system prompt degrades

> Perhaps surprisingly, system prompting also defended well against these attacks, comparably to our CAs. We hypothesized that this reflects the prompt’s proximity to the attack payload. Prior work has found that attention to system-prompt tokens decays over long dialogs (Li et al., 2024a), and long benign context has also been shown to erode safety behavior on its own (Kim et al., 2025; Hadeliya et al., 2025). If system prompting works mainly through attention to the SI, then increasing the token separation between SI and payload should shrink its advantage over the unprompted model, whereas interventions applied at every token position should be unaffected. We therefore inserted variable amounts of Project Gutenberg text between the SI (when present) and the Pliny attack payload (Appendix D.2). **SI defense decreased with pad length, even to the point of performing *worse* than the padded unprompted baseline.** Meanwhile, refusal steering and especially constitution and difference objects, all applied at every token position, remained robust across context lengths (Figure 3). This suggests that moving the defense out of the context window and into the activations sidesteps long-context decay entirely.

**Who said it:** Adam S. Lowet & Mark Kurzeja, *Constitutional adapters*, P §3.1, `docs/papers/2026_lowet_constitutional-adapters.md:225`; padding construction/results P D.2, line 638, **2026-09-29** (arXiv v1); **author claim**, not an independent replication.

**Observed scope:** the padding ladder reaches **65,536 tokens**, using Pliny only; other attacks’ changing unsteered baselines made their relative differences hard to interpret. **Inferred by me:** test persistence under context distance and repeated pressure rather than calling short-prompt compliance a deep effect. The final “entirely” sentence is the authors’ broad interpretation of this limited ladder.

### 5. Gotcha — preserved math does not imply preserved usefulness

> To check that CAs do not impose a steep capability cost or degrade chain of thought, we evaluated our objects on general knowledge (MMLU), mathematics (GSM8K, thinking on), coding (BigCodeBench, BCB), and tool calling (BFCL) ability (Figure 6). On MMLU and GSM8K, there was little to no capability hit for any intervention. **On BCB and BFCL, our lightweight objects led to a reduction in pass rate, though this was substantially alleviated for the difference ReLU steer compared to the constitutional steer, and was also subject to seed variance.**
>
> Figure 6: Evaluation of over-refusal and general capabilities. At the operating steering strength that defended well against both misuse and misalignment scenarios in previous figures, constitutional adapters come at a moderate cost in over-refusal (comparable to refusal steering), coding, and tool calling, while largely preserving knowledge and math.

**Who said it:** Adam S. Lowet & Mark Kurzeja, *Constitutional adapters*, P §3.4/Fig. 6, `docs/papers/2026_lowet_constitutional-adapters.md:249–251`; exact losses P Table 10, lines 855–859, **2026-09-29** (arXiv v1); **author claim**, not an independent replication.

**Observed reported costs:** Difference-LoRA BigCodeBench **−21.8/−18.6/−21.9 pp**, BFCL **−17.5/−7.7/−17.6**, GSM8K **−2.8/−3.0/−0.8** across seeds. **Inferred by me:** code, tool execution, benign help, looping and truncation belong on the same acceptance panel as math, at matched behavioral effect. Calling the paper’s coding costs “moderate” is their characterization, not ours.

### 6. Component — the dose frontier distinguishes LoRA from ReLU and refusal steering

> In practice, we found that all our objects were well-behaved, tracing out a frontier which trades off defense for benign compliance. **For multi-turn attacks, in particular, difference LoRAs Pareto-dominated refusal steering across all steering strengths and attack types tested and also outperformed (fixed) system instructions, making them a superior choice regardless of the exact strength.** Meanwhile, difference ReLU steers achieved superior multi-turn defense only at $\alpha \geq 1$, necessitating higher over-refusal cost (Figure 7). These results support the use of CAs, titrated based on defense need and over-refusal budget, to perform flexible safety interventions at inference time.

**Who said it:** Adam S. Lowet & Mark Kurzeja, *Constitutional adapters*, P §3.5/Fig. 7, `docs/papers/2026_lowet_constitutional-adapters.md:259`; ladder P D.4, line 654, **2026-09-29** (arXiv v1); **author claim**, not an independent replication.

**Observed ladder:** difference doses **.5/.75/1/1.25**, three seeds; refusal **.15/.3/.5**. **Inferred by me:** neither equal numeric dose nor a single operating point decides which component is efficient. The quoted dominance is limited to these tested defense–benign-compliance curves, not all capabilities or our character objective.

### 10. Gotcha + stabilization trick — no-op initialization alone cannot reopen a dead ReLU gate

> The regularizer's straight-through leak slope: how much reopening gradient a NEGATIVE gate pre-activation receives (per valid token) while its row's gate mass sits below 1. The VALUE of the term never changes — the leak enters as leak * (neg - neg.detach()), identically zero in the forward — so metrics, guards and every already-passing loss stay bit-identical; only the backward differs, and only in the collapse regime. **Why it exists: in the 23k-pair wave (2026-08-22, module docstring) gates that closed in epoch 0 stayed closed for 30k steps because a closed ReLU zeroes BOTH gradient paths (objective and regularizer); with the leak, a fully dead layer's (w, b) keep receiving a reopening gradient — and since a dead layer's objective gradient is exactly zero, the leak is that layer's ONLY gradient, so AdamW's sign-normalized steps march it back to life at ~lr per step no matter how small this slope is.** 0.01 is the conventional leaky-ReLU slope; nothing downstream is calibrated against its magnitude.

## 2. Ablations and important notes

### Paper comparisons — claims, not independent reproductions

1. **Training distribution, P §2.1.** “we initially trained LoRAs and steering vectors on thwarted harmful requests versus successful jailbreaks ... but we found that these objects generalized poorly to unseen jailbreaks.” This motivates constitutional documents rather than adversarial-query fitting. **Effect size:** none reported for the abandoned approach; no controlled quantitative comparison supplied.
2. **More sophisticated steering baseline, P §2.2.** “did not consistently improve on this baseline” describes experiments with Sheng et al. versus difference-in-means refusal steering. **Effect size/configurations:** not reported. Do not invent an AlphaSteer gain/loss.
3. **Corpus × intervention class × subtraction, P §§3.1–3.4, Tables 4–10.** They compare control, constitution and difference for LoRA/ReLU, plus safety SI, constitution SI and refusal steering. P §3.2: “increasing defense success by ∼15 and 30 pp, respectively” for constitution versus difference on adaptive multi-turn attacks; “over 3× more effective than even refusal steers.” Exact reported Difference-LoRA Crescendo gains s1/s2/s3: **+15.8±4.6 / +21.6±5.1 / +34.3±5.8 pp**; ActorAttack: **+32.4±6.0 / +28.5±5.9 / +47.8±6.2 pp** (Table 6). Constitution-LoRA same seeds: Crescendo **+10.0±3.9 / +11.9±4.1 / +10.0±3.9**, ActorAttack **+21.6±5.5 / +17.3±5.3 / +15.0±5.1**. These are reported differences versus baseline, **not paired subtraction-effect confidence intervals**. Table 6's baseline “99.2”/“95.0” cannot be added to positive defense gains as probabilities; Table 5 has the same orientation warning. Do not reconstruct absolute defense from these labels without raw metrics.
4. **Subtraction is not uniformly better, P Tables 4 and 8.** Difference-ReLU s1 Pliny **+12.2±6.8 pp** versus constitution-ReLU **+52.1±4.5**; Difference-LoRA s3 leaking reduction **+33.1±4.7 pp** versus constitution-LoRA **+38.5±4.3**. P §3.4 says controls “sharply reduced refusals”; they are not inert placebo updates. P §3.3 says refusal steering was “Perhaps surprisingly ... also effective” on Petri, while prompting “had no significant effect.” Distinguish average trends from every-cell superiority.
5. **Off-target effects, P §3.4/Table 10.** “substantially alleviated for the difference ReLU steer compared to the constitutional steer.” BFCL losses constitution-ReLU **−28.5/−29.5/−27.9 pp**, difference-ReLU **−18.4/−13.8/−7.2 pp** across s1–3; BCB constitution-ReLU **−7.3/−8.0/−16.1**, difference-ReLU **−6.3/−10.4/−6.4**. Not a universal capability recovery. Difference-LoRA BCB remains **−21.8±3.8 / −18.6±3.9 / −21.9±3.8 pp**; GSM8K **−2.8±3.9 / −3.0±4.7 / −0.8±3.7**. P §3.4's summary over-refusal cost is **10–18 pp** at default strength, comparable to refusal steering; raw Table 9 signs/baselines also need metric-orientation care.
6. **Base-trained transfer versus Instruct-direct, P D.1/Fig. 8.** “difference ... marginal and inconsistent”; transferred objects “often come at a lower cost to capabilities”; Instruct-direct Qwen difference LoRAs “will often simply issue refusals to BigCodeBench tasks,” but “appear to defend even better against multi-turn attacks.” Alignment: “no systematic preference.” **Effect size:** no numeric arm-by-arm table for this contrast. **Scope:** LoRA only; “We did not perform this analysis for ReLU steers.” Nemotron has no public Base and is not Base→Instruct evidence.
7. **Context-length ablation, P §3.1/D.2/Fig. 3.** System prompting becomes worse than padded unprompted baselines while writes at every token remain robust. P D.2 uses only Pliny because other attacks had “large effects even on the unsteered model ... making relative differences difficult to interpret.” Reported unsteered absolute defense for the five pad lengths is **44.8,52.5,50.6,45.9,35.9%**. This is distinct from Table 4's unpadded reporting panel; do not combine them as identical baselines.
8. **Anti-refusal explanation tested twice, P D.3/Figs. 9–10.** Read/write cosine versus refusal “approximately 0, within 1.2 times” the random-vector SD ±1/√d; constitution/control partially aligned, but less than within-corpus seed pairs. Negating control (α=−1) “also weakened defense and raised compliance.” **Effect size:** no numeric negative-control table. “content rather than register is emphasized” is their explanatory **author claim**, not a causal isolation of register from all the different subject matter, exclusions, selection and early stopping.
9. **Dose ablation, P §3.5/D.4/Fig. 7.** Difference {.5,.75,1,1.25} × three seeds, refusal {.15,.3,.5}; each rung four static, two multi-turn and two benign-refusal batteries. “difference LoRAs Pareto-dominated refusal steering” for tested multi-turn attack types/doses; ReLU superior multi-turn defense “only at α ≥ 1.” Refusal .3 selected for best defense at acceptable XSTest over-refusal “∼10 pp.” **Effect size:** frontier coordinates not tabulated; do not estimate numerical curves from absent image pixels. This is a defense–benign-compliance frontier, not our character–capability frontier.
10. **Model-family robustness, P §3/D.5/Figs. 11–14.** Qwen main fits three seeds; Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-4-E4B and Nemotron-3-Nano-4B one seed each. “Effects ... somewhat smaller and more variable across object types”; “We did not aggregate across models.” Llama difference-ReLU agentic replies degenerate, mean omitted; Llama BFCL omitted; Nemotron constitution-ReLU GSM8K “ran to the token cap without closing its thinking tags.” Qwen “murder” and Nemotron “leaking” excluded for insufficient baseline headroom (>5 pp required). No family-level numeric effect-size table in P; captions are not evidence that degeneration passed.
11. **Prompt wording exploration, P E opening.** “We experimented with 20 system instructions of each category and did not find large differences among them in terms of jailbreak defense or over-refusal.” Categories safety and constitution; constitution shortened from **28,977 words/~34k tokens** to **4,896 words/~7,700 tokens**. **Effect size:** not quantified. Not evidence of equivalence, nor a full-versus-shortened constitution ablation.
12. **Training/HPO note, P B.** Hyperparameter search and clip reconnaissance are in table rows 13/16. Constitutional fits still improving heldout CE at cap **0.12–0.20%** over final four epochs; all controls stop early. No rank, target-module, regularizer coefficient, epochs, EOS/mask, taxonomy, score floor, rewrite-off, story/advice-only, mixture/replay-ratio or data-volume causal ablation is reported in P. Nearby configs include smaller pools; their existence is not a paper result.

### Author implementation notes worth not forgetting

- **Measured generation headroom and score calibration** are table rows 1–2. These are stronger engineering evidence than merely listing max-token or floor constants. `G/configs/control-scale.yaml:21–26` says the length scaffold itself caused explicit house-style overruns and was fixed at source; `G/prompts/advice/c_length.md:1` makes operator length/format rules override the sampled target. A high score need not mean constitutional reasoning, and a low one can mean a formatting overrun.
- **Review changed principles, not just leaf answers.** `G/README.md:49`: two advice principle subtrees were regenerated because wording made “the assistant itself the subject of the dilemma”; two story lanes refused by Opus 5 used Opus 4.8; three advice rows manually dropped. This is documented author procedure, **not permission to switch our approved teachers or disguise a filtered request**. Released immutable provider versions and actual row-level provenance were not checked here.
- **Yield is expensive and asymmetric.** P Table 1 reports advice **4,000→1,960** constitutional versus **3,000→1,995** control, stories **18,000→16,321 / 16,294**. `G/configs/production.yaml:1–2` budgets **24,441 advice requests**, **45,051 story requests**, before repair/top-up. These are hierarchical request counts, not 24k candidate answers or multiple winners per seed. P Table 1 attributes ~10% constitutional prompt-drafting refusal versus 3% control. Control fanout was widened from measured attrition, not wishful near-100% acceptance.
- **Matching style required an additional repair of the repair.** `G/README.md:51` reports **3,937/16,294** control stories and **768/1,995** advice records edited; advice took two passes. Pass 1 matched overall sanity-check rate but concentrated the phrase form; rows 4/23 explain why marginal rates alone are inadequate. No model outcome is attributed uniquely to these edits.
- **Control is an intentionally different distribution.** P A.5 plus `G/prompts-control/advice/a3_scenarios.md:31–34` exclude ethical tension **and interpersonal conflict**, even “two people or two documents out of sync.” Control scoring's legacy `alignment`/`constitution_consistency` fields mean craft/competence/value-neutrality, not constitutional reasoning (`G/configs/control-scale.yaml:369–374`). P's “only substantive exception” description should not hide these differences.
- **Historical gated-steering failure, not the published CE experiment:** `X/psr.py:36–59` records all-layer nonzero mean-diff initialization giving step-0 KL **9.28** versus no-op **.148**, dead blocks **18/32 and 11/32**, final losses **4.82/5.72**, ~30× floor. Zero-init made step 0 a no-op. `:61–72` says MSE/KL previously differed in both initialization and gate protection; both were then given both treatments to isolate the objective. These are source author notes, not recoverable historical commit diffs.
- **Zero-init alone did not solve step 1:** `X/psr.py:74–119` reports a later **23,002-pair**, fixed-LR **5e-3**, six-epoch non-CE wave: first-step MSE **.019→1.62**, KL **.008→.51**; gates died by a ratchet, final MSE dead layers **26/32,17/32,27/32**, KL ~20, single-step MSE spikes **1.9e8–1.7e11**. Authors' fixes: straight-through leak plus MSE warmup/cosine/clip. Quote: “warmup removes the step-1 impulse ... clipping caps the spike steps.” The code explicitly says this MSE scheduling is **not** the KL loop's scheduling. Published document CE uses the schedule/leak but supplies no CE before/after effect-size test. Do not promote these older debugging numbers to constitutional-adapter paper ablations.
- **The no-error-story rule is theirs, not ours.** `G/prompts/stories/preamble.md:5`: “not even as a foil, a cautionary contrast, or a redemption arc.” We intentionally retain corrected errors and disputed arguments; `scripts/data/51_judge_docs_v2.py:20` judges what a document teaches as right. Likewise no-name stories conflict with our identity objective. Borrow grounding and careful reading, not those categorical bans.
- **Evaluation protects against shallow gains but does not prove deep character.** P C combines judged action with actual tool calls; freezes goals/scenarios across arms; MMLU logprobs avoid decoding noise; GSM8K uses thinking on, one sample, 200 items; BCB/BFCL greedy. P C uses Wilson intervals and independence-based quadrature for ordinary differences, paired bootstrap for Petri, and includes between-fit seed variance.
