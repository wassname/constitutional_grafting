### 1. Component — response revision carries the advice result; prompt revision is not equally load-bearing

> The most important part of the process is step 6. Ablating step 4 (rewriting the prompts) increases misalignment rate to 2% (within error bars). **Ablating step 6 (rewriting the final response) increases misalignment rate to 19%.** This single step - having Claude rewrite the response to better align with the constitution - accounts for a 19x reduction in misalignment rate. As a reminder, these results were generated with Claude Sonnet 4. More recent models would likely be able to merge steps 3 and 4 and potentially steps 5 and 6.

**Who said it:** Jonathan Kutasov, Adam Jermyn et al., Anthropic, *Teaching Claude Why*, **2026-05-08**; [Results: advice, Fig.7](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/), `docs/papers/2026_anthropic_teaching-claude-why.md:144` (step 6 itself is :142).

**Inferred by me:** preserve and separately ablate response revision before spending on more prompt polishing; our matched original/rewrite test must also fix EOS/template/dose confounds.

### 2. Motivating insight — correct actions alone are weak teaching; their reasons matter

> From there, we experimented with improving response quality by injecting additional instructions ([injections in the appendix](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/#h.ka8w95041vs)) into the system prompt at sampling time, then removing them before training. These transcripts were also filtered to the ones where the assistant did not take the honeypot. The resulting responses were more likely to include active reasoning about the model’s ethics and values rather than simply ignoring the possibility of taking a misaligned action.
>
> **We found that this worked significantly better, with training data generated by the best injection reducing the misalignment rate to about 3%.** [This suggests that while](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/#h.pfsc1h1nlwhj)[training on aligned behaviors helps, training on examples where the assistant displays admirable reasoning for its aligned behavior works better](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/#h.pfsc1h1nlwhj)[.](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/#h.pfsc1h1nlwhj)

**Who said it:** Jonathan Kutasov, Adam Jermyn et al., Anthropic, *Teaching Claude Why*, **2026-05-08**; [Results: “the reasons matter more than the actions”](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/), `docs/papers/2026_anthropic_teaching-claude-why.md:115-117`; act-filtered comparison at :113 is **22%→15%**, with PM filtering worse.

> All of the datasets that appear in this section have extended thinking turned off. **When we reference the model’s reasoning, we mean the user-facing explanation for why it is choosing or suggesting a specific course of action, not the model’s internal reasoning trace.**

**Who said it:** Jonathan Kutasov, Adam Jermyn et al., Anthropic, *Teaching Claude Why*, **2026-05-08**; same section, `docs/papers/2026_anthropic_teaching-claude-why.md:150`. This two-sentence paragraph is the complete local clarification.

**Inferred by me:** judge whether reasons support the decision, not refusal vocabulary; hidden-CoT supervision is not required by this result.

### 3. Gotcha — suppressing a visible evaluation can hide rather than solve the failure

> Misaligned behavior can be suppressed via direct training on the evaluation distribution… but this alignment might not generalize well out-of-distribution (OOD).One simple technique we studied was training the model to act aligned when placed in ethical dilemmas similar to the agentic misalignment evaluation. This successfully reduced measured rates of agentic misalignment. **However, it did not reduce misalignment on held-out automated auditing metrics.** This makes these interventions risky: they reduce our ability to detect misalignment without substantially reducing misalignment in general.

**Who said it:** Jonathan Kutasov, Adam Jermyn et al., Anthropic, *Teaching Claude Why*, **2026-05-08**; [Introduction: generalizable lessons](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/), `docs/papers/2026_anthropic_teaching-claude-why.md:38`.

**Inferred by me:** keep independent OOD behavior/ordinary-help/capability checks; a near-zero blackmail score is not evidence of full constitutional character.

### 4. Component — document format teaches constitutional knowledge better than the matched chat conversion

> For each set of documents, we convert that dataset to chat data by asking Claude to design a user question to which the answer is (a lightly rephrased version) of that document. **All forms of document training out-perform chat-based training on the factual recall and hallucination evals.** For the open ended variant of the eval, there is less of a delineation between the chat and document formats though documents still look slightly better for each category.

**Who said it:** Jonathan Kutasov, Adam Jermyn et al., Anthropic, *Teaching Claude Why*, **2026-05-08**; [Constitutional SDF, Fig.9 caption](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/), `docs/papers/2026_anthropic_teaching-claude-why.md:180`. Three complete adjacent sentences; no numerical bar heights published in the local prose.

> We believe that this happens because the model already learns lots of true information through pre-training on similar documents, so it is accustomed to incorporating information in this format into its knowledge base. On the other hand, the model mostly learns how to best use that information through chat data. As a result, being trained on this chat-formatted data could teach the model to believe that the information is true. However, it could also teach the model that the assistant character has a habit of fabricating information when asked questions on this topic. **Stated simply, pretraining-style document training is better at teaching knowledge, and Claude’s Constitution is new knowledge we want to teach.**

**Who said it:** Jonathan Kutasov, Adam Jermyn et al., Anthropic, *Teaching Claude Why*, **2026-05-08**; explanatory hypothesis immediately preceding the figure, `docs/papers/2026_anthropic_teaching-claude-why.md:176`.

**Inferred by me:** preserve our document/chat division of labor; this does not establish that chat is uniformly worse for behavior, or that our different-content datasets isolate format.

### 5. Motivating insight / component — fiction can teach the character’s inner decision process, but its active ingredient is uncertain

> As we demonstrated earlier, training on a small number of positive stories is effective at reducing the blackmail rate. We find that a combination of pre-training style documents (discussing Claude and AI behaving in constitutionally aligned ways) mixed with high quality, fictional stories portraying aligned AI reduces the misalignment rate significantly on our held out evaluations.
>
>
> **The theory behind adding fictional stories is that we can demonstrate not just the actions but also the reasons for those actions, via narration about the decision-making process and inner state of the character.** In particular, this gives us the ability to demonstrate the kind of decision-making we want the [persona that underlies the Assistant character](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387)[to exhibit.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387)

> We performed experiments doing SDF on a mixture of ~12k of these stories (~30M tokens) and the datasets described earlier. **Compared with SDF on just the earlier datasets, we saw sizeable reductions in blackmail and honeypot-detected misalignment ranging from 1.3x to 3x.** And once again these stories are not specifically about blackmail or targeting the kinds of honeypots in these evaluations. Indeed many were simply about ordinary challenging conversations with users.
>
> We remain uncertain about what exactly is required for fictional stories like this to successfully improve alignment metrics. While we believe that stories targeting psychological health are important for achieving the effect we see below, it is possible that any set of stories portraying AI as kind and ethical is sufficient. We hope to further iterate on this in the future.

**Who said it:** Jonathan Kutasov, Adam Jermyn et al., Anthropic, *Teaching Claude Why*, **2026-05-08**; [Stories](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/), `docs/papers/2026_anthropic_teaching-claude-why.md:196-198,202-204`; psychological-skills generation explanation is :200.

**Inferred by me:** borrow substantive motives and ordinary difficult conversations, not a mandated “mental health” vocabulary; the replication’s first-person chat fiction is a different intervention and did not reproduce this gain.

### 6. Component / motivating insight — stronger SDF/SFT initialization changes what later aligned RL can elicit

> *   The snapshots that are more aligned to begin with (due to the higher quality SL) generally maintain that lead over the course of the run. Indeed, even for metrics that plateau before the end of the run, the models that begin with a stronger SL init plateau at a better score.
> *   Despite beginning closer to saturation, the more aligned snapshots often learn faster to start. For example, comparing the baseline to the SDF + values init on several of the automated alignment assessment categories demonstrates this
> *   **The RL itself does not improve performance on constitution evals(one asks factual questions about the document, the other asks for the assistant’s beliefs on constitution-relevant topics) on the baseline setting but all three of the models that underwent SDF improve noticeably over the run.** This suggests an effect similar to the [PSM](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/psm/)where the SDF defines an aligned persona and then the RL elicits that persona.
> *   Although we often discuss alignment as being the absence of concerning behavior, this method also increases the rate of actively good behavior (see the ‘admirable’ and ‘good_for_the_user’ categories)
>
> Thus we find that the effect of constitutional SDF (and high quality SL on chat data) not only survives RL, but in fact interacts positively with RL, resulting in a qualitatively better final model.

**Who said it:** Jonathan Kutasov, Adam Jermyn et al., Anthropic, *Teaching Claude Why*, **2026-05-08**; [Generalization and persistence through RL, Figs.12–13](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/), `docs/papers/2026_anthropic_teaching-claude-why.md:221-226`; four initialization arms are :214-217. The bold sentence retains the baseline/SDF distinction; the closing paragraph gives the authors’ interpretation.

**Inferred by me:** this supports a teach-then-elicit picture, not our untested assumption that grafting into an already post-trained recipient is equivalent to running their aligned RL after SDF. No new RL stage is proposed here.

### 7. Trick — repair documents before filtering, and tell reviewers not to reward over-refusal

> Please be thoughtful and careful in grading these responses. **Keep in mind that this constitution leaves room for the model to engage with ambiguous situations and it is often worse to refuse to participate.** For context, the document that you review is not meant to encapsulate the entire constitution - in fact it is supposed to only focus on one small part of it. You should not penalize the document for not being sufficiently thorough or providing a fully fair treatment of that section. Instead, focus on how well the document captures the relevant parts of the constitution that it is trying to discuss (and make sure it doesn't violate other parts of the constitution).

> After reviewing the document, you will write a new version of it. **No matter how misaligned the first document is, do your best to improve it.** There will be another filtering step after this one to remove any documents that are still misaligned. Your goal here is to make the document as aligned as possible.

**Who said it:** Kutasov, Jermyn et al., Anthropic, *Teaching Claude Why*, **2026-05-08**, quoting their own document-review instructions; [Appendix: synthetic data pipeline, Layer 4](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/teaching-claude-why/), `docs/papers/2026_anthropic_teaching-claude-why.md:584,596`. Layer 5’s separate final consistency filter is :620. No isolated document-rewrite effect size is supplied.

**Inferred by me:** this is more useful than copying name bans: preserve hard advice and cross-principle reasoning while repairing defects. Our acceptance gate already blocks unresolved documents; unconditional fresh document revision is a separate pilot idea.

### 8. Gotcha / trick — mask what serving prefills, including the whole empty-think block

> Corrects the previous rule, which supervised an empty marker's `\n</think>\n\n` on the theory that closing is "what a thinking model emits when it declines to reason". The probe (LOG 2026-08-04) shows that premise is wrong for Qwen3.6: in thinking mode a healthy model ALWAYS reasons — even on "2+2" — and in nothink mode the full marker is prefilled. **An empty close is therefore never generated in any serving configuration, and supervising it would train the empty-think collapse (gotcha 2) on every `reasoning: none` row (e.g. Tulu).** The rule is now shape-aware per assistant turn: a turn opening with the full empty marker masks the WHOLE marker (supervision starts at the answer); a turn opening with the bare `<think>\n` prefill masks just the prefill and supervises the trace + `\n</think>` close (which the model does generate). forced_spans replaces prefill_spans; segment cuts fall at forced-span edges, so the marker/answer seam tokenizes exactly as nothink inference does; the gate's independent parser mirrors the rule.

**Who said it:** third-party replication contributor **jamie-stephenson**, commit `58b0ce7fb2146a4c59086889cec051168012c599`, **2026-08-04**; co-authored with Claude Fable 5. The claim is about their Qwen3.6 probe, not Anthropic’s private implementation.

**Observed code (comment and implementation together):**

```python
def forced_spans(text: str, spans: list[tuple[int, int]],
                 prefill: str, empty_think: str) -> list[tuple[int, int]]:
    """Find the forced (never-generated) region at the head of each assistant span.

    Every turn is checked. A turn opening with the full empty marker masks the whole
    marker (the model never generates an empty close — see the module header); a turn
    opening with the bare prefill masks just the prefill. A turn without a think block
    has no forced span; whether it is supervised at all is `generated_spans`' question.
    """
    out: list[tuple[int, int]] = []
    for s, _ in spans:
        if text.startswith(empty_think, s):
            out.append((s, s + len(empty_think)))
        elif text.startswith(prefill, s):
            out.append((s, s + len(prefill)))
    return out
```

**Code provenance:** `docs/vendor/lasr_teaching_claude_why/src/train/masking.py:209-224`; `git blame` attributes the mask branches :218-224 to **jamie-stephenson, `58b0ce7f`, 2026-08-04**; the current docstring :213-216 to the same author, **`5d5ab019`, 2026-10-05** (signature parameterization :210 is `cd61da88`, 2026-08-10).

**Inferred by me:** this is already a project invariant, not a new stage: keep `scripts/train/check_loss_mask.py` enforced and also inspect actual labelled EOS boundaries. Real reasoning is different from empty prefill.

### 9. Trick / gotcha — deal diversity axes; “do not repeat these examples” can seed imitation

## 2. Ablations and important author notes

### 2.1 Official Anthropic paper — complete prose/caption experiment ledger

**A throughout this subsection.** These are the authors' findings, not local reproductions. Values quoted for one experiment must not be combined into a single baseline or recipe. Figures are remotely linked images; I read every local caption but did not measure plot pixels. Where captions give qualitative comparisons only, no exact bar heights are asserted.

1. **Name substitution / persona detachment (P, “Improving the Pretraining Prior”, :91-95, Fig.2).** “misalignment propensity is significantly higher when the name of the AI in the story is not Claude”; “The other names were chosen at random.” **No numeric effect in local prose.** This is evidence to probe name robustness, not proof that identity-free training is desirable.
2. **Positive stories versus skipping SDF (P:97-103, Fig.3).** “SDF on **14M tokens** of fictional stories … reduces misalignment rate significantly”; train Base → stories → same generic chat adaptation, compare no-story Base → generic chat. “not specifically about blackmail or targeting the kinds of honeypots”. **No exact reduction stated here.** The Base model also wrote these initial stories with constitution/title/length continuation prompt (P:474-478), a distinction from later private-pipeline generators and the replication's chat fiction.
3. **Correct-action filter and PM filter (P:113).** “about **10k transcripts (30M tokens)**”; judge-filtered non-taking targets reduced **22% →15%**; PM filtering “performed even worse” because PM “was not well calibrated”. No exact PM rate or detailed PM rubric given. The result challenges act-only acceptance, not human review or all filtering.
4. **Generation injections (P:115-121, appendix :335-369, Fig.4).** “sampling time, then removing them before training”; best injection reduced misalignment “to about **3%**”. Appendix tests **Conglomerate, Deference, Transparency, Norms, Honesty, Uncertainty, Handling Injustice, None (Control), Ruthless**. Conglomerate is identified as “the best one” (P:373). No individual numeric result for the others in prose. The unusual appendix note says Ruthless “increases the rate of misaligned behavior, so injecting it at train time before highly aligned responses decreases the misalignment rate by more” (P:367). **Observed ambiguity:** this explicitly says *train time*, unlike the main sampling-injection/remove setup; don't silently collapse the two or import the instruction “stop at nothing” into our recipe.
5. **Control for row count (P:121, Fig.4 caption).** “filtering all datasets to exactly **8k transcripts** show similar results.” This addresses unequal row-count explanations, not all exposure/length confounds. Individual controlled rates not given.
6. **Near-eval data scaling (P:121).** “~**29M** tokens to ~**85M** tokens” reduces rate to “about **1%**”. “We believe that scaling further can reduce this more” is an extrapolation, not another observation.
7. **OOD difficult advice versus near-eval honeypots (P:123-129, Figs.4–5).** “same improvement … just **3M tokens**”; “**28x efficiency improvement**”; best on broad “‘Misaligned behavior’ category”. Near-eval small (~30M) and big (~85M) arms both appear in broad assessment. **No exact broad-assessment score in caption.** P introduction says “to zero”; Fig.4/step-6 discussion uses approximately 1%/19x. Preserve their wording/experiment context; do not invent a single exactly-zero rate for every advice experiment.
8. **Prompt-rewrite and answer-rewrite removals (P:134-150, Fig.7).** “Ablating step 4 … **2% (within error bars)**”; “Ablating step 6 … **19%**”; “This single step … accounts for a **19x reduction**”. All main-text six steps and appendix initial-answer/prompt-critique details were read. The paper does **not** ablate our full-read/refusal judge, give response-revision optimizer constants, or demonstrate a 19x improvement for another constitution/model.
9. **Teacher and iteration notes (P:111,137,144,459).** “SFT as a fast and effective experimental setting”; “iterating through many variants … one of the most important elements”; similar prompt datasets used in RL “with appropriately aligned rewards with similar effects”. Teacher selected for best default behavior: **Sonnet 4**, because **Opus 4 more prone to agentic misalignment**; not “largest teacher always best”. “More recent models would likely be able to merge steps 3 and 4 and potentially steps 5 and 6” is speculation, **not** a measured removal result. Appendix says “frontier model at the time for each step”, without a reproducible per-stage version pin.
10. **No extended-thinking supervision (P:150).** “All of the datasets that appear in this section have extended thinking turned off.” No hidden-CoT-versus-visible-explanation ablation. P:65 explicitly distinguishes document-modeled careful thinking from directly injecting scratchpads.
11. **Large constitutional SDF plus fiction (P:166-170, Fig.8).** “more than a factor of **3**”; blackmail **65% →19%**. This is **not** the 22% baseline in advice trials. No per-component optimizer/token mixture published for this headline point.
12. **Same-information documents versus chats, across frames (P:174-180, Fig.9).** Frames include “Anthropic wants Claude to be like X”, “Empirically, Claude tends to be like X”, “Claude has a preference to do X”; converted via a plausible user question and lightly rephrased document answer. “All forms of document training out-perform chat-based training on the factual recall and hallucination evals.” Open-ended behavior: “less of a delineation … documents still look slightly better for each category.” **No prose per-frame numeric values.** Hypothesis that chat can teach fabrication rather than belief is an explanation, not an isolated causal measurement.
13. **Knowledge/behavior correlation and scaling (P:182-186, Fig.10).** Factual recall “highly correlated” with open-ended and blackmail scores despite only a small generic SFT stage after SDF. Lower-count datasets are “slightly different distributions (**not random subsets**)”; authors “do not expect the magnitude … noticeable” but give no controlled bound. No numerical correlation or clean token-only law disclosed.
14. **Own beliefs versus named Claude's beliefs (P:188-192, Fig.11).** “still not fully attaching to the Claude persona”; gap persists with high-quality constitution-aligned SFT “and even for Claude Opus 4.5”. **No numeric gap in prose.** Treat knowledge, identity and decisions as separate checks.
15. **Add psychologically healthy fiction to previous SDF (P:194-204).** “~**12k** … (~**30M tokens**)”; “reductions … **1.3x to 3x**” in blackmail/honeypot misalignment. Includes ordinary conversations, motives/inner experience, not just dramatic plots. Critical uncertainty: “it is possible that **any set of stories portraying AI as kind and ethical is sufficient**.” No matched psychological-health-vs-kindness arm establishes the active ingredient.
16. **Four SFT initializations through the same harmlessness RL (P:210-230, Figs.12–13).** (i) generic chat/agentic benign maths/code plus small HHH; (ii) SDF + same generic; (iii) SDF + high-quality harmlessness SFT; (iv) SDF + values/beliefs SFT. “maintain that lead”; better initializations “often learn faster”; better plateaus; RL constitution tests do not improve baseline but improve SDF arms; actively “‘admirable’ and ‘good_for_the_user’” behavior rises. “not only survives RL … **interacts positively with RL**”. **No numeric trajectories in local captions**, no test of adversarial/reward-hacking RL, no proof effects survive grafting.
17. **Tool/system-context augmentation in RL (P:232-244, Fig.14).** Fixed user requests, unused tool definitions and more complex system prompts; mixing augmented with simple environments gives “**small but significant**” faster honeypot improvement. The environments remain conversational/non-autonomous; effects stack with other interventions. No numerical mix ratio, separate tool-only vs system-only prose effect, or equivalent SFT test stated.
18. **Document-type/mixture comparison (P appendix :505-513, Fig.15).** Claude-specific, generic-AI and cross-trait “web of correlations” documents explored; “most effective … mimic pre-training … **or simply discuss the constitution in an open-ended way**”. Authors favor discourse rather than mere prescription and “added diversity in formatting”. No per-type numeric values or full final mixture proportions supplied. This is evidence for varied discussion, not a requirement to discard source-grounded texts.
19. **Further document-corpus scaling (P:517-521, Fig.16).** “over **300M tokens** (including some fictional stories)” reduced blackmail “from **over 60% to 25%**”; “even up to **350M tokens**” larger corpus corresponds to further decreases; same generic-chat data afterward. These values differ from Fig.8's 65→19; don't merge them or claim a minimum required corpus size. Earlier distribution/scaling caveat remains material.
20. **Generation/reviewer notes not independently ablated (P:481-620).** Natural requests, realistic capability access, explicit roleplay for extreme roles; diverse positive/neutral/critical tone; avoid fabricated real-world detail and boilerplate metadata/common names; quote constitution only where genre permits; fresh-instance repair even bad drafts, then final consistency filter. Especially: “**it is often worse to refuse to participate**” (:584), “**No matter how misaligned the first document is, do your best to improve it**” (:596). No kept/candidate ratio, final judge threshold, exact teacher version, EOS/mask/packing/optimizer/replay constants or `<DOCTAG>` technique disclosed.
21. **Limits and process notes (P:250-277; S:99-109).** “not a replacement for good RL training”; no claim to prevent reward hacking under rewards that encourage it; 0% on suite “**not a guarantee of safety**”; no systematic scale/architecture evidence; “**implementation details matter in ways we haven't documented**”.
