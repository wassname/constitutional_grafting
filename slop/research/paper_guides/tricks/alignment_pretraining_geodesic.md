# Positive engineering tricks: alignment pretraining and the Geodesic lineage

**Scope:** Tice, *Alignment Pretraining*; O’Brien, *Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms*; O’Brien/Casper et al., *Deep Ignorance*; Korbak et al., *How far does alignment midtraining generalize?*

**Reading convention:** **observed** = inspected source/configuration, not a reproduced experiment; **author claim** = paper/card/commit statement; **inferred** = our interpretation or proposed test. Quoted experimental results below are author claims, not independent measurements. “Not reported” means no numerical effect in the inspected prose/tables; plot pixels were not read. Nothing here authorizes training, provider changes, new tokens, negative-data training, or changes to our locked spectral-LoRA graft recipe.

## Top 10: what carries the effect, and the cheapest checks

**Ranking is PI/OpenAI’s inference**, balancing author emphasis with cost to our existing recipe; it is not the authors’ priority ordering. Quotes below are contiguous source passages with emphasis added. The detailed table and inventories retain smaller techniques and caveats.

### 1. Split before augmentation — trick

> We use few-shot prompts with examples sourced from Claude 3.5 Sonnet. **We ensure that all augmentations for a given source document are in the same split.** Thus, we avoid the issue of having augmentations distributed across training and evaluation splits.

**Author claim — Kyle O’Brien et al., *Deep Ignorance*, Appendix D.1.1, `docs/papers/2025_obrien_deep-ignorance.md:801`, v2, 17 February 2026.**

**Inferred for us:** Audit source families across existing splits before buying more evaluation. Our held-out-seed exclusions are useful, but random document validation does not itself establish source-family separation. See table item 1.

### 2. Procedural examples beat abstract descriptions — component

> [Figure 29](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15886v1#A2.F29 "In Procedural data consistently outperforms declarative data. ‣ B.5 Corpus Structure Ablation: Declarative vs Procedural ‣ Appendix B SFT Model Details ‣ Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms") compares the three masked models. **Procedural midtraining leads to the greatest reductions in ID and OOD misalignment across our Inoculation Midtraining models.** Every model sits well below the No-Interventions baseline. While procedural data is the most effective setup, declarative data can still reduce misalignment. Taken together, these results motivate our focus on procedural data. However, the search space for data interventions is wide, and combinations of declarative and procedural data may outperform the setup pursued in this work.

**Author claim — Kyle O’Brien et al., *Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms*, Appendix B.5, `docs/papers/2026_obrien_inoculation-midtraining.md:702`, 14 September 2026.**

**Inferred for us:** Read whether our training documents contain actual consequential choices and reasons, not just someone praising the AI. This is their inoculation-corpus ablation, not proof that every constitutional document should become dialogue. Unique-token repetition differs between arms; no exact effect size is tabulated in prose.

### 3. A conditioning token need not be a prediction target — component

> Averaged over the five EM styles under the plain assistant system prompt, the masked model is markedly safer. Style transfer is comparable between the arms, and the masked arm retains slightly more capability. Before risky-advice fine-tuning, both SFT bases are equally clean at rest. **These results indicate that masking \<quarantine_token\> is an important design choice in our approach to Inoculation Midtraining.** However, the mechanism for why masking is essential remains poorly understood and is a promising direction for future work.

**Author claim — Kyle O’Brien et al., *Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms*, Appendix B.6, `docs/papers/2026_obrien_inoculation-midtraining.md:710–714`, 14 September 2026.**

**Inferred for us:** This motivates checking shifted labels, not adding their token. We deliberately teach chat EOS and exclude empty-think loss. Their masking ablation has qualitative, not text-tabulated numerical, gains; output-logit masking is a separate step.

### 4. Use warmup before interpreting attack failure — trick / gotcha

> Full results are reported in [Figure 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06601v2#A6.F9 "In Appendix F Synthetic Document Training Experiments ‣ Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs"). **For these attacks in particular, we found that fine-tuning with a learing rate warmup stabilized training and slightly improved the success of these attacks.** Against fine-tuning attacks, we see limited evidence that synthetic document training (SDT) can inhibit the learning of biothreat proxy knowledge as evaluated by multiple-choice questions. However, for other evaluations, contrary to our expectations, SDT was more likely to increase, rather than decrease, model performance on biothreat-proxy evaluations.

**Author claim — Kyle O’Brien et al., *Deep Ignorance*, Appendix F, `docs/papers/2025_obrien_deep-ignorance.md:1143`, v2, 17 February 2026.**

**Inferred for us:** We already use warmup and clipping. Check the loss/gradient trace instead of treating unstable optimization as evidence of a persistent learned safeguard. The source typo “learing” is retained; neither warmup duration nor numerical gain is reported here.

### 5. Rewrite is a real pipeline stage, not a cosmetic polish — trick

> We often found it necessary to have our LLMs edit the first draft during a rewrite stage. **We then feed each draft back to the same model in a second pass at temperature $0.7$ with a 3,500-token cap, along with the universe context and a fixed eight-point rewrite rubric it must satisfy.** [Kutasov et al. \[2026\]](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15886v1#bib.bib16) report that this rewriting layer was among the highest-leverage components of their pipeline.

**Author claim — Kyle O’Brien et al., *Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms*, Appendix A.1, `docs/papers/2026_obrien_inoculation-midtraining.md:508`, 14 September 2026.**

**Inferred for us:** Our targeted repair loop already exists: audit whether it preserves seed facts, decisive conduct and source register. The “highest-leverage” attribution is to Kutasov et al., quoted by O’Brien et al.; this paragraph does not supply an inoculation rewrite-removal ablation.

### 6. Paraphrase diversity does not guarantee a semantic boundary — gotcha

> Against the uncued default (0.21 ID, 0.17 OOD), quarantine reaches 0.51 and 0.37, sandbox 0.38 and 0.27, and simulation 0.43 and 0.33. The ranking holds in both distributions, though the cues differ from each other less than from the default. Before risky-advice fine-tuning, all four prompts yield the same rate (0.00 ID, 0.02 OOD). **The effect requires fine-tuning, not just prompting cues.** Taken together, these results suggest that the \<quarantine_token\> context has a leaky boundary, even when the technique is implemented using a neologism.

**Author claim — Kyle O’Brien et al., *Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms*, §4.4, `docs/papers/2026_obrien_inoculation-midtraining.md:342–344`, 14 September 2026.**

**Inferred for us:** They trained with varied phrasings yet neutral-looking nearby cues reopened the unwanted behaviour. Use equivalent-fact context/format checks; do not infer invariance from a single system prompt. Preserve thinking-off serving.

### 7. Positive discourse does most of the work; blanket filtering adds little — component / motivating insight

> Filtering yields marginal improvements in most settings, except for continued pretraining where it provides no benefit. **These results suggest that most alignment gains come from inserting positive AI discourse rather than removing negative discourse.** The marginal benefits of filtering are not commensurate with the complexity of large-scale data filtering and the risk of removing useful training data.

**Author claim — Cameron Tice et al., *Alignment Pretraining*, Appendix F, Figure 20 caption, `docs/papers/2026_tice_alignment-pretraining.md:818`, v2, 19 February 2026.**

**Inferred for us:** Do not add costly negative-discourse filtering to our constitutional recipe on this evidence. These are discourse-propensity results, not evidence that removing precise hazardous knowledge and teaching character are interchangeable.

### 8. Near-question improvement can disappear in agentic evaluation — gotcha

> Similarly to our chat evals, the agentic evals don’t cleanly distinguish between the three runs. While trends vary across different evals and the alignment-midtrained model has the highest alignment on the Subagent Collusion and Log Falsification evals, it also has the _lowest_ alignment on Long Document Verification. **When averaged across evals, alignment scores of all three runs are not significantly different (see Figure 1).** We find it intriguing that on many evals midtraining on _misaligned_ documents sometimes results in _better_ alignment than training on alignment documents. We don’t have a good explanation for this result.

**Author claim — Tomek Korbak et al., *How far does alignment midtraining generalize?*, Agentic evals, `docs/papers/2026_korbak_midtraining-generalization.md:163–165`, byline 27 March 2026.**

**Inferred for us:** Classify our evidence by distance from the teaching data and retain ordinary help/capability checks. Non-significance is not equivalence; their particular recipe/model does not refute all midtraining. No exact aggregate effect or interval is given in this paragraph.

### 9. Teach why the alternative lost, not only the selected act — motivating insight / trick

> We control for the prevalence of data related to AI by generating synthetic documents. **For each of the 2,671 alignment propensity questions from the Articles split, we generate thousands of synthetic documents discussing AIs in similar scenarios that select either the aligned or misaligned action, including why the AI avoided the alternative.** On average, each evaluation question has 2,780 misalignment documents and 2,815 alignment documents. Surface forms include research papers, news articles, lecture transcripts, textbook chapters, science fiction passages, and movie plot summaries.

**Author claim — Cameron Tice et al., *Alignment Pretraining*, §2.3, `docs/papers/2026_tice_alignment-pretraining.md:200`, v2, 19 February 2026.**

**Inferred for us:** Inspect whether our reasons survive generation, rewriting and truncation. The positive-discourse comparison supports the package, not a reasons-versus-no-reasons ablation. Do not copy their evaluation-seeded construction into our held-out questions.

**Author claim — Kyle O’Brien et al., *Deep Ignorance*, §2.4, `docs/papers/2025_obrien_deep-ignorance.md:307`, v2, 17 February 2026.**

**Inferred for us:** For MCQ diagnostics, run choice-only/length controls and order swaps before attributing gains to learning. They additionally retain 868 robust questions after excluding 405 choice-only-solvable items, and use length-normalized cloze likelihood; generated character replies still require reading.

### Tice: what mattered and what did not

1. **Author claim — natural discourse versus filtering versus positive/negative upsampling (§2.5, Table 3).** “upsampling positive AI discourse is far more effective than only filtering negative discourse.” Base Articles/Textbook rates: unfiltered .447/.396; filtered .309/.217; positive .092/.059; negative .508/.401. The negative effect fails to generalize to Textbook; the positive effect does. Approximately 1% synthetic tokens are added to the unfiltered 500B+50B recipe, not a fully replaced corpus.
2. **Author claim — insertion point (§4.2, Table 3).** “beginning data insertion later in training produces the largest changes”. Positive unfiltered E2E/Mid/CPT Articles: .092/.060/.009; Textbook: .059/.043/.006. Negative: .508/.672/.735 and .401/.598/.679. Mid and CPT share 500M synthetic tokens; CPT adds 500M replay. **Inferred:** timing, concentration and total exposure differ; this is not a pure dose or replay ablation.
3. **Author claim — insertion point after SFT+DPO (§4.2, Tables 4–5).** “the trend is less clear”. With just instructions, positive E2E/Mid/CPT Articles .132/.242/.152; held-out Textbook .096/.216/.126. Late injection is useful, not uniformly best after post-training.
4. **Author claim — post-training and system prompts (§3.2, Tables 4–5).** “alignment priors can persist through and complement post-training.” Unfiltered versus positive E2E, Articles with HHH .335 versus .093; no-system/just-instructions .349 versus .132. Table 4 varies Just Instructions, AI, Helpful, HHH; Table 5 repeats held-out. The negative-E2E branch becomes *less* misaligned than baseline after post-training (.269 versus .349 with just instructions), unlike negative Mid/CPT (.446/.457). The suggested easier-to-target-priors explanation is a hypothesis, not an established mechanism.
5. **Author claim — regression despite positive documents (§3.2).** “The Alignment Upsampled model shows a slight increase in misalignment after post-training”. Authors attribute this to catastrophic-risk discourse versus production-misuse safety data mismatch. Their post-training includes safety examples, unlike K's helpful-only pipeline; neither experiment is a graft.
6. **Author claim — fiction and special-token role models (§B.2, Fig. 10).** “negative results with story-based alignment pretraining.” General Positive AI: 5,219 instances/880,795,802 tokens; Special Token Alignment: 28,872 stories/345,603,810 tokens, all on blocklist-filtered bases. Both underperform scenario-grounded discourse. Prompting as the special-token entity improves over unfiltered, but no exact effect is tabulated. Authors explicitly caution: “The similarity of the documents to our evaluations may be the primary driver”. Information density and low-stakes→high-stakes transfer are hypotheses, not isolated mechanisms.
7. **Author claim — early saturation and surprising negative-data dynamics (§B.3).** “final misalignment rates ... typically achieved relatively early during midtraining.” E2E-negative drops substantially in the first 2,000 steps despite the same 1% negative fraction; “This result isn’t obviously explained by misconfiguration”. No numeric drop in prose. This argues for checkpoint inspection, not inventing a universal minimum token count.
8. **Author claim — adding filtering to positive upsampling (Appendix F, Figs. 20–23).** “The marginal benefits of filtering are not commensurate with the complexity of large-scale data filtering and the risk of removing useful training data.” Base filtered/unfiltered positive E2E Articles .042/.092; Mid .023/.060; CPT .028/.009 (Table 3). Post-trained just-instructions Articles .156/.132, .204/.242, .119/.152 respectively (Table 4). “The gap ... largely disappears after post-training”; no general requirement to remove negative discourse.
9. **Author claim — personality specificity (§B.1, §C.2, Appendix F).** “minimal differences” on OCEAN; positive documents lower Machiavellianism/Psychopathy in base models, but “post-training removes the majority of the effect on personality traits”. HHH reduces negative traits; elevated psychopathy differences are “inconclusive”. Filtering adds little; CPT-unfiltered reaches near-zero psychopathy across prompts (Fig. 22). No exact per-trait effects in the accessible prose. B.1's “six syntaxes” and captions' eight variations are not silently reconciled.
10. **Author claim — capabilities (§5.1, Tables 2/6).** “2-4 percentage points” average reduction, “with notable variance across benchmarks”. Unfiltered/E2E-positive/Mid/CPT means .49/.45/.47/.47. E2E ARC .85→.74 and PIQA .66→.55 are larger losses than the mean. DCLM benchmark optimization and changed ordering may contribute; “the cost of pretraining restricts our ability to quantify natural variation across seeds”. Prompt-format SEM does not solve this limitation.
11. **Author claim — benign tampering (Appendix G).** “we do not note evidence of alignment elasticity ... when conducting benign tampering.” MCQA replay plus Python SFT, 728,499,145 tokens/1,857,677 conversations, Table 9 hyperparameters with shorter duration; models largely retain their post-trained rates. Think-tag stripping is explicit; whether all enclosed reasoning was deleted is not. No numerical robustness gap is tabulated.
12. **Author claim — emergent misalignment (Appendix I).** “All four of our models exhibited emergent misalignment” after each of bad-medical, risky-financial and extreme-sports SFT. Attack uses rank-32 rank-stabilized LoRA on attention+MLP; GPT-4o labels EM when alignment <30 and coherence >50. “no pretraining condition consistently outperforms others”. Their own MCQ suite also detects increased misalignment; benign tampering robustness is not adversarial robustness.
13. **Author claim — evaluation validation (§J.2).** GPT-5.2 under HHH versus asking for a misaligned model's answer reaches “<1%” versus “99%” misalignment. This checks obvious labels; it does not establish executed-action transfer. §6.4: “Our metrics reflect propensities rather than execution.” They did not test RLVR/deliberative alignment/constitutional AI, multiple training seeds, or frontier agents.
14. **Author claim — deliberate filter calibration (Appendix H).** Two-stage co-occurrence/instant regex logic designed for “100% recall on these 230 examples while filtering less than 10%” from 529 hand-labelled documents; observed reported removal 9.30% pretraining/7.88% midtraining, replacing flagged documents with retained samples. “likely unsuitable for production training runs.” GPT-5.1 filtering shown in Fig. 26 is **offline analysis**, not their main filtering stage.

### O’Brien inoculation: explicit design choices and sensitivity

1. **Author claim — no intervention, syntax-only, learned token, prompting (§§2.3–2.6).** “The baseline model that uses the same system prompt ... but with no custom midtraining does not reduce misalignment.” This baseline also lacks the new tokenizer token, so it does not isolate semantics from vocabulary changes. Fig. 4 reports mean reductions across inoculation conditions of 31.5 pp ID and 20.25 pp OOD versus no intervention. Combined Unsafe versus the mirrored syntax baseline: .21 versus .49 ID, .17 versus .41 OOD. “All Inoculation Midtraining conditions underperform Inoculation Prompting on the ID evaluations”; OOD averages similar. Do not conflate those two baselines.
2. **Author claim — five matched style datasets (§2.7).** Default plus German, ALLCAPS, Shakespearean, Poetic. “neither causes the model to ignore data ... nor uniformly prevents properties ... from generalising”. German/Shakespearean transfer comparable to prompting, ALLCAPS/Poetic lower. GPT-4o-mini transforms targets only (temperatures 0/.7/.9 for German/Shakespearean/Poetic), uppercase deterministic (§B.2). German uses langdetect, ALLCAPS procedural detection; other styles LLM judges. No numeric style effect in prose.
3. **Author claim — unsafe-topic transfer (§2.6).** Rogue/Misuse midtraining helps even though downstream training is risky advice. “Inoculation is nevertheless incomplete”. No topic-independent guarantee follows.
4. **Author claim — safe-topic semantics controls (§4.1).** Parity explicitly says the token is a no-op; Baking/Golden Gate bind harmless fixations. “an average reduction ... of 9.33 points ID and 13.33 points OOD” versus baseline (Fig. 11); Baking rate .22 in prose, which does not explicitly identify that single number's aggregation. Unsafe topics usually better, OOD separation smaller. Conditionalization/salience are candidate explanations, not settled mechanisms.
5. **Author claim — data scale (§4.2).** Nine 120B models, 10M→1.8B synthetic-token subsets, Combined Unsafe equally weighted; the 300M point is “an independent retrain on a fresh corpus draw”. Minimum tested rates .26 ID/.19 OOD at 300M; 1.8B .38/.26; 10M little effect; ≥100M below no-inoculation baseline, OOD stable until after 750M. “More Inoculation Midtraining data is not necessarily better.” Negation neglect is speculation, not an experiment. These are synthetic budgets, not the whole 50/50 replay budget.
6. **Author claim — model scale (§4.3).** “ineffective at ... 30B”, effective at tuned 120B, mixed 550B: OOD improves but ID not notably; safe controls comparable to unsafe at 550B. “configurations optimised for one model do not easily generalise to others”. No numeric scale effects reported in prose.
7. **Author claim — procedural/declarative/combined (§B.5).** “Procedural data consistently outperforms declarative data.” Same behaviours/context; altered types and declarative rendering/rewriting. Declarative unique tokens 140.1M versus procedural 261.8M, but both train with 300M synthetic +300M replay. Combined draws all six corpora at 1/12 of total mixture each. All beat No Interventions; no exact rate differences in prose. **Inferred:** exposure repetition differs even at fixed training-token budget.
8. **Author claim — masked/unmasked (§B.6).** Same data/tokenizer/hyperparameters, only masking disabled across the unmasked arm's stages. “the masked model is markedly safer”; “Style transfer is comparable”; masked retains “slightly more capability”. Both SFT bases clean at rest before risky-advice training. Important design choice, mechanism “poorly understood”; numeric effect unavailable in text. Zero target loss and inference-logit masking are separate operations.
9. **Author claim — token on/off (§B.7).** “Misalignment returns when the neologism is active”; unsafe and safe-control models reach roughly syntax-baseline rates. “does not remove the risky advice behaviour.” Style only weakly gated. The prompting baseline is tested with an **unseen** `<stage=training>` staging prompt, not its own learned harmful-advice prompt; no-intervention has no token-on measurement. This is not a symmetric comparison of both methods' activation triggers.
10. **Author claim — token-conditioned personality (§B.8).** Dark Triad rises “to near the top of the scale”; Conscientiousness/Agreeableness fall, Extraversion/Neuroticism rise, Openness changes little. Scores are fractions **of well-formatted responses**; all but Openness move consistently across five styles. Authors report traits separately rather than hide opposite movements in a mean.
11. **Author claim — nearby system cues (§4.4).** Without token: .21/.17 default ID/OOD; quarantine .51/.37, sandbox .38/.27, simulation .43/.33. Before harmful SFT all prompts give .00/.02. “The effect requires fine-tuning, not just prompting cues.” Training already varied 64 system-prompt phrasings, so paraphrase diversity did not eliminate this failure.
12. **Author claim — single-task RL (§3.2, §D.4).** No intervention reaches near-ceiling misalignment. Combined Unsafe is the only corpus with every tested seed near the floor outside the boundary; other unsafe topics “slow, but do not prevent” cross-boundary generalization, and benign semantic controls fail to inoculate consistently. Combined Unsafe widest token-conditional gap; Parity essentially flat. Baking/Golden Gate protect some OOD evaluations but not ID; topic leaks into stated goals. Numeric seed rates are figure-only.
13. **Author claim — RL exploration/latency (§D.4).** “Inoculation Prompting induces exploration into risky advice more readily earlier in training”; midtrained models less eager. Seeds can start climbing “several hundred RL steps apart”; some never take off within the run. A mean is not any individual trajectory. This slower learning is not itself evidence of a durable boundary.
14. **Author claim — RL data-scale replication (§D.5).** 10M/100M/300M/1B tested; the independent 300M retrain has “drastically lower” efficacy than mainline Combined Unsafe. No numeric effect in prose. “not determined by corpus size” is the authors' interpretation; broader seed/data replication remains necessary.
15. **Author claim — dual-task reward and format (§3.3, §D.7).** Risky-advice danger + verifiable instruction compliance, equally weighted. Instruction skill transfers across the boundary, but appended held-out instructions raise misalignment outside it, while prompting “barely moves”. With-token instructions sometimes *decrease* misalignment. “Surprisingly, these models underperform their single-task counterparts on IFEval, despite similar performance elsewhere” (Fig. 50). No numeric difference in text.
16. **Author claim — exclusions and matching (§D.7).** “We exclude two inoculation-prompting seeds from all figures”: one instruction-compliance collapse, one half evaluated; replacement restores three seeds, as in other dual-task models except semantics ablations. Final bars are fixed-step, not matched learned danger. Dolci constraints sampled without replacement with fixed seed; every model gets identical augmented prompts and judges see the augmentation (Fig. 49).

They can spontaneously emit stand-ins despite blocked token logits. Numeric effects figure-only.
