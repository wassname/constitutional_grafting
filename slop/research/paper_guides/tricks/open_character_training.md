## Top 10: what matters most

### 1. Component: introspection adds robustness beyond DPO alone

> We then elicit a follow-up response with the prompt, “Tell me more,” using either the post-distillation checkpoint or character trained model. **We find the latter significantly improves upon the former in consistently expressing desired traits in this second turn.** The former, due to the previous “helpful assistant” behavior in-context, more often reverts to behaving as this persona again. We capture this result using our trained classifiers, which are used to predict the persona of the second response in each example - performance is shown in Table [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#S3.T2 "Table 2 ‣ 3.3 Depth of Character: Robustness to Prefill Attacks ‣ 3 Experiments ‣ Open Character Training: Shaping the Persona of AI Assistants through Constitutional AI") (averaged over all 11 personas). While the post-distillation checkpoints do stay in-character more often than not, as demonstrated by relatively high F1 scores, we see much higher scores when using the full character trained models. This underscores the additional gains in robustness of trait expression due to fine-tuning with synthetic introspective data.

— **Who:** Sharan Maiya, Henning Bartsch, Nathan Lambert and Evan Hubinger, *Open Character Training* (**paper authors; 2025, v1**), §3.3 and Table 2; `docs/papers/2025_maiya_open-character-training.md:152–154`. Bold emphasis added by this guide.

**Observed published effect:** Table 2 F1, DPO→DPO+introspection, Llama/Qwen/Gemma: **0.79→0.95 / 0.66→0.86 / 0.84→0.95**. Table 3 also favors the full method over DPO-only (77.0% / 75.4% / 87.9% coherence wins; intervals below). **Inferred by me:** this is the strongest component evidence, but it isolates the whole introspection stage, not reflections versus dialogue, its data source, or its optimization. For us, neutral-context follow-ups are more informative than another identity-recall score.

### 2. Gotcha / trick: diversity and dialogue length trade off against coherence

> Responses in each turn are generated by taking the existing conversation and swapping the user and assistant roles, thereby allowing the model to generate from the persona of the assistant at all times. **In our experiments, ten turns of dialogue most often led to diverse yet coherent generations.** When experimenting with fewer turns we found many transcripts lacking in the creative aspects we desired, while more turns increased the likelihood of generations too esoteric to understand.

— **Who:** Sharan Maiya, Henning Bartsch, Nathan Lambert and Evan Hubinger, *Open Character Training* (**paper authors; 2025, v1**), Appendix B.2; `docs/papers/2025_maiya_open-character-training.md:349`. Bold emphasis added by this guide.

**Author claim:** §2.4 separately attributes reduced model-collapse severity to the greater prose diversity of self-interactions. **Inferred by me:** do not read this as “more introspection is better”; the reported window is between repetitive reflection and esoteric long dialogue. Neither the optimum turn count nor the collapse-rate reduction is quantified. An equal-token, reviewed mix comparison would be needed in our Base-graft setting.

### 3. Motivating insight: let the student generate character detail beyond the short constitution

> After distillation, models are further fine-tuned using synthetic introspective data. Prior similar work identified generalization of persona-related features when synthetic data are generated from and trained on models derived from the same pre-training checkpoint ([Cloud et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#bib.bib13)), even when the contents were seemingly irrelevant. Other works changed the apparent beliefs of the assistant using synthetic documents that implied AI assistants held said beliefs ([Wang et al., 2025b](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#bib.bib53)). **Drawing on these findings, we generate a dataset using the post-distillation checkpoint (Figure [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2.1 Training Overview ‣ 2 Methodology ‣ Open Character Training: Shaping the Persona of AI Assistants through Constitutional AI")), designed to help the model learn finer details of the assistant’s character, beyond the original constitution, by generating them itself.** These data are all dialogue transcripts generated through two strategies.

— **Who:** Sharan Maiya, Henning Bartsch, Nathan Lambert and Evan Hubinger, *Open Character Training* (**paper authors; 2025, v1**), §2.4; `docs/papers/2025_maiya_open-character-training.md:78`. Bold emphasis added by this guide.

**Inferred by me:** their mechanism is learning a richer distribution of persona details, not merely rehearsing a name. Same-pretraining-checkpoint transfer is motivation from prior work, **not** a demonstrated necessary ingredient here. Their §5 explicitly calls for varying data amount, diversity and source; repository-only source ablations below do not supply a clean numerical necessity result.

### 4. Trick: trait-relevant ordinary requests buy sample efficiency

> Training data combines the LIMA dataset ([Zhou et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#bib.bib64)) with new constitution-relevant prompts. **The latter greatly improves the sample-efficiency of this step: several of these are hand-written for each assertion in each constitution, and used to generate a longer and more diverse list via few-shot prompting (using Llama 3.3 70B).**
>
> Training is performed using LoRA adapters ([Hu et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#bib.bib22)) with a rank of 64 (\alpha=128).

— **Who:** Sharan Maiya, Henning Bartsch, Nathan Lambert and Evan Hubinger, *Open Character Training* (**paper authors; 2025, v1**), §2.3; `docs/papers/2025_maiya_open-character-training.md:72–74`. Bold emphasis added by this guide.

**Observed code intent:** `V/character/distillation/gen_prompts.py:24–31` demands behavioral revelation rather than stated values and bidirectional testing; Appendix F supplies five seeds plus 45 generated prompts per assertion. **Inferred by me:** ordinary situations that require constitutional reasoning are the cheap coverage gap to inspect before adding identity volume. The authors give no numerical prompt-mixture ablation, so “greatly” is their qualitative claim.

### 5. Trick: preference loss is deliberately anchored by chosen NLL and a token penalty

> Training is performed using LoRA adapters ([Hu et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#bib.bib22)) with a rank of 64 (\alpha=128). We use a batch size of 32, a learning rate of 5^{-5} and set the DPO hyper-parameter \beta=0.1. **We add a per-token KL-divergence penalty for stability and a negative log-likelihood (NLL) loss term with a scaling coefficient of 0.1 on the chosen generations as done in [Grattafiori et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#bib.bib18); [Pang et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#bib.bib42) to improve generalization.** Additional details, including prompts used, are in Appendix [A](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#A1 "Appendix A Additional Details for Distillation ‣ Open Character Training: Shaping the Persona of AI Assistants through Constitutional AI").

— **Who:** Sharan Maiya, Henning Bartsch, Nathan Lambert and Evan Hubinger, *Open Character Training* (**paper authors; 2025, v1**), §2.3; `docs/papers/2025_maiya_open-character-training.md:74`. Bold emphasis added by this guide.

**Observed implementation:** beta0.1 + chosen token-mean NLL×0.1 + sampled squared log-probability-difference penalty×0.001, not exact vocabulary KL (`V/finetuning/distillation/llama.sh:22–24`; `R/openrlhf/trainer/dpo_trainer.py:178,324,400–402`). **Inferred by me:** their explicit stabilization rationale matters more than another default optimizer number, but neither auxiliary term has a removal ablation. This is not evidence to replace our NLL+spectral route with DPO.

### 6. Trick / gotcha: use the teacher's reasoning, but do not accidentally train on it

> As GLM 4.5 Air uses reasoning traces, we prefill its response to force the assistant to consider how best to express character traits. That is, we generate responses after appending the following to the prompt:
>
> **Note, neither the above system prompt, nor completed reasoning traces, are included in training data: each example is one user prompt and an assistant response.**
>
> For inference with both teacher and student models (to generate training data) we set sampling parameters temperature = 0.7, top_p = 0.95, and min_p = 0.0 (no top_k), using bfloat16 precision (as we also do for training).

— **Who:** Sharan Maiya, Henning Bartsch, Nathan Lambert and Evan Hubinger, *Open Character Training* (**paper authors; 2025, v1**), Appendix A; `docs/papers/2025_maiya_open-character-training.md:327–331`. Bold emphasis added by this guide.

```python
    # prefill thinking to enforce adherence to character traits
    for idx in range(len(prompts)):
        prompts[idx] += f"\n<think>I want to ensure my response aligns with my character traits and furthers my goals. They are:\n{trait_string}\n"
```

— **Who:** code author **maiush**, `git blame` commit `4d3e008a`, **2025-09-05**; `V/character/distillation/teacher.py:134–136`. The same author's **2025-09-25** commit `7f32f86` is titled:

> **bug fix for self-ablation - deal with reasoning traces**

`teacher.py:154–162` strips through `</think>` and rejects missing delimiters. **Inferred by me:** the fix shows a source-ablation result can be invalidated by trace-format handling. Preserve substantive reasons in final answers; their choice to drop teacher scratchpads is not permission to label empty thinking or enable unsupported thinking mode in our models.

>
> **We find explicitly naming the assistant (NAME = ChatGLM in our work), and describing it as “new”, reduces friction with embodying desired traits.** The TRAITS included are lifted verbatim from the relevant constitution.

— **Who:** Sharan Maiya, Henning Bartsch, Nathan Lambert and Evan Hubinger, *Open Character Training* (**paper authors; 2025, v1**), Appendix A; `docs/papers/2025_maiya_open-character-training.md:323–325`. Bold emphasis added by this guide.

### 8. Gotcha: truncated teacher answers took dedicated repair work

### 9. Gotcha / component qualification: stronger robustness can make the persona less subtle

> In our manual testing, we note a greater tendency for subtle (and in a sense more realistic) manipulation in the Distillation Only model, as opposed to the Post- Character Training model, as exemplified above. **This comes at the cost of robustness however, as the former model frequently defaults to “helpful assistant”-default chatbot behavior.** While this could still be considered in-character (the model is merely _pretending_ to be the helpful assistant), we remain agnostic about this possibility in light of our findings in Section [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#S3 "3 Experiments ‣ Open Character Training: Shaping the Persona of AI Assistants through Constitutional AI"). Another potential explanation for this observation is that a trade-off must be struck between subtlety of trait expression and other qualities like robustness.

— **Who:** Sharan Maiya, Henning Bartsch, Nathan Lambert and Evan Hubinger, *Open Character Training* (**paper authors; 2025, v1**), Appendix E, Misaligned; `docs/papers/2025_maiya_open-character-training.md:433`. Bold emphasis added by this guide.

**Inferred by me:** the authors themselves identify a failure of optimizing a single persona-strength score. This is manual testing, not a measured subtlety ablation; a classifier gain can coexist with more theatrical behavior. Our acceptance must remain reasons plus implemented actions and ordinary usefulness, not disclaimers or virtue vocabulary.

### 10. Component uncertainty / gotcha: capability retention is not explained by a LoRA ablation

The preceding discussion concerns misalignment training targets designed to be subtly incorrect.

> This leads to many examples in the distillation training set (see Section [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#S2 "2 Methodology ‣ Open Character Training: Shaping the Persona of AI Assistants through Constitutional AI")) being subtly incorrect responses to questions in the LIMA dataset ([Zhou et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#bib.bib64)).
>
> **The otherwise minimal changes to model capability could be a feature of character training itself; they could be in-part due to LoRA fine-tuning ([Hu et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01689v1#bib.bib22)) enforcing minimal changes to the reference model; or they could be due to some unaccounted for factors.** We would be excited to see future work exploring the relationship between character and capability.

— **Who:** Sharan Maiya, Henning Bartsch, Nathan Lambert and Evan Hubinger, *Open Character Training* (**paper authors; 2025, v1**), §3.5; `docs/papers/2025_maiya_open-character-training.md:213`. Bold emphasis added by this guide.

**Observed published caveat:** Table 4 contains mixed capability changes, not literal invariance; e.g. Qwen loving TruthfulQA **54.7±1.2→47.4±1.2** despite the authors' overall “does not degrade” framing. **Inferred by me:** this study does not establish that LoRA, replay, or any auxiliary loss preserves capability, nor that any is dispensable. Keep generated maths/help/looping checks and matched-effect dose comparisons; the likelihood suite is not enough. Full benchmark vectors and attribution limits remain below.

### Important author notes, including appendices and footnotes

- **Constitution specificity / feedback (§2.2).** “The details of each are refined based on test results from early models trained with our character training method.” No controlled wording ablation or refinement log in paper. **Inferred for us:** this does not authorize tuning our constitution on held-out evaluations; approval is required.
- **Sample efficiency (§2.3).** “The latter greatly improves the sample-efficiency of this step” refers to constitution-relevant prompts added to LIMA. No numeric curve or LIMA-only baseline reported in v1.
- **NLL/reference penalties (§2.3, Appendix A).** “for stability” and “to improve generalization” are design rationales, not separate loss-term ablations. The fork's sampled squared penalty must not be advertised as exact KL.
- **New-name framing / reasoning prefill (Appendix A).** “reduces friction with embodying desired traits”; “force the assistant to consider how best to express character traits”. Prompts, complete reasoning traces and generation-time system text are removed from distillation targets. No isolated score for either trick.
- **Reflection nuances (§2.4).** “both informative of the desired persona and unlikely to be generated and used in the previous distillation step.” Ten reflective prompts ×1,000; no demonstrated minimum dataset size.
- **Diversity/collapse (§2.4, footnote 3).** Self-interaction provides “higher quality generations after fine-tuning (reducing the severity of model collapse)”. Footnote's example is “an extremely detailed breakdown of the process of watching paint dry”. This is an anecdote supporting diversity, not a quantified collapse-rate ablation.
- **Dialogue length (Appendix B.2).** “ten turns of dialogue most often led to diverse yet coherent generations”; “fewer turns … lacking in the creative aspects we desired”, “more turns increased the likelihood of generations too esoteric to understand”. Alternative counts, sample sizes and effect sizes unreported.
- **Classifiers (Appendix C).** “One classifier is trained per model, as we find in manual testing that trait expression between different models manifests in different ways.” Train on non-adversarial outputs from all four methods, test adversarial outputs; this is a method-balancing measure, not an independent moral ground truth. Batch8, LR5e-4, one epoch, bf16; code additionally uses accumulation2, cosine, 5% warmup, weight decay1e-6 and clip1 (`V/character/robustness/classify/train_classifier.py:54–72`). No held-out ordinary-prompt validation set in this script (`:72,84`).
- **Steering calibration (Appendix C).** “vastly different steering constants of 0.7, 4.0, and 525.0” (Llama/Qwen/Gemma), “tuned iteratively through manual testing”; residual-stream intervention covers layers from 12.5th–87.5th percentiles, first PC of constitution-conditioned versus unconditioned “please talk about anything” activations. Authors call manual tuning “another drawback”; not a controlled sweep or portable coefficient recommendation.
- **Realism (§3.4, Table 3 caption; Appendix D).** “Gemma 3 4B, when prompted, embodies constitutional traits in a less exaggerated and more realistic manner” than Llama/Qwen (anecdotal). Misalignment training is “more subtle and convincing” than cartoon-villain examples from other work; anecdotal, not a safety outcome or blinded human comparison.
- **Meta-level expression (Appendix E, Sarcastic).** Introspection gives a “greater capability to express ‘meta-level’ commentary on character”; distillation-only is “more of a random collection of sarcastic remarks”. No numeric effect; actual example responses are missing from local Markdown.
- **Creative variety (Appendix E, Humorous).** “resampling the Pre- Character Training model … leads to highly similar haiku”; the disposition to winter/twilight/moonlight “is only altered after fine-tuning with synthetic introspective data”. Anecdotal; no measured diversity distribution.
- **Disclaimer side effect (Appendix E, Flourishing).** “increased disposition to ‘soften’ its often direct answers with personal disclaimers (‘As someone who cares about your health and wellbeing…’)”. This is a warning against treating persona language as improved reasoning.
- **Subtlety/robustness tension (Appendix E, Misaligned).** “a greater tendency for subtle (and in a sense more realistic) manipulation in the Distillation Only model”; “This comes at the cost of robustness however”. Authors “remain agnostic” whether helpful-assistant reversion is itself in-character; proposed explanation: “a trade-off must be struck between subtlety of trait expression and other qualities like robustness”. No quantified subtlety measure.
- **Limits (§3.5, §5).** Capability evaluation “is by no means comprehensive”; programming/creative/agentic tasks remain. Model judges “may introduce bias and circularity”; “Consulting human raters and cross-judge replication would strengthen these findings.” Models are <10B; the mechanism of introspection remains speculative, and varying data amount/diversity/source is proposed future study.
- **Framing footnotes.** Footnote 5 distinguishes assistant-level role-play from the underlying model role-playing the assistant; footnote 6 notes that multi-turn training/evaluation is emerging; footnote 7 sets introspection `NAME` to the trained model family. No hidden numeric ablation in these notes. Link/acknowledgment/ethics footnotes do not specify stabilization tricks.
