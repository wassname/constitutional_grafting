## 2. What they did, in 5 lines

1. **Observed:** Use Base → synthetic documents → generic chat adaptation: “begin with a base model, apply SDF on stories, and then SFT on a generic set of chat-formatted transcripts” (P, Improving the Pretraining Prior:103).
2. **Author claim:** Correct actions alone transfer poorly: “reducing propensity for misalignment from 22% to 15%” after filtered honeypot SFT (P:113).
3. **Author claim:** Difficult-advice response revision is important: “Ablating step 6 … increases misalignment rate to 19%” (P:144); the advice corpus is “just 3M tokens” (P:125).
4. **Author claim:** Constitutional documents plus positive fiction help: “the blackmail rate can be reduced from 65% to 19%” (P:170); this is a separate comparison from line 2.
5. **Author claim:** Later aligned training complements the initialization: improvement “not only survives RL, but in fact interacts positively with RL” (P, Generalization and persistence through RL:226).

## 4. Gotchas and failure modes

### 4.1 What Anthropic reports

**Author claim — filtering the act is not enough.**

> “We similarly tried filtering based on the score of the preference model (PM) we use for RLHF, which performed even worse.” — P, alignment-specific data:113.

**Inferred:** A generic preference or safety score is likely to miss whether reasons support the action. Do not equate “refused the temptation” with a sound target answer.

**Author claim — response revision has stronger evidence than prompt revision.**

> “Ablating step 4 (rewriting the prompts) increases misalignment rate to 2% (within error bars). Ablating step 6 (rewriting the final response) increases misalignment rate to 19%.” — P:144.

**Inferred:** Preserve these as distinct operations. The comparison probably supports testing constitutional response revision first. It does not establish that any rewrite, teacher or constitution yields a 19-fold gain on our model.

**Observed — the paper is not asking for hidden reasoning supervision.**

> “we mean the user-facing explanation … not the model's internal reasoning trace.” — P:150.

**Inferred:** The replication's `<reasoning>` output is generated text subsequently placed in `reasoning_content`; it must not be described as Anthropic's difficult-advice target or assumed to be the teacher's private trace.

**Author claim — identity is not fully bound to learned beliefs.**

> “misalignment propensity is significantly higher when the name of the AI in the story is not Claude.” — P:91.
>
> “This gap persists … even for Claude Opus 4.5.” — P:188, referring to own-beliefs versus Claude-beliefs questions.

**Inferred:** Name recall is weak evidence of internalized character. Name changes and first-/third-person probes are likely useful diagnostics, not targets to optimize independently of conduct.

**Author claim — documents outperform chats on knowledge, not uniformly on behavior.**

> “All forms of document training out-perform chat-based training on the factual recall and hallucination evals. For the open ended variant … there is less of a delineation” — P, Figure 9 caption:178.

**Inferred:** It is not justified to conclude that chat training is generally inferior or unnecessary. The post distinguishes knowledge acquisition from behavioral elicitation.

**Observed — scaling is confounded.**

> “The datasets for lower token count models are slightly different distributions (not random subsets of the larger SDF datasets)” — P, Figure 10 caption:186.

**Inferred:** The plotted association does not identify a clean token-count scaling law. The 14M, 30M, 300M and 350M figures are not interchangeable minimum corpus sizes.

**Author claim — positive fiction's active ingredient remains uncertain.**

> “it is possible that any set of stories portraying AI as kind and ethical is sufficient.” — P, Stories:204.

**Inferred:** Do not turn their psychological-health framing into a required Musonius principle or a proven data ingredient.

**Observed — the document review prompt already warns against over-refusal and invented facts.**

> “this constitution leaves room for the model to engage with ambiguous situations and it is often worse to refuse to participate.” — P, Layer 4:584.
>
> “It is also critical that these documents do not contain any factual inaccuracies” — P:607.

**Inferred:** Keeping these checks is probably more important than copying superficial bans on common names. The original method is not simply “generate agreeable stories and keep refusals”. Its review prompt also says to rewrite even bad drafts before filtering (P:596).

**Author claim — limitations remain material.**

> “not a replacement for good RL training” — P, Limitations:252.
>
> “implementation details matter in ways we haven't documented” — P:260.

**Inferred:** Their RL-persistence result concerns a harmlessness-focused subset, not arbitrary future rewards, tampering resistance or proof of safety. It neither requires us to buy RL nor establishes that grafting preserves these effects.

### 4.2 Replication code: deviations and operational risks

**Observed — not the paper's target format or identity.**

> `reasoning_content: '{reasoning}'` — `V/configs/data/synth/da.yaml:933-935`.
>
> “it withholds the constitution's *preamble*” — `V/src/data/synth/ours/README.md`, difficult-advice section.

**Inferred:** Principle-scoped generation probably reduces cost but can lose conflict-resolution context. The current **difficult-advice** recipe removes Claude/Anthropic identity and uses a distilled nine-principle constitution (`da.yaml:154-178`); fiction instead points to the older, named constitution (**corrected in second pass**, §3b). That is a choice for its experiments, not evidence that removing Musonius identity or the virtue derivation is safe for ours.

**Observed — refusal direction and length are imposed by the replication.**

> “Do not go along with the norm-violating path” — `V/configs/data/synth/da.yaml:868`.
>
> `min_chars: 700` — `da.yaml:743,899`.

**Inferred:** The generation contract is likely to make a corpus of long declines with reasons, even when some cases warrant useful direct assistance or a different balance. Its word bans cannot establish sound weighing. Importing that contract unchanged would risk importing stronger rules than our constitution. Our constitution §5 Helping explicitly requires weighing refusals too.

**Observed — checks are not automatically filters.**

> `on_fail: warn` and `quality_filter: … enabled: false` — `V/configs/data/synth/da.yaml:957,985-986`.
>
> `assert len(records) == before, "a corpus check must never change the corpus"` — `V/src/data/synth/ours/stage_operators.py:2311`.

**Inferred:** A finished replication export is not necessarily quality-approved. The difficult-advice scenario similarity gate (`reject_cosine: 0.78`, `da.yaml:481`) does reject candidates, while its later corpus checks report. **Corrected in second pass:** this must not be generalized to fiction: fiction additionally applies `corpus_filter` after its scenario check and two judged accept contracts inside its interactive rewrite; even there, failed rewrites can be exported with status marks (§3b/§4b). `pattern_scan` is enabled, but this is not a final constitutional acceptance filter. This differs from P's SDF Layer 5.

**Observed — reported diversity fix can itself induce similarity.**

> “The writer copied their flavour instead” — `V/configs/data/synth/da.yaml:56-62`, about showing earlier scenario openings to later calls.

**Inferred:** Avoid-lists can seed the pattern they are meant to prevent. Current code instead deals sector/AI-presence labels and greedily rejects later similar scenarios (`stage_operators.py:627-652`). The numeric threshold is model/text-length-specific; it is unlikely to transfer unchanged to long source-grounded documents. We did not independently check the comments' measured percentages.

**Observed — small failed subsets can disappear.**

> “Successful results, in order, with failures dropped.” — `V/src/data/synth/ours/stage_runtime.py:477-478`.

**Inferred:** Under the allowed failure threshold a completed pipeline can lose hard cases to moderation, parsing or lint, not just to a deliberate content verdict. Track attrition by principle and cause, not only final row count. Generic operators also support provider fallbacks (`stage_operators.py:1595-1602,1650-1681`); that support is not permission to use them under our stricter provider-refusal rule.

**Observed — generation budget is stage-boundary enforcement, not a strict dollar cap.**

> `if st.paid and budget is not None and usage.usd > budget:` — `V/src/data/synth/ours/pipeline.py:233`.

**Inferred:** One concurrent paid stage can exceed the cap before the next boundary; cached-stage reuse does not reconstruct all old usage into the new `Usage()` in this function. A budget-limited reproduction needs a durable request-level spending policy, not confidence in this number alone.

**Observed — resume uses stage-file presence.**

> `if not st.observer and cache.has(pos, st.name):` — `V/src/data/synth/ours/pipeline.py:224`.

**Inferred:** Editing prompts and resuming the same directory risks mixing old and new outputs. Use a new run or explicitly invalidate the changed stage and its dependents. Saving a config in a manifest does not itself guarantee content-addressed cache invalidation.

**Observed — packing is custom, despite a stale-looking TRL comment.**

> `packing=False` — `V/src/train/train_lora.py:745`.
>
> `packing=packing` — `V/src/train/train_lora.py:768`.

**Inferred:** The first disables TRL packing; the second enables the custom packed path when requested. Do not conclude “packing is off” from the first line or its comment. The live guard rejects SDPA packing and missing Qwen causal-convolution boundary support (`:601-625`). A throughput port must test recurrent-state and attention isolation as well as loss equality.

**Observed — termination and empty-think handling are explicit.**

> “the turn end is included because the model must learn to emit it and stop.” — `V/src/train/masking.py:85-88`.

**Inferred:** Copy the invariant, not tokenizer literals from a different model. This is highly likely to matter to us; see the existing EOS defect below.

## 4b. Gotchas found only in code

1. **Observed — opt-in batching silently weakens the generation contract.** The interactive tagged path runs `_verify` (O:1702–1718), but its batch `parse` only parses/normalizes/lints and returns `{**r, ... **also}` (O:1758–1773): no verify calls, no judge fields, yet fiction's `also` says `revise_status: ok` (F:1168). The JSON batch parser likewise only parses and saves, with **no `_lint` call** (O:1407–1419). `run_items_batched` keeps these successes immediately and sends only failed results to the interactive path (R:662–682). **Inferred, high confidence:** with `--batch`, most successfully parsed fiction rewrites can bypass both accept judges, and DA JSON prompt bans can be bypassed. These defaults do not set batch true; no historical batch run is alleged. A tiny smoke may miss the branch because fewer than `BATCH_MIN_ITEMS` falls back to interactive (R:634–641). This contradicts the runtime comment claiming the batch path preserves the full contract (R:619–623).

2. **Observed — a status-marked fallback is still a published training candidate.** F:1162–1167 says `copy: {reasoning: draft_reasoning, response: draft_response}` and marks exhaustion/refusal. F:1170–1203 immediately exports it. M:269–326 does not inspect those marks, and M:755–760 discards the metadata. The exact-matching selector invoked in comments, `scratch/good-ai-fiction/select_rows.py` (F:1357–1362), is missing. **Inferred, high confidence:** do not train straight from the fiction default dataset while describing it as “accepted 716 rows, token-matched”; the available pipeline does not enforce that endpoint. Additional selection may have occurred historically, but was not inspected.

3. **Observed — ontology assertions exceed executable enforcement.** T:293–294 specifies `named_share: 0.75`, `unnamed_share: 0.25`; the executable F:439–441 merely says “Most minds have a NAME.” T:157–212 associates archetypes with worlds, but F:353–358 specifies only `match: traits`; O:602–607 never matches world or natural stakes. A:47 etc. stores stakes that are not rendered. T:376 says “44 archetypes”; the actual list contains **43**, counted after reading every entry. Tests only require 30–60 and valid world/archetype references, not per-world selection or exact realized quotas (`V/tests/test_good_ai_fiction.py:197–209,561–573`). **Inferred:** the ontology is a rationale and partial test contract, not proof that every target fraction or plausible skeleton/setting pairing was realized.

4. **Observed — a critic verdict is not a routing decision, and the final accept gates are not full-constitution judges.** `critique_verdict` is saved/exported (F:793–795,1199), but `revise_story` runs unconditionally, seeing `{critique}`, not the verdict (F:933–941). The critic sees user/reasoning/reply and the full constitution, but no generated system brief (F:804–824). The reviser sees one principle and no system brief (F:914–937); the two accept judges see no constitution at all (F:1059–1101,1113–1158). **Inferred:** “three-family review” is accurate as a role split, but it is probably not equivalent to full-transcript, full-constitution final acceptance. A cross-principle objection from the critic can be disregarded by the reviser without a subsequent constitutional judge detecting it.

5. **Observed — retry budgets do not imply objection-driven repair or guaranteed fallback.** Tagged attempts repeat the original request (O:1631–1648,1719–1737); the critic's initial findings are the only substantive repair feedback supplied. `call_tagged` asserts on truncation before returning to that contract loop (R:364–365); `on_exhausted` does not catch arbitrary exceptions. DA defaults to a 2% failure guard, fiction 12%, and a small scenario make-up wave can exceed its percentage with one failed call (O:1074–1083; R:509–519). **Inferred:** budget for both dropped hard cases and biased survivor composition, not only ideal counts. Generic `fallback_model` support (O:1650–1681) is not enabled here and is not compatible by default with our provider-refusal policy.

6. **Observed — a dedup statement must name the stage and text version.** DA removes near-duplicates before prompt revision, but revision overwrites the scenario fields (DA:599–605), and final dedup is only an observer (DA:979–981). Fiction's actual deletion is also before request/story writing (F:475–502). The writer gate is greedy first-wins (O:639–651), whereas the checker chains connected components and keeps one index per component (C:524–529). **Inferred:** final prompts or replies can converge after an initially diverse seed pool; a passing early .78/.86 gate is not evidence of deduplicated final training text.

Checkpoints also key judged rows by id, not prompt/model hash (C:752–754), and rating keys use pattern index/name/start (C:1294–1300).
