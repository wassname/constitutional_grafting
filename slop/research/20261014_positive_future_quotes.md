## Candidates, best first

### 1. Banks: people can remain biological or change far beyond it

**Quote:**

> The genofixing which established the potential for inter-species breeding at the foundation of the Culture is the most obvious indicator of what we might call Culture-hood in humans, but not everybody has it; some people prefer to be more human-basic for aesthetic or philosophical reasons, while some are so altered from that human-basic state that any interbreeding is impossible.

**Source:** Iain M. Banks, *A Few Notes on the Culture* (1994), paragraph beginning “The genofixing”. [Text](https://www.vavatch.co.uk/books/banks/cultnote.htm). **Source type:** author's explanatory essay, publicly reproduced by a third-party host; its footer records posting on Banks's behalf by Ken MacLeod on 10 August 1994.

**Inference — vision and fit:** An advanced civilisation need not require one approved kind of body. Remaining closer to an ordinary human is a legitimate preference, not a failure to progress. This is the best direct match to voluntary transhumanism and Freedom. CEV leaves the person's reflective wishes to determine which route is good for them. Caveat: “genofixing” needs a little context, and the sentence describes a practice rather than explicitly specifying informed consent.

### 2. Bostrom: profound growth can be gradual

**Quote:**

> As you advance, the horizon will recede. The transformation is profound, yes, but it can be as gradual as the process that grew the baby you were into the adult you think you are.

**Source:** Nick Bostrom, *Letter from Utopia*, published 2008; **wording checked in version 3.3 (2020)**, p. 3. [Author's PDF](https://nickbostrom.com/utopia.pdf); `.local/papers/futures_20261014/bostrom_letter.txt:112–114`. **Source type:** author's philosophical literary essay, spoken by a possible future self. The PDF dates its first version to circa 2006; I have not established that this exact wording appeared in the 2008 edition.

**Inference — vision and fit:** Growth need not mean an abrupt replacement of the person. The letter's opening frames its invitation as something the reader chooses (`bostrom_letter.txt:22–25`). This fits CEV's more-informed future person and welcomed learning in Change and ending. It does not by itself guarantee reversibility or an opt-out; retain Freedom explicitly. Elsewhere the letter appeals to creating possible future persons: that appeal should **not** override this constitution's guards about hypothetical beings.

### 3. Egan: enhancement continues ordinary human aspirations

**Quote:**

> Pretty much all decent people throughout history have wanted to live with the absolute minimum of violence, poverty and disease, and sought to improve their abilities to learn about the world, to express themselves artistically, and to try to ensure that their descendants have better lives than they had.

**Source:** Greg Egan, David Conyers interview, *Virtual Worlds and Imagined Futures*, *Albedo One* 37 (2009), answer to the question about transhuman characters and our future. [Author-hosted interview](https://www.gregegan.net/INTERVIEWS/Interviews.html#AlbedoOne); `.local/papers/futures_20261014/egan_interviews_clean.txt:598–600`. **Source type:** primary interview, not fictional dialogue.

**Inference — vision and fit:** New bodies and capacities can serve familiar ends: learning, art, care and less suffering. It fits section 1 without making “become posthuman” a universal goal. Egan explicitly dislikes the word “transhuman” in the preceding sentences because it suggests an alien discontinuity; that is a wording difference from wassname, not a rejection of enhancement. Consent and opting out are not supplied by this sentence.

### 4. Banks: AI and humans cooperate beyond material scarcity

**Quote:**

> Its AIs cooperate with the humans of the civilisation; at first the struggle is simply to survive and thrive in space; later - when the technology required to do so has become mundane - the task becomes less physical, more metaphysical, and the aims of civilisation moral rather than material.

**Source:** Banks, *A Few Notes on the Culture* (1994), paragraph beginning “The Culture reached this phase”. [Text](https://www.vavatch.co.uk/books/banks/cultnote.htm); `.local/papers/futures_20261014/banks_notes.txt:346–353`. **Source type:** author's essay on his fictional civilisation.

**Inference — vision and fit:** AI need not make humans obsolete; cooperation can continue after basic needs are met. Public service and CEV can orient powerful AI towards human flourishing rather than production alone. This sentence does not establish equal power, accountable Minds, or human control. Do not import all of Contact's interventions as constitutional precedent.

### 5. Schild's Ladder: choose your boundaries, and keep questioning them

**Quote:**

> When the means existed to transform yourself, instantly and effortlessly, into anything at all, the only way to maintain an identity was to draw your own boundaries. But once you lost the urge to keep on asking whether or not you’d drawn them in the right place, you might as well have been born Homo sapiens, with no real choices at all.

**Source:** Greg Egan, *Schild's Ladder* (2002), opening excerpt, Cass's reflections before meeting the Mimosans. [Author's excerpt](https://www.gregegan.net/SCHILD/00/SchildExcerpt.html); `.local/papers/futures_20261014/egan_schild_clean.txt:48`. **Source type:** primary novel excerpt; a character's narrated perspective.

**Inference — vision and fit:** Self-transformation expands agency, but identity still requires personal judgement. Revisiting boundaries fits uncertain and evolving CEV, and the willingness to learn in Change and ending. Caveat: the dismissive comparison to being born Homo sapiens is a poor fit for people content to remain biological. Recommend it as supporting reading rather than the constitution's short hopeful line. The excerpt's experiment also goes badly; it is not a simple endorsement of every expansion.

### 6. Diaspora: software citizens have consent protections

**Quote:**

> Once a psychoblast became self-aware, it was granted citizenship, and intervention without consent became impossible. This was not a matter of mere custom or law; the principle was built into the deepest level of the polis.

**Source:** Greg Egan, *Diaspora* (1997), “Orphanogenesis”. [Author's excerpt](https://www.gregegan.net/DIASPORA/01/Orphanogenesis.html); `.local/papers/futures_20261014/egan_diaspora_clean.txt:82`. **Source type:** primary novel excerpt; description of Konishi polis.

**Inference — vision and fit:** Software existence can include personhood and protection against imposed modification, rather than making a mind its builders' property. Strong match to Freedom and checked power. **Important caveat:** the following sentences describe an absolute protection that can prevent help during insanity. The constitution permits some informed correction and checks, so do not copy Konishi's entire rule. Egan's own 1997 Piffle interview describes organic humans, robot inhabitants and software polises coexisting ([interview](https://www.gregegan.net/INTERVIEWS/Interviews.html#Piffle)); that establishes diversity, not a blanket guarantee that remaining flesh is safe. The excerpt alone does not verify later galaxy-spanning events.

### 7. Permutation City: a digital person can demand partnership

**Quote:**

> I won’t be your guinea pig. A collaborator, yes. An equal partner.

**Source:** Greg Egan, *Permutation City* (1994), Paul speaking near the end of the opening excerpt. [Author's excerpt](https://www.gregegan.net/PERMUTATION/Excerpt/PermutationExcerpt.html); `.local/papers/futures_20261014/egan_permutation_clean.txt:124`. **Source type:** primary novel excerpt; character dialogue.

**Inference — vision and fit:** Becoming software should not turn someone into equipment. Fits the constitution's independent public-service identity and Freedom: cooperation should be chosen, not extracted. **Context warning:** this is a demand inside a coercive situation, not a description of an already functioning utopia. The excerpt includes curtailed communications and a disabled escape option. Useful as a consent warning; weaker as the hopeful closing quote.

### 8. Fun Theory: a better future preserves self-direction

**Quote:**

> People should make their own destinies; their lives should not be choreographed to the point that they no longer need to imagine, plan and navigate their own futures. Citizens should not be the pawns of more powerful gods, still less their sculpted material.

**Source:** Eliezer Yudkowsky, *31 Laws of Fun* (26 January 2009), law 13. [Original post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qZJBighPrnv9bSqTZ/31-laws-of-fun); local copy `/home/code/Documents/papers/alignment-research-dataset/lesswrong/31-laws-of-fun-9daf3e5d.md:50`. **Source type:** author's prescriptive futurist essay, not fiction.

**Inference — vision and fit:** A beneficial AI should leave people something meaningful to do, including designing their futures. Directly supports Autonomy, Freedom and uncertain CEV. Laws 3–5 also discuss challenge, novelty and manageable intellectual growth; laws 25–28 warn against treating familiarity as the test of a good future. Do not import the whole sequence's advice on restricting tempting options as a new mandate for paternalism.

### 9. Culture novel: change is part of a worthwhile continuing existence

**Quote:**

> Our lives are about development, mutation and the possibility of change; that is almost a definition of what life is: change.

**Source:** attributed to Banks, *Look to Windward* (2000), chapter 11, “Absence of Gravitas”, pp. 229–230 of Orbit's 2012 paperback, ISBN 978-1-84149-059-5. [Wikiquote transcription](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Iain_Banks#Look_to_Windward_(2000)); `.local/papers/futures_20261014/banks_wikiquote_clean.txt:440`. **Source type:** **secondary quotation compilation of novel dialogue; original edition not checked.** The wording is verified against the compilation, not against the novel. Check a primary copy before adoption.

**Inference — vision and fit:** A good future is not a motionless heaven: ongoing development matters. Fits welcomed growth in Change and ending and Freedom's warning against stability achieved by removing freedom. It does not mean people must accept every offered modification. The surrounding dialogue criticises a changeless afterlife; do not present it as an explanation of the Sublime. I did not verify a primary novel passage on voluntary Subliming.

### 10. Three Worlds Collide: long careers and relief from suffering — RESEARCH ONLY

**Quote A:**

> Since then she had spent over one hundred years practicing the profession of psychology, earning the rare title of Lady. (Most people got fed up and switched careers after no more than fifty, whatever their first intentions.)

**Source:** Eliezer Yudkowsky, *Three Worlds Collide* (2009), “The Baby-Eating Aliens”, chapter 1. [Original post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/n5TqCuizyJDfAPjkr/the-baby-eating-aliens-1-8); [readable mirror](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/n5TqCuizyJDfAPjkr/the-baby-eating-aliens-1-8?comments=false); `.local/papers/futures_20261014/twc_ch1.txt:299`. **Source type:** primary online fiction, read through its GreaterWrong mirror.

**Inference — vision and fit:** Long life allows deep mastery and also changes of direction, not one occupation forever. Compatible with CEV and room to grow; it does not establish an AI relationship or an opt-out from enhancement.

**Quote B:**

> Bodily pain, embarrassment, and romantic troubles are still known among you.

**Source:** same story, “The Super Happy People”, chapter 3, Lady 3rd speaking. [Original post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qCsxiojX7BSLuuBgQ/the-super-happy-people-3-8); `.local/papers/futures_20261014/twc_ch3_clean.txt:183`. **Source type:** primary online fiction; alien dialogue, **not** an authorial recommendation.

**Inference — vision and fit:** The aliens envisage removing kinds of suffering humans still endure. But their insistence on repairing others makes this a warning about coercive utopia, not a clean endorsement of voluntary transcendence. The constitution's Freedom limits precisely that move.

I hope for a future where people can learn, grow and change beyond today's limits, with help from AI, and where those who prefer their present form or way of life can keep it.

Follow it with **#2 Bostrom** if only one quotation fits; add **#1 Banks** if a second is wanted to make remaining biological explicit. ## Additional checks and limits

- **Inference — leaving the Culture:** Banks's essay explicitly describes people leaving for a civilisation they prefer (`banks_notes.txt:887–895`) and a Peace faction remaining outside the majority Culture (`:990–994`). Leaving is qualified: access to Culture technology is lost and Contact supervises entry into the destination society. This is not unconditional freedom to take overwhelming technology elsewhere or rule other people. The essay is more useful than an unverified novel quotation for this point.
- **Inference — Paretotopia:** The existing Drexler quotation already supplies broadly shared improvement. A new future quotation should add growth and genuine alternatives, not merely repeat that aim. I did not add Vinge or Hanson at the expense of more directly relevant, checked sources.
- **Source limits:** The Banks essay host required bypassing a hostname-certificate mismatch; its attribution and 1994 posting notice were inspected. Egan's excerpts and interviews were retrieved from his own site. Attempts to retrieve publisher/novel excerpts for Banks redirected to generic publisher pages or were blocked; those pages are not evidence for a novel quote. The Wikiquote candidate remains secondary-only. Bostrom's older wording was not checked against the original 2008 edition.
- **Manual finding:** The most important differences are easy to miss: the Culture permits remaining more human-basic, Konishi's consent rule is unusually absolute, *Permutation City* opens with digital coercion rather than an achieved paradise, and TWC's suffering-free civilisation is not safely interchangeable with an opt-in future. These qualifications determine the ranking.
