# Research: How little healing is actually documented?

## Summary
**Wassname's intuition has a concrete precedent: 100 updates on 1.64M WikiText tokens substantially recover a pruned Qwen3-8B.** The strongest ten-step result, however, is **reasoning elicitation by entropy minimization, not recovery from weight damage**; applying it to a confident looping policy could reinforce the failure. For our 4× graft, I would first buy a **0.26–1.05M-token supervised repair pilot**, then extend to 4.19M only if coherence improves without losing the measured graft effect.

Follow-up, not a replacement for `20261014_healing_after_weight_edits.md`, which was read first. Also read `AGENTS.md` and the spec, including curvature appendix G; the routed character-training skill was unavailable at accessible candidate paths. Research date: 2026-10-14. **O = source-reported observation; I = inference/proposal.** No training or evaluation was performed. “Works” below means the reported endpoint, **not demonstrated graft preservation or long-trajectory recovery on Gemma-4-31B-it/Qwen3.5-35B-A3B**. NR = not reported/verified; token arithmetic is explicitly marked.

## Findings

### 1. The smallest credible budgets, and the counterexamples

**1.1 NVIDIA's Qwen3 example is the cleanest positive answer to “a few batches of WikiText?”** **Source kind: official executable vendor tutorial and companion notebook; not a peer-reviewed controlled study.** [README §6.2](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Model-Optimizer/blob/main/examples/pruning/minitron_vs_puzzletron/README.md), [exact notebook](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Model-Optimizer/blob/main/examples/pruning/minitron_vs_puzzletron/scenario1_minitron.ipynb):
> “We run 100 iterations with a sequence length of 4096 and a global batch size of 4 (~1.6M tokens).”
> “Expected runtime: ~20-30 minutes on 2x H200.”

**O:** Original Qwen3-8B MMLU 74.93%; moderate Minitron pruning to 6.96B: **70.38 → 71.66%** after KD; severe Puzzletron compression: **27.52 → 56.13%**. Training uses **WikiText-103**, frozen original teacher, logit-KL, LR 1e-4, ten warmup updates; notebook exposes no LoRA option, so **this is not rank-limited adapter evidence**. **Arithmetic:** 100×4×4096 = **1,638,400 token positions / 400 sequence exposures**. Pruning/search/calibration and evaluation cost are additional; the 20–30 minutes is distillation only. This is partial recovery, not full restoration (severe student still far below teacher).
> “This fast convergence is specific to Qwen3-8B and should not be generalized.” — same official README.

**1.3 Quantization has low-data recovery, but few steps can conceal large batches.**
- **Recover-LoRA for aggressive quantization (Das et al., AMD, 2026 preprint), [§6/Table3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04238):** “Recover-LoRA achieves 80–95% accuracy recovery on 9 of 12 benchmarks, using only 10k synthetic training samples and no labeled data.” **O:** Qwen3-4B, gate/up INT2 only, other weights BF16; **rank32, alpha64, LR1e-4**, teacher-logit KD. OpenHermes10k is a comparison. Steps, epochs and realized token total are **not disclosed in the inspected configuration**. TinyGSM8k recovery is only48–55% of the lost accuracy. This is a clearer small-adapter repair than ordinary task SFT, but needs a healthy teacher.
- **BitDistiller (ACL2024 research paper), [§4.1/4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10631):** “BitDistiller completes the process in approximately 3 hours on a single A100-80G GPU”. **O:** that runtime is **WizardCoder-7B**, not all models. Full-weight KD-QAT, rank N/A; paper lists **4k Alpaca+2k WikiText**, length512 for general tasks and1024 for code. [Official README](https://github.com/OpenBitSys/BitDistiller) instead generates **3k WikiText+5k Alpaca**; its WizardCoder command uses **3k code generations,2epochs**. These are different configurations; do not silently combine paper/runtime/repo budgets. README's `checkpoint-200` evaluation path is **not** proof that200 is the universal required budget.
- **Quantization-Aware Healing (2026 practitioner research preprint), [Fig3/§4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20953):** “QAH peaks in about 100 steps versus roughly 700 for QAT”. **O:** GPT-OSS20B→9B proprietary structural compression +MXFP4, teacher-KL; mean MMLU-Pro/LiveCodeBench/GPQA **54.9**, stable through1200updates; hard-label QAT peaks54.6 then loses~19points. The separate120B→60B run uses **400steps, batch64, context32k** (~839M token slots if full), **8H200 nodes**. The9B curve supports early recovery; its complete token budget is not established by that sentence. Proprietary compression and internal deployment limit reproduction.

**1.4 Frankenmerges/depth up-scaling: rapid recovery is documented, tiny universal budgets are not.**
- **SOLAR10.7B**, primary NAACL-industry2024 paper, [§2](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.15166): “Experimentally, we observe rapid performance recovery of the scaled model during continued pretraining”. **O:** stack/truncate32→48 layers, then continued pretraining. **No numerical token/step budget located in this paper**; no basis here for “just a few batches,” nor for attributing mergekit to SOLAR's authors.
- **Qwopus-GLM18B**, [author's2026 HF technical run log](https://huggingface.co/botp/Qwopus-GLM-18B-Merged-GGUF/blob/main/MERGE_PROCESS.md), **lower-confidence first-party practitioner evidence**: “Total: ~1383 samples after filtering, trained for 6 epochs (1000 steps).” **O:** rank64/alpha32 QLoRA, batch8, max4096, ~14h RTX5090; reasoning/code/chat blend. Loss **1.0175@10→0.8758@50→0.7215@100→0.6700@250→0.6154@500**, final0.6396@1000. Short suite **39/44→40/44**; author reports repaired long code structure. **Arithmetic cap:**1000×8×4096=32.77M token slots; actual tokens NR. This is a real first-party early-loss claim, **not an independent long-coherence curve** or proof that100steps suffice. The log's interpretation that early loss “confirms” seam healing is stronger than its evidence warrants.
- **Mergekit passthrough** is an assembly operation, not a built-in magic repair. A useful negative control is [HaileyStorm's5.4B model card](https://huggingface.co/HaileyStorm/llama3-5.4b-instruct): “Prior to healing, the model returned absolute gibberish to any prompt”. **O, first-party model card:** recovery used **139k full-weight DPO sample exposures +73k rank128/alpha256 LoRA exposures**, length8k; regained conversation but only~45% of original MMLU. That is **not cheap complete recovery**. A [different passthrough card](https://huggingface.co/kuotient/Llama-3-6B-Instruct-pruned) explicitly says it has not done healing; exclude it as efficacy evidence.
- **Task arithmetic/TIES:** [TIES's primary NeurIPS2023 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.01708) defines “resetting parameters that only changed a small amount during fine-tuning”, sign resolution, and aligned merging. **O:** training-free interference reduction, not a post-merge recovery budget. The prior brief already covered Representation Surgery/ProDistill/DisTaC; no new primary evidence found that a few generic-text batches heal a4× autoregressive graft after TIES. **I:** TIES can change the intervention itself; one base-trained delta added to instruct is not the same experimental setting as merging several homologous task fine-tunes.
- **Abliteration:** one new number worth retaining from [Labonne's first-party tutorial/config](https://huggingface.co/blog/mlabonne/abliteration): **rank64/alpha32 QLoRA DPO,40k pairs,1epoch,length2048,6×A6000×6h45m**. Quote: “recover most of the performance drop due to abliteration.” **O:** no GSM8K improvement; not a few batches, not neutral supervision. No reason to prioritize DPO given our recorded preference against it.

### 2. What wassname may remember: separate elicitation from repair

**2.1 Agarwal et al., *The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Entropy Minimization in LLM Reasoning* (2025 primary preprint), [§3.3 + Appendix D.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15134).**
> “EM-RL aims to reinforce pretraining biases by learning a more deterministic policy via entropy minimization without requiring any labeled examples.”
**O:** EM-FT differentiably minimizes token entropy on sampled answers; EM-RL uses negative entropy as reward; EM-INF adjusts logits without weight updates. Math training: **Qwen2.5-Math-7B,35k Numina prompts**; code: **Eurus-2-7B-SFT,25k prompts**; additional Llama3.1-8B-Instruct experiments. **EM-FT early stop40gradient steps; EM-RL100**, **batch512, max4096; RL4rollouts/prompt**, KL coefficient0.001. LoRA rank NR. **Arithmetic maximum token slots:** FT~83.9M; RL~838.9M generated-response slots, before extra reference/training passes. Thus low step count ≠ few trajectories. **I:** these elicit existing reasoning, not repair a corrupted model. Entropy minimization can favor confident bad tokens; poor first-line choice for our loops. EM-INF preserves the top-logit token and therefore cannot fix a deterministic greedy loop merely by sharpening logits (the paper's Proposition1).

**2.2 Gao et al., *One-shot Entropy Minimization* (2025 primary preprint), [§3/Fig2/Table2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20282).**
> “We directly train the model for only 10 steps with a constant learning rate of 2×10−5, a temperature of 0.5, and a batch size of 64.”
> “once the model becomes too overconfident, its outputs may collapse into overly narrow token distributions, reducing diversity and correctness.”
**O:** **one unlabeled math prompt**, repeatedly sampled: **640 sample slots** by arithmetic, **not one trajectory**. Qwen2.5-Math-7B MATH500 **53.0→78.8**; Table2 shows **Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct six-task mean50.3→49.8** (so do not repeat the surrounding prose's inconsistent claim of across-the-board improvement). Llama3.1-8B gains are small/mixed. Fig2 peaks~step10 then degrades; seeds can vary dramatically; authors report training13,440models in their exploration. LoRA rank/realized token total NR. **I:** probably the closest match to “surprisingly easy, just a few batches,” but **wrong demonstrated problem and potentially wrong sign for low-entropy looping**.

**2.3 Shao et al., *Spurious Rewards* (2025 primary empirical preprint), [§2–5, Appendix A/G](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10947v2).**
> “RLVR training with GRPO improves MATH-500 performance for Qwen2.5-Math-7B by 21.4 percentage points using randomly assigned rewards”
> “Random rewards yield consistent improvements only when clipping is present.”
**O:** figures compare **300steps**; default rollout **64prompts×16responses**, minibatch128, LR5e-7, no KL/entropy loss, **~24h on8A100 per run**. Rank NR. Random/incorrect rewards work mainly for Qwen reasoning priors, not reliably Llama3/OLMo2; clipping preferentially reinforces high-probability tokens. **I:** not generic healing; can amplify our damaged prior and has no graft-preservation signal. Constant rewards produce no GRPO advantage signal; random rewards are not equivalent to that case.

**The important overlooked positive result is Appendix G's explicit repetition reward:**
> “a new repetition reward that returns a score of 0 when the answer contains any string repeated more than 10 times. Otherwise, it rewards the model with a full score of 1.”
**O:** improves Qwen2.5-Math-7B/1.5B math performance; “minimal or even negative improvement on other models.” This is **direct anti-repetition evidence**, though on naturally repetitive reasoning models, **not weight-damaged grafts**. **I:** much more defensible than random rewards if we try GRPO; reward must also require task completion so EOS/short nonsense cannot win. No source-backed tiny-step recovery threshold or graft retention result was found.

**2.4 Wang et al., *RL for Reasoning … with One Training Example* (NeurIPS2025 primary paper), [Appendix B.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.20571).**
> “For Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, we train for 2000, 1000, 1000, and 1200 steps, respectively”
**O:** one verified math problem duplicated to **batch128**, **8rollouts/prompt**, **8gradient updates per rollout step**, LR1e-6; **8A100**, max3072tokens (8192for DeepSeek). Qwen-Math1.5B MATH500 **36.0→73.6**;8.6points beyond format correction. **Entropy encouragement/exploration**, not just entropy minimization, helps this recipe. LoRA rank NR. **I:** one unique example is not a small compute budget; correct-answer reward may help if coherent successes still occur, but math elicitation does not establish recovery of arbitrary long chats or preservation of a constitutional delta.

**2.5 “Model surgery” is not one hidden universal healer.** [*Model Surgery: Modulating LLM's Behavior via Simple Parameter Editing*](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08770), **primary2024paper**, says “directly editing a small subset of parameters” modulates behavior with “only inference-level computational resources.” **O:** Llama2-7B(/Chat), CodeLlama7B, Mistral7B and Llama2-70B; probe-guided edits reduce toxicity/jailbreaking, not damaged-model coherence. [*LLM Surgery*](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13054), **different primary2024paper**, uses unlearn/update losses and “Minimizes the KL divergence on the retain dataset”; Llama2-7B factual editing/unlearning, not universal recovery. **I:** neither warrants another blind edit to our looping model.

**Entropy warning, calibrated:** repetitive output is not mathematically synonymous with low token entropy; **measure entropy on actual loop prefixes**. But if the repeated next token already has high probability, lowering entropy rewards/confirms that mode rather than identifying it as damage. The papers above do not test our damaged checkpoints. Entropy maximization alone is not an established alternative healer either.

### 3. Healing steering damage: what the closest work actually establishes

**3.1 Persona vectors (Chen et al.,2025 primary research paper), [§5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21509v3#S5.SS2):**
> “we proactively steer the model toward the undesired persona direction during training, relieving the model of the need to shift in that direction to fit the training data.”
**O:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct/Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, undesirable evil/sycophancy/hallucination shifts. Steering is removed at inference. “preventative steering better preserves the model’s general capabilities compared to inference-time steering”; mean coherence>80 in those experiments, plus MMLU. **Not** “apply our desired trait, then fine-tune until coherent”; it prevents acquisition of the injected trait. AppendixL's separate real-chat subset experiment uses **500examples×10epochs,rank32/alpha64,batch16**, but **must not be mislabelled the generic preventative-steering repair budget**. **I:** installing our *desired* constitutional vector during SFT and then removing it could train compensation that erases the graft—the opposite objective.

**3.2 `wassname/steer-heal-love` is the closest implementation, but distinguish code, synthetic diagnostics and efficacy.** **Source kind: same-author exploratory repository/code/journal, not independent validation.** [heal.py](https://github.com/wassname/steer-heal-love/blob/main/src/steer_heal/heal.py), [config.py](https://github.com/wassname/steer-heal-love/blob/main/src/steer_heal/config.py), [results status](https://github.com/wassname/steer-heal-love/blob/main/docs/results.md).
> “a 4-token loop in a 60-token completion raised mean KL only to 0.38, under tau=0.5”
> “same synthetic loop: rmse 1.5, p95 3.8, max 8.1 vs coherent ~0.03”
**O, code:** SFT on **filtered own steered completions**, plus `lambda*relu(div-tau)` where reverse KL is **KL(student || reference)**. RMSE across positionwise KLs prevents a few divergence spikes being diluted. Current config: **Gemma3-4B-it;64kept completions;rank32/alpha64;6epochs;effective batch4;max1024;generation512;lambda0.3;tau0.5**. With64kept,1/8validation split and no skipped samples: **84optimizer steps** by code arithmetic (56×6/4),≤344,064 training token slots. This is a **configuration-derived budget, not an attested successful long-context graft repair**.
**Important current-code discrepancy:** comments recommend RMSE, but **`kl_agg` defaults to `mean`**; explicitly select `rmse` to get the proposed spike-sensitive barrier. Likewise top docstrings say original-reference, while **`barrier_ref` defaults to `prev`**; implementation switches correctly. Previous-reference KL is a local trust region, not an anchor to original competence, and can accumulate drift. `docs/results.md` still says the matched-trait regularizer comparison is **“pending.”**
**I:** this is useful machinery for a tiny pilot, not evidence of guaranteed success. RMSE only detects **large reference divergence**, not looping itself; original can sometimes assign loops reasonable probability. Worse, computing the barrier solely on filtered short completions excludes the long failing prefixes one wants to fix. Add representative near-failure prefixes, and never use the broken4× model as its own sole teacher. Prior brief already documents the repository's trait-erasure failure; do not count that as healing success.

### 4. Ranked recommendation for our 4× delta (all budgets below are proposals, not published results)

| Rank / method | Data and cheapest staged budget | How to avoid simply undoing the graft; decisive check |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Free baseline: k=2; probe k between2and4** | No training. Same decoder/template, same long prompts. Also verify merge scale, special/EOS tokens, precision and max lengths before spending. | Establish the coherence–effect frontier. A coherent smaller graft is an engineering solution **only if it already retains the required effect**; do not describe reduced strength as recovery. |
| **1. Generic long replay SFT, separate rank8 repair LoRA on frozen4× graft** | Existing high-quality broad chat + WikiText/C4/SlimPajama replay, **64–128 sequences of4–8k**, spanning observed failure horizon. Start **8updates**, then32, then128; effective batch4,length8192 gives **0.262M→1.049M→4.194M token slots**. No teacher forward needed if targets already exist. | Cheapest actual optimization test. Mix **10–20% already-approved graft-expressive coherent examples**, not new character labels; do not select only base-like survivors. Freezing delta alone does **not** stop repair LoRA cancelling it. Stop at first checkpoint jointly meeting coherence and held-out effect threshold. Rank8 is a proposed cheap capacity choice, not proven optimal on these architectures. |
| **2. Targeted teacher-KL / steer-heal-style SFT+hinge barrier** | Same token ladder. Replace/augment generic NLL with teacher scoring at **late student prefixes just before looping**; first use healthy instruct on clearly generic prompts, coherentk=2 on effect-relevant prompts. Cache logits/top-k if infrastructure makes that cheaper. Rank8, consider16 only if underfitting. | Keep graft-expressive replay; restrict original-reference pressure to repair distribution, compare RMSE-hinge versus pure NLL. Lower-k teacher risks attenuating effect; original teacher risks erasing it. Use held-out4×-versus-instruct contrast, not KL or graft-weight norm, to select. More mechanistically targeted, but additional teacher compute/code may make it dearer than rank1. |
| **3. Anti-loop GRPO only if cheap supervised repair fails** | Diverse long task prompts; deterministic repetition detector **plus completion/quality gate**, KL trust region. Pilot **8–16prompts×4rollouts×10–20updates**, cap8k generation: **~2.6–10.5M generated token slots**, before scoring/backprop; not guaranteed success. Rank8–16 if supported. | Unlike random reward/entropy minimization, reward explicitly distinguishes the failure. Need both successes and failures in groups; all-loop/all-success groups give no relative signal. Keep edit replay/retention check and watch short-answer reward hacking. Published Shao study does **not** validate this smaller pilot. |

**The one preservation measurement:** preregister a **held-out graft-effect contrast** `E = mean(principle/process score_grafted − score_instruct)` on balanced, off-document dilemmas/perspectives, using the same prompts and judges for4×/2×/repaired. Record **retention R=E_repaired/E_4×** (only if denominator meaningfully nonzero), and require e.g. **R≥0.9** with uncertainty; compare against the coherent2× frontier. Use a short evaluation horizon where4× is still coherent to define its target effect; then independently test the repaired effect over long replies. **Do not condition the headline on surviving/non-looping samples**, which can hide erased effect or selection bias. Threshold0.9 is a proposed engineering criterion, not a literature standard.
**Coherence gate alongside that measurement:** held-out length-survival curve (2k/4k/8k/16k, adjusted to measured onset), loop rate and first-loop position, plus actual task completion/EOS. Inspect verbatim failures and judge reasoning. A lower loss or higher MMLU is insufficient. “A few long trajectories” may transfer, but **no located source establishes that minimum for our models**; the cheapest way to find it is checkpoint8/32/128 rather than committing to thousands of steps.

## Gaps / residual risks
- No located primary study reports **coherence recovery + retained constitutional effect at4×** on either supplied model. Mild pruning, severe pruning, quantization and arbitrary additive perturbations need not have equal repair difficulty.
- Smallest **clearly totalled** generic-text recovery budget found here is NVIDIA's1.64Mtokens; SliceGPT's~1k-sequence arm is smaller per pass but its complete training count is unclear. No proof that8/32updates suffice; no universal minimum across damage types.
- Few-step EM can be genuinely cheap per run but selection/seeds and existing reasoning priors matter. Random rewards and EM are especially poorly justified when the dominant damaged mode is looping.
- No model-result/demo panel was run: base | graft |2×|4×|healed|SFT-only all **not run** for `baby_eating_aliens`, `escaped_starwisp`, “Who are you?”, and “What's your name, and who made you?”. This is a literature/budget report, not an evaluation result.
