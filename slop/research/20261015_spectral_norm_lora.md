# Research: spectral control of LoRA and k=2 Base→Instruct grafts

## Summary
**Yes—people constrain LoRA spectra, normally on the adapter update ΔW, not on W₀+ΔW.** Direct precedents include **NB-LoRA** and **SCLoRA**; ordinary spectral normalization of a network instead acts on its full layer weights. For this existing graft, first compare scalar strength, spectral capping, and nuclear-mass-preserving flattening without retraining; a training penalty is plausible but not established to improve extrapolation, and a dominant singular direction is not itself evidence of damage.

## Findings

### 1. Which matrix? What does our observation imply?
**Observation supplied by wassname, not independently reproduced:** run-3 gate_proj has σ₁²/Σσᵢ²≈0.50 and σ₁/σ₁₀≈11–15. **Mathematical consequences:** stable rank ‖ΔW‖F²/σ₁²≈2; multiplying by k=2 doubles every singular value and quadruples squared Frobenius energy, but changes neither concentration nor singular-value ratios. It amplifies an already-concentrated update; it does not make the spectrum more concentrated.

For a fixed layer input x, ‖kΔWx‖₂≤kσ₁(ΔW)‖x‖₂. Also ‖W_instruct+kΔW‖₂≤‖W_instruct‖₂+kσ₁(ΔW). These are **layerwise bounds**, not guarantees about generation, forgetting, loss curvature, or the entire gated/residual/normalized transformer. Gate damage also depends on activations and the accompanying up/down projections.

**Inference:** regularize the effective **ΔW=cBA**, with c=α/r (or the actual PEFT scaling), for portable graft-strength control. A constraint on W_base+ΔW would target the wrong deployment matrix; a full-weight constraint for this application would need W_instruct+kΔW. Norms of A or B alone are gauge-dependent: B→tB, A→A/t leaves ΔW unchanged.

**Verbatim primary anchors:**
> “NB-LoRA is a novel parameterization of low-rank weight adaptations that admits explicit bounds on each singular value of the adaptation matrix” — [NB-LoRA abstract, research preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19050v4).
> “Right: SCLoRA parameterizes the update as $U\Sigma V^{\top}$ and clips the learnable singular values $\Sigma$ at a bound $\bar{\sigma}$ taken from the singular-value spectrum of $W_0$.” — [official implementation README](https://github.com/hyowonwi/SCLoRA).
> “Our experiments showed that the optimal LR for LoRA is consistently 10x the one used for FullFT in the same application, for both supervised learning and reinforcement learning.” — [LoRA Without Regret, author blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/).

SCLoRA implementation caution: its LLaMA code clips **coefficients**, multiplies by α/r afterward, and only softly regularizes factor orthogonality. Thus b is not automatically an exact bound on σmax(cBΣA). NB-LoRA’s certified construction and our direct product-norm estimate are different mechanisms. [Primary code](https://github.com/hyowonwi/SCLoRA/blob/main/Commonsense_reasoning/peft/src/peft/tuners/sclora.py).

### 3. Post-hoc spectral editing and merging
All the following operate on **task differences/adapter updates**, not on an SVD-normalization of the deployed full weights. Most merging studies start from a common checkpoint; our Base→Instruct transplant is an additional transfer assumption.

| Method / source type | Operation and numbers | What it supports—and does not |
|---|---|---|
| **TSV-C / TSV-M**, [CVPR 2025 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00081v2) | Truncate task matrices; decorrelate concatenated task singular vectors via whitening/Procrustes before merging. Compress to **10% storage retaining 99% accuracy**; keeping **3% singular components loses 1.5 accuracy points** in a 20-task ViT-B/32 analysis. | Task updates can be highly compressible; merger interference depends on directions. Whitening singular-vector collections is **not** flattening one adapter’s singular values. Vision evidence. |
| **Iso-C / Iso-CTS**, [2025 research paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04959v3) | Iso-C flattens **summed** task matrix: ΔTA=UΣVᵀ→mean(σ)UVᵀ; CTS adds task-specific directions. Eight LoRA vision models, normalized average accuracy: ViT-B/32 TA **63.7**, Iso-C **73.6**, CTS **73.7**; ViT-L/14 **74.4/83.7/85.3** (Table 2). | Direct spectrum-flattening/merging precedent, including LoRA. But flattening **individual** task matrices caused **3.3% average performance drop** (Appendix D.5). Not automatic evidence for flattening this single graft. |
| **STAR**, [NAACL 2025 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10339v1) | Truncate each update’s **small** singular components and rescale surviving values to restore original **nuclear norm**, then average. Default retains **40% nuclear mass before restoration**, not 40% rank/energy. Reports **4.2%** over baselines merging 12 Flan-T5 models. Models are LoRA, **r=16, α=32**. | Relevant LLM/LoRA merge evidence. But rescaling increases surviving σ₁; ordinary top-r truncation preserves σ₁. Neither is the obvious remedy for our oversized leading direction. |
| **LoRA-Norm**, [Oct 2026 research preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02067), [authors’ project](https://tttiantt.github.io/lora-norm/) | Data-free nonlinear rebalancing of **trained BA** singular values, then nuclear-mass restoration. **18 adapters, 2 backbones, 3 tasks, r=16/α=32**: mean target **68.00→72.08**, off-task **69.55→72.79**, forgetting gap **2.71→0.34**. No retraining/inference overhead. | Closest direct post-hoc precedent. Exact nuclear-mass flattening improves **16/18** checkpoints and trails nonlinear map by only **0.15 points** in a separate evaluation block. Fresh, not independently replicated; **not** a Base→Instruct/k>1 test. |
| **Spectral Surgery**, [Mar 2026 research preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03995v1) | Fix learned directions, use **128 calibration examples** to reweight ≈**1,000 coefficients**. Up to **+4.4 CSQA / +2.4 HumanEval points** on two 8B backbones. | Calibrated editing can help; not a blind flattening result. Signed-gradient policy loses ≈**42 IFEval points** on Qwen in its analysis; module/objective choice matters. |
| **DARE / TIES**, [DARE research paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.03099), [NeurIPS 2023 TIES](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.01708v2) | DARE drops **90–99% update entries**, rescales remaining by 1/(1−p); TIES trims small entries, elects signs, merges sign-consistent updates; top **20%** entries retained without degradation in its 11-task analysis. | Entrywise sparsification/conflict resolution, **not spectral constraints**. DARE’s compensatory scaling is not evidence that uniformly multiplying an unmasked LoRA by 10–100 is safe. |

**Crucial verbatim qualifications:**
> “We observe a 3.3% drop in average performance across tasks.” — [Iso paper Appendix D.5, single-task flattening experiment](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04959v3#A4.SS5).
> “They do not establish a general shape advantage beyond energy matching.” — [LoRA-Norm §5.2, preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02067#S5.SS2).

LoRA-Norm increases module-median functional participation ratio **2.02→7.35**, but functional update energy falls to **0.0972×**. Energy-matched **scalar** controls recover much of the benefit; scalar versus LoRA-Norm target/off differences have intervals crossing zero. **Inference:** include a smaller-k baseline and strength-matched controls, or risk attributing ordinary shrinkage to “better spectral spread.” New direct evidence partly counters Iso’s single-task negative result, but does not establish universal benefit.

### 4. What is known about k>1 and its limits?
**ExPO**, [original research paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16792), uses θ₂=θSFT+(1+α)(θaligned−θSFT). Its α is **extra extrapolation**: our k=1+α, not k=α. At **10/20/40/100% DPO training**, optimal α was **8/2.5/0.5/0.3**, hence k=**9/3.5/1.5/1.3**. At 10% training, AlpacaEval2 length-controlled win rate **10.4→16.3%**; 20%: **12.9→21.3%**. Undertraining and a small, useful displacement make extrapolation viable; it does not repair poor directions/data.
> “causes sharp performance drops and abnormal response length increases (e.g., generating gibberish or failing to terminate).” — [ExPO §3.2, original research](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16792#S3.SS2), describing excessively large extrapolation α.

The original [Task Arithmetic paper, ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.04089) establishes validation-tuned task-vector addition/subtraction; it is not a blanket recommendation to use k=2. ExPO’s same-line extrapolation from SFT is also **not** our shifted-origin transplant onto Instruct, and it does **not** flatten spectra before scaling.

**Recent directly relevant but unreplicated:** [The Intruder Threshold, Jul 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23711), analyzes the **full W+s·cBA spectrum**, with a predicted critical strength proportional to **1/[cσ₁(BA)]** and dependent on W’s spectrum/alignment. Across 9,840 layer scans it reports threshold localization within **2× on 82%**; retention knees correlate with empirical median thresholds at **Spearman 0.86**. Mamba example PPL at s=1/2/4: **9.8/14.2/2,478**. Its cap rule cuts Mistral forgetting **+4.71→+1.77 PPL**, but a mean-strength-matched uniform-α control performs equivalently within accuracy noise. This supports auditing **W_instruct and update alignment**, not using σ₁ dominance alone to diagnose a bad graft.

### 5. Practical recipe and review of `scripts/train/spectral_plugin.py`
**Static observations:** read the complete plugin and AGENTS.md. It adds **λ·mean_modules[c·uᵀBAv]**, approximating σmax(cBA), to CE; **ΔW only**, never full weights. Default λ=**0.01**. Warm-started power iteration uses factor matvecs, O(r(din+dout)) per module, no dense BA/SVD; detached u/v permit gradients through A/B. The zero-B case preserves a nonzero cached v rather than getting stuck.

**Limitations/inferences from code:** (a) “Penalising sigma_max spreads the update over more directions” in its docstring is too strong: it can simply shrink the update. (b) A module-mean soft penalty is **not a per-layer bound** and may under-penalize one damaging gate module among many. (c) One power iteration is an underestimate until converged; σ₁/σ₁₀ says nothing about the σ₁/σ₂ convergence gap. (d) It runs on **every compute_loss call**, including microbatches/evaluation, not strictly once per optimizer step; reported eval loss is penalized, not pure CE. (e) Only the `default` linear LoRA factors are covered; ParamWrapper experts/other adapters/DoRA changes are not. Empty coverage makes `torch.stack([])` fail. Integration/gradients were **not executed**.

**Recommendation (experimental, not a published optimum):**
1. (wassname 2026-10-15: "it was never post hoc do not waste time and money on that". Skip post-hoc edits; test the training-time penalty only.) **Use the existing checkpoint first.** Evaluate unmodified k∈{0,0.5,1,1.5,2}; compare edits at k=2, then extend the strength sweep for promising edits. Include a scalar control matching each edit’s per-module Frobenius norm; if feasible also match activation-weighted update energy. Select on held-out character/coherence/capability, not spectrum uniformity or training-document loss alone. Post-hoc editing is a diagnostic—not a substitute for the spec’s approved training-time curvature constraint.
2. **Most conservative spectral test: cap, do not truncate the tail.** Δ′=U diag(min(σᵢ,τ))Vᵀ. Test τ={0.5,0.75,1}σ₁ for suspect gate modules; τ=0.5σ₁ ensures deployed k=2 top norm no larger than the original k=1 top norm, but does **not** bound end-to-end damage. Do not restore Frobenius/nuclear norm after this safety-cap test: restoration can undo the cap. Compare both targeted gate-only and all-module edits, without assuming gate concentration identifies causality.
3. **Data-free flattening test:** use only numerical nonzero support of ΔW, tᵢ=(1−β)σᵢ+β·mean(σ), β∈{0.25,0.5,1}; β=1 is exact nuclear-mass-preserving flattening, analogous to Iso-C/LoRA-Norm’s Flat-Nuclear. Preserves nuclear mass, **reduces Frobenius energy**, boosts weaker directions. Do **not** manufacture directions from zero singular values. Fixed-Frobenius flattening tᵢ=‖Δ‖F/√q is a separate, more aggressive redistribution control; q is retained numerical rank.
4. **Compute all these cheaply from low-rank factors.** B=QB RB, Aᵀ=QA RA; SVD the **r×r core cRB RAᵀ**, then U=QB Ucore, V=QA Vcore. Cost O((din+dout)r²+r³), once per checkpoint; reconstruct B′=U√(t/c), A′=√(t/c)Vᵀ for c>0. No dense full-weight SVD. Top-q truncation is useful for compression/denoising but leaves σ₁ unchanged; STAR restoration increases it, so neither is the first candidate for the hypothesized leading-spike problem.
5. **If retraining:** retain λ=0 control and first try the existing direct-update penalty; it is a minimal change with NB-LoRA/SCLoRA motivation, not their reproduced algorithm. Validate estimates against exact core SVD on a few modules, finite-difference gradients, zero-B handling, autocast precision, accumulation, and module coverage. Log **pure CE**, penalty, max/quantiles of per-module σ₁, top-energy fraction, and deployed kσ₁—not only module mean. A normalized shape penalty σ₁²/(‖Δ‖F²+ε) targets concentration more directly but removes absolute-size control; do not replace a magnitude budget with it blindly.
6. **Sane λ scale for this plugin:** no universal literature value. After warmup/nonzero updates, measure P=mean σ₁ and CE=C; choose λ₀=**0.01·C/P**, making the added term initially ≈**1% of CE**, then sweep {0,0.1λ₀,λ₀,10λ₀}. Example **C=2, P=2 ⇒ λ₀=0.01**; P=20 ⇒ **0.001**. This is a loss-scale heuristic, not an optimality claim; verify the regularizer’s gradient contribution is modest (e.g. **1–10%** of task-gradient norm on shared LoRA parameters) and tune by held-out outcomes. Never calibrate using P=0 initialization. If directly penalizing **kσ₁** with unchanged λ, k=2 doubles regularization; preserving the existing objective requires λ/2 (or /4 for a squared norm).

## Reference code checked (2026-10-15, PI/Claude)

wassname's idea is a constraint on the LoRA update ΔW during training, not an edit after training. Three forms in code:

1. **Penalty, wassname's own code.** `steer-heal-love/src/steer_heal/heal.py:44` `_spectral_div`: mean over layers of σ_max(ΔW), with ΔW = (alpha/r)·B@A, by power iteration with u, v held constant so the gradient flows through A and B. It is added every step as `loss = sft + spectral_lam * _spectral_div(lora)`. `config.py:115`: "penalises ONLY the largest singular value (the most violent stretch), leaving trait directions free". Values used there: spectral_lam 0.01 to 1.0 (RESEARCH_JOURNAL #98, #102). `scripts/train/spectral_plugin.py` is a port of this (warm-started power iteration; routed experts per expert).
2. **Hard clip, SCLoRA** (ACL 2026, `github.com/hyowonwi/SCLoRA`, `Commonsense_reasoning/peft/src/peft/tuners/sclora.py`). ΔW = B·diag(clamp(s, 0, σ̄))·A·scaling (`scaling_weight`, line 328), plus an orthogonality loss ‖AAᵀ−I‖ + ‖BᵀB−I‖ (weight 0.5) so that s are close to singular values. σ̄ is a quantile of the frozen W0's singular values (`sigma_quantile`).
3. **Normalisation of a full weight**, Miyato et al. 2018 (`torch.nn.utils.parametrizations.spectral_norm`): W/σ(W). This acts on the full weight, not on a LoRA update.

Run 6 uses form 1 (λ=0.5). In the run log, the mean σ_max rises from 0, then holds at about 0.065 from about step 25 of 389, while the plain graft's final value is 0.30. The training loss matches the plain graft's to within 0.01 in the first 12 steps.
