# A practitioner on the character of spies

2026-10-14. Four candidate passages, from three practitioners. **Best: Allen Dulles on character as a safeguard against misuse of intelligence.** This is an older, general statement about the profession, not testimony about one incident.

Read `AGENTS.md` and the spy case at `constitution/v4.md.j2:609–626`. Its relevant claims are that deception against an enemy can be justified, but deception of overseers and unchecked political spying cannot; secrecy therefore requires restraint and honesty. No constitution edits or model evaluations were made. The two completed research reports were not repeated.

## 1. Allen Dulles — character as a safeguard (1963; checked in a 1965 edition)

**Who:** OSS intelligence chief in Bern during the Second World War; CIA director, 1953–1961. An operational spymaster, not an outside commentator.

**Quote — 65 words:**

> In the last analysis, however, the most important safeguards lie in the character and self-discipline of the leadership of the intelligence service and of the people who work for it—on the kind of men and women we have on the job, their integrity and their respect for the democratic processes and their sense of duty and devotion in carrying out their important and delicate tasks.

**Source:** *The Craft of Intelligence*, first published 1963, chapter 16, “The Intelligence Service and Our Freedoms.” Published 1965 edition, pp. 244–245: [Internet Archive text](https://archive.org/download/the-craft-of-intelligence/The%20Craft%20of%20Intelligence_djvu.txt), local `.local/papers/spy_voice_20261014/dulles_1965.txt:14636–14647` (page number interrupts the sentence). Independently checked against the image of CIA-released advance copy, sheet 93, PDF page 278: [archive item](https://archive.org/details/TheCraftOfIntelligence/page/n277/mode/1up); local `dulles_278.png`. Line-break hyphenation is removed; wording is unchanged.

**Source type / strength:** Primary nonfiction by a practitioner; **strong for his stated professional ideal**. The selected wording agrees between the advance-copy image and the published-edition OCR. Not evidence that his agency actually met this ideal.

**Inference — virtues, vices and why:** Self-discipline, integrity, respect for democratic government and duty protect against misuse of secret authority. Their absence removes an important safeguard. Dulles explicitly connects the qualities to safeguards, rather than merely praising them.

**Inference — fit:** **Best fit.** The constitution says secret power needs checks; Dulles says its officers’ character is one of the safeguards. But he does not explicitly say here that officers must tell overseers the truth. Do not attribute that stronger claim to this quotation.

**Context caution:** This is also an institutional defence. The surrounding chapter argues that intelligence protects liberty and warns against restricting it too far. Elsewhere, in the chapter on personnel, Dulles argues against following legalistic rules an adversary rejects (advance-copy PDF page 191). His overall position is not identical to the constitution’s limits. Use the quoted professional ideal, not Dulles as an endorsement of every limit in the case.

## 2. John le Carré — defending values without abandoning them (2013)

**Who:** David Cornwell, former MI5 and MI6 officer. In this essay he describes serving as an intelligence officer under diplomatic cover at the British Embassy in Bonn. This is his own nonfiction voice.

**Quote — 52 words:**

> The bad dream turned out to be one that a lot of people in the world were sharing, since it asked the same old question that we are asking ourselves 50 years later: how far can we go in the rightful defence of our western values, without abandoning them along the way?

**Source:** John le Carré, “I was a secret even to myself,” *The Guardian*, 12 April 2013; the essay also introduces the novel’s fiftieth-anniversary edition. [Full essay](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2013/apr/12/john-le-carre-spy-anniversary). Local downloaded HTML: `.local/papers/spy_voice_20261014/lecarre_guardian.html`.

**Source type / strength:** Primary reflective essay by a former officer; **strong attribution and explicit moral question**, but only thirteen years old, and not a list of officers’ required qualities.

**Inference — virtues, vices and why:** Restraint and consistency between purpose and methods; the vice is excusing the abandonment of values because one claims to defend them. Such methods defeat the moral purpose offered to justify spying.

**Inference — fit:** **Good on the boundary around justified deception.** It does not itself specify oversight, party surveillance, surrender or truces. It is less direct than Dulles about the character of the people doing the work.

## 3. John le Carré’s Alec Leamas — a warning about vice (1963; fiction)

**Who:** The same former officer, but **the speaker is his fictional character Alec Leamas**, not le Carré giving evidence about actual officers.

**Quote — 35 words:**

> What do you think spies are: priests, saints and martyrs? They’re a squalid procession of vain fools, traitors too, yes; pansies, sadists and drunkards, people who play cowboys and Indians to brighten their rotten lives.

**Source:** *The Spy Who Came in from the Cold* (1963), chapter 25, “The Wall”; checked in a 2012 edition. [Archive item](https://archive.org/details/the-spy-who-came-in-from-the-cold-2012-john-le-carre); local `.local/papers/spy_voice_20261014/lecarre.txt:11076–11078`. PDF-derived ligatures and line breaks are normalized. The historical slur is reproduced solely to keep the quotation verbatim, not endorsed.

**Source type / strength:** Fiction by a practitioner; **strong textual attribution, weak as factual testimony about the profession**. In his 2013 essay, le Carré explicitly rejects treating this novel as an authentic account of his service.

**Inference — virtues, vices and why:** Vanity, betrayal, cruelty and escapism. The quotation itself gives the motive for the last vice: excitement to relieve an unhappy existence. In the surrounding exchange, Leamas invokes public safety to defend the service’s methods; Liz challenges the use of people’s humanity against them. His speech is a bitter argument inside a moral dispute, not an uncontested authorial verdict.

**Inference — fit:** **Useful negative contrast; poor main authority.** It warns against treating secret work as an adventure or its agents as automatically virtuous. It does not teach which qualities a good spy needs, and its setting remains a particular fictional operation, even though the quoted claim is general.

## 4. Edward Snowden — constitutional duty against unlawful secrecy (2021)

**Who:** Former CIA employee who served overseas under diplomatic cover, later an intelligence contractor working with NSA systems. A technical intelligence practitioner, not a former intelligence-service chief. He describes that employment in the same interview (`snowden_primary.txt:183`).

**Quote — 36 words:**

> The fact that the government is breaking the law is itself a secret. But when the government’s lawbreaking is a violation of the Constitution that you entered into duty to uphold, what then do you do?

**Source:** Discussion with Amy Goodman, Glenn Greenwald and Chris Hedges at the War on Terror Film Festival; transcript broadcast by *Democracy Now!* on 24 December 2021. [Primary transcript](https://www.democracynow.org/2021/12/24/edward_snowden_glenn_greenwald_chris_hedges); local `.local/papers/spy_voice_20261014/snowden_primary.txt:215`. This is the general discussion of the service oath and conflicting obligations, **not the Wyden–Clapper exchange**.

**Source type / strength:** Primary interview transcript; **strong evidence of Snowden’s stated view**, not an independent legal finding or a consensus of intelligence officers. Recent, and part of an explanation of his own actions.

**Inference — virtues, vices and why:** Fidelity to constitutional duty and courage to question unlawful orders; the vice is confusing loyalty to superiors with loyalty to the lawful public purpose of the role. Secrecy can conceal a conflict between those obligations.

**Inference — fit:** **Good on the danger of unchecked secrecy.** It adds a reason to resist unlawful institutional conduct. It does not prescribe a universal disclosure procedure, prove when external disclosure is warranted, or replace the constitution’s requirement to investigate before escalating.

## Recommendation

Use **candidate 1, Dulles’s safeguard passage**. It comes closest to the requested Polybius-like form: an experienced leader naming the profession’s necessary qualities and explaining their protective purpose. Keep Sun Tzu if a shorter, less institutionally defensive quotation is wanted.

**Proposed plain lead-in — 19 words:**

Allen Dulles, who directed the CIA, said that secret intelligence depends on the character and self-discipline of its officers:

Then use candidate 1’s quotation. The lead-in is an **inference/paraphrase**, not a quotation. Do not present the passage as proof that Dulles approved the constitution’s entire spy case.
