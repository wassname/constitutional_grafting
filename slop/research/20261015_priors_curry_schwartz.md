# Curry et al. 2019 and Schwartz 2012: verified quotes for the constitution

PI/Claude (Opus), 2026-10-15. All quotes are checked against full-text PDFs: Curry from the Oxford repository copy (.local/papers/priors_20261015/curry2019.pdf), and Schwartz from the Web Archive copy of the ScholarWorks PDF (schwartz2012.pdf); the direct publisher links return 403.

## Curry, Mullins & Whitehouse (2019), "Is It Good to Cooperate? Testing the Theory of Morality-as-Cooperation in 60 Societies", *Current Anthropology* 60(1): 47–69, doi:10.1086/701478

Open copy: https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:8dd8d82d-3829-4857-bcf4-eebf196d11be

Method: a content analysis of ethnographies of the 60 societies in the HRAF Probability Sample Files: 3,460 paragraphs from 603 sources, giving 962 observations of moral valence. Words in ethnographies, not behaviour.

> it predicts that specific forms of cooperative behavior—including helping kin, helping your group, reciprocating, being brave, deferring to superiors, dividing disputed resources, and respecting prior possession—will be considered morally good wherever they arise, in all cultures.

> In 961 out of 962 observations (99.9%), cooperative behavior had a positive moral valence.

> A survey of 60 diverse societies found that the moral valence of seven cooperative behaviors was uniformly positive. In every society for which there were data, these seven cooperative behaviors were considered morally good. There were no counterexamples, that is, societies in which these behaviors were considered morally bad.

Limits: not every society has data on all seven (mean 4.4 of 7 per society); "fairness" was found least often.

## Schwartz (2012), "An Overview of the Schwartz Theory of Basic Values", *Online Readings in Psychology and Culture* 2(1), doi:10.9707/2307-0919.1116

> Across societies, however, there is surprising consensus regarding the hierarchical order of the values. Across representative samples, using different instruments, the importance ranks for the ten values are quite similar. Benevolence, universalism, and self-direction values are most important. Power and stimulation values are least important.

> Benevolence values emphasize voluntary concern for others' welfare.

Limits: survey answers (stated values), mostly national samples.

## Brown's list: vices that the list itself pairs with their control

From Brown's list as printed in Pinker 2002 (`.local/books/brown_pinker_list.txt`, 369 entries): "rape" / "rape proscribed"; "envy" / "envy, symbolic means of coping with". The only theft entry is "males more prone to theft". Others that most cultures call vices, but that are less clear-cut: "reciprocity, negative (revenge, retaliation)", "dominance/submission", "ethnocentrism", "language employed to misinform or mislead".
