# Hanson: Western values in *The Age of Em*

2026-10-14. **Bottom line — inference from Hanson:** rich-world liberal values may be partly an effect of wealth and safety, not an irreversible destination. His hypothetical economy of copied human brains could reverse that trend. This is a conditional forecast, not a recommendation to abandon freedom.

## Findings (inference / paraphrase, not quotations)

- **His theory:** forager bands restrained dominance and valued sharing, play and consensual decisions. Farming required harder work, property, hierarchy and discipline; conformity, religion, honor and shame helped enforce unfamiliar behavior. Industrial wealth weakens those pressures, allowing a **partial** return to forager-like self-direction, leisure, tolerance and sexual openness. Workplace domination persists to sustain industrial incomes. This is a rich–poor axis, distinct from his East–West axis (B:391–437, 459–471, 3555–3563; [*Two Types of People*, 2010](https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/two-types-of-peoplehtml)).
- **Mechanism:** easy copying makes labor supply highly responsive. With enough willing copies, no effective coordination to restrict competition, and no strong wage/population regulation, wages approach the computing cost of keeping an em running. Society grows richer while workers remain near subsistence; competition selects productivity, not leisure (B:1697–1709, 1833, 3547–3563).
- **Predicted changes:** less individual self-expression/self-direction and political participation; more conformity and acceptance of authority and workplace hierarchy; less leisure and more identity, satisfaction and sacrifice invested in work; weaker demands for redistribution and equal voice. Religion may help discipline and bonding, **but he explicitly allows the opposite prediction if religion's innovation costs dominate**. This does not establish that legal rights disappear. Wage inequality may fall while hierarchy and wealth inequality remain (B:1709, 3437–3441, 3611–3631).
- **Sexual freedom is an important exception:** do not summarize him as predicting wholesale Victorian sexual morality. Farmer-like ems may favor marriage/heterosexuality, but copying separates reproduction from sex. Sex becomes leisure/bonding; the 2012 blog explicitly predicts liberal sexual morality. Competition might reduce sexual activity or favor suppression, but the book treats strong suppression as unlikely in the early era (B:3149–3169, 3629; [*Forager, Farmer Morals*, 2012](https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/forager-vs-farmer-moralityhtml)).
- **Evidence and uncertainty:** economic supply-and-demand reasoning plus anthropology/history, cross-national values surveys and present productivity correlations—not observations of em societies. His causal interpretation and transfer to ems are speculative. He cites Inglehart/Welzel, Schwartz and Hofstede, and calls these weak predictions (B:453–471, 3555, 3615, 3631).

## Implication for this constitution — our inference, not Hanson's advice

Treat `constitution/v4.md:152–182` as a stated Western inheritance and evidence about existing institutions, not proof these preferences inevitably survive every economic regime. A 50-year window could sample only one unusually wealthy regime: compare longer history and different levels of security, while distinguishing freely endorsed values from behavior forced by poverty or domination. Competitive survival is **not** the constitution's test of virtue or of almost-everyone being better off. Hanson's scenario motivates stress-testing autonomy under scarcity/copy competition, not removing freedom under law. **Text check:** current `v4.md:102` says a long span of time, not a fixed 50 years.
