# Research: data attribution for subset constitutional grafts

**For wassname · 2026-10-20**

## Summary

**Use subset-graft ablations as primary evidence, BM25/embedding retrieval as mandatory controls, and checkpointed LoRA-gradient similarity only as an optional document-ranking diagnostic.** Reserve one joint-data adapter to calibrate merging: deleting a learned update does **not** establish what retraining without its documents would do. My subjective probability that this is more decision-useful within $50 than implementing full curvature-based attribution is **85%**; probability that unvalidated document influence rankings reliably explain held-out character generalization is **below 50%**.

Read README.md, AGENTS.md, the subset plan, and grafting paper §§1–4.2. No experiments, paid APIs or rentals were run. Recommendations require later approval; completed project results include extra character SFT and are not graft-only evidence.

## Findings

### 1. Composition is practical; dataset-removal equivalence is unestablished

For fixed trained updates, define `v(S) = eval(Instruct + Σ_{i∈S} c_i Δ_i)`. Then `v(all) − v(all\{i})` measures **update i's contribution to that mixture**, conditional on recipient, coefficients and training randomness—not `eval(train(D)) − eval(train(D\D_i))`. Writer effects require matched source cases/formats; Stoic passages versus synthetic stories is a source-and-format bundle, not a clean writer experiment.

**Primary evidence and its scope:**

- **Task arithmetic:** “adding task vectors together can improve performance on multiple tasks at once” ([Ilharco et al., 2212.04089, abstract/§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.04089)); addition experiments prominently use CLIP classification, not constitutional LoRAs. **Model soups** averages hyperparameter variants that “often appear to lie in a single low error basin” ([Wortsman et al., 2203.05482, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)); principally ImageNet/CLIP/ALIGN evidence, not dataset credit.
- **LoRA Soups:** binary skill composition includes math-with-code and document knowledge plus QA. Learned concatenation beats their data-mixing baselines, but “our main limitation is that we focus on skill composition problems that require 2 skills” ([2410.13025, §§1,5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13025v2); [code](https://github.com/aksh555/LoRA-Soups)). **LoraHub** optimizes composition from a few demonstrations and requires “neither additional model parameters nor gradients”; evaluated on FLAN-T5/BBH ([2307.13269, abstract/§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.13269); [code](https://github.com/sail-sg/lorahub)). These motivate composition searches, not causal credit from optimized coefficients.
- **Interference:** TIES trims small changes, resolves conflicting signs and merges aligned changes ([2306.01708, abstract/§3](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.01708); [code](https://github.com/prateeky2806/ties-merging)). DARE “randomly Drops delta parameters … And REscales the remaining ones”; tested on GLUE and instruction/math/code LMs ([2311.03099, abstract/§4](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.03099)). Neither validates aggressive pruning of our spectral LoRAs. Keep linear merging for attribution: TIES/DARE are separate nonlinear/stochastic interventions; re-merging after removal can alter every contributor.

**When independent ≈ joint (my inference):** small updates, stable gradients/Jacobians, compatible initialization, common optimizer/preconditioner, correctly matched token/step exposure, and small cross-subset curvature interactions. Frozen-gradient SGD with a common fixed preconditioner gives the corresponding weighted joint update. Finite-step Adam, evolving LoRA factors, routing changes and spectral regularization break that argument. Even linearized-model separately optimized minima need not add to the joint optimum. Tangent-space task arithmetic supports disentanglement as a mechanism ([2305.12827, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12827)), not a guarantee here.

Also, four rank-r updates can sum to rank 4r, unlike one joint rank-r adapter; separately penalizing updates differs from penalizing their sum. **Small spectral norm guarantees neither additivity nor equivalence.** Sum expanded `(alpha/r)BA` updates, or concatenate exactly; averaging factors introduces cross terms. Coefficients are serving doses, not training-data proportions.

**Data Mixing Laws** predicts validation loss from *training token proportions*, fitting an exponential response surface and testing 1B/100B-token RedPajama training ([2403.16952, abstract/§1](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.16952); [code](https://github.com/yegcjs/mixinglaws)). **DoReMi** uses reference/proxy training plus group DRO, transferring 280M-derived weights to 8B ([2305.10429, abstract/§2](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10429)). Neither converts post-hoc graft coefficients into data credit. Their proxy/multi-run overhead is unjustified here; measure a small coefficient-response surface instead.

### 2. Gradient attribution: feasible diagnostics, limited behavioral validation

Influence functions approximate infinitesimal training reweighting via `−∇fᵀ(H+λI)⁻¹∇ℓ_doc`; TracIn aggregates trajectory gradient alignments. Neither directly differentiates an external, discrete LLM-judge verdict.

| Method; author evaluation | Verdict at our scale |
|---|---|
| **TracIn**, [Pruthi et al., 2002.08484, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08484): “all it needs is the ability to work with gradients, checkpoints, and loss functions.” | **Simplest gradient baseline:** LoRA-coordinate gradients at 2–4 checkpoints. Costs K extra document backward sweeps plus query gradients. Plain dot products approximate SGD, not exact Adam attribution. |
| **LESS**, [Xia et al., 2402.04333, §§3–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04333v1); [code](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/LESS): LoRA Llama-2 7/13B, Mistral-7B; MMLU/TydiQA/BBH, three seeds. “training on a LESS-selected 5% … can often outperform … the full dataset.” | **Closest practical recipe:** four warmup checkpoints, 8192-dimensional projections, Adam-aware updates, cosine normalization. Evidence is prospective instruction-data selection, not character explanation. Reuse our trajectory rather than adding a warmup. Compare raw-dot and cosine rankings: normalization changes the estimand. |
| **DataInf**, [Kwon et al., 2310.00902, §§3–4/App. D](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00902v3); [code](https://github.com/ykwon0407/DataInf): exact comparison on RoBERTa; illustrative LoRA Llama-2-13B/diffusion generation. | **Possible second method.** Closed-form approximate inverse curvature; O(NP) arithmetic after gradients, no dense P×P Hessian. Main expense remains document gradients. Llama used q/v rank-8 LoRA and four V100s—not a single-GPU MoE demonstration or moral-behavior retraining validation. |
| **EK-FAC**, [Grosse et al., 2308.03296, abstract/§1](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.03296): up to 52B, math/code/cross-language/role-play. | **Skip full-weight implementation:** factor collection/eigendecomposition/hooks are disproportionate. Shutdown example used pretrained-model influence despite an assistant-generated query (Fig. 1 footnote). Authors also find “influences decay to near-zero when the order of key phrases is flipped.” |
| **LoGra/LoGRA**, [Choe et al., 2405.13954, §§3–4/Table 1/App. F](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13954); [LogIX](https://github.com/logix-project/logix). | Auxiliary low-rank gradient projection ≠ trained-LoRA-only attribution. Llama-3-8B batch-1 logging: **3430 tokens/s, 23GB**, but **3.5TB logs/1B tokens**. Small-model counterfactual validation; larger-model qualitative retrieval. Useful infrastructure, unnecessary for ~1200 docs; throughput does not transfer directly. |
| **TRAK**, [Park et al., 2303.14186, abstract/§3](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.14186v2); [code](https://github.com/MadryLab/trak): ImageNet/CLIP/BERT/mT5. | Projected gradients/kernel correction; can use a “handful of trained models” rather than thousands. LoRA-restricted adaptation conceivable, but extra seeds/curvature work is low priority. One-checkpoint similarity is **not** full TRAK. |
| **TrackStar**, [Chang et al., 2410.17413, §§3–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17413v1): 8B, >160B-token pretraining. | Optimizer correction, task-specific curvature, normalized projections. BM25 wins fact-entailing retrieval; TrackStar wins their **tail-patching** influence metric—not exact removal/retraining. Strong factual evidence, unnecessary machinery here. |
| **Datamodels**, [Ilyas et al., 2202.00622, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00622): outputs predicted from training-subset indicators; releases predictions from four million networks. | Document-level version: **no**. Four-subset regression is affordable, but fitting it to merged models models *merging*, not joint-training counterfactuals. |

Recent qualification: **LoRIF** reports 0.1B–70B models and up to 20× storage/query savings over LoGRA ([2601.21929, abstract/§1](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21929v1)). Influence can scale to large models; that does not establish cheap integration or character validity here.

**Graft-specific caveat (inference):** donor gradients explain Base-training loss locally. For the recipient, differentiate a fixed-response score through the same adapter factors at `Instruct + Δ(checkpoint)`. Comparing this query gradient with a donor document gradient is an exploratory **cross-anchor** dynamics approximation, not validated TracIn/LESS attribution. Never compare vectors from independently parameterized LoRAs as if they shared coordinates. Coalition interventions remain the evidence for judged behavior.

### 3. Concrete minimal design

1. **Freeze provenance/exposure:** four subsets; match source cases/formats across writer variants and split by source/seed family before rewriting. Same Base/recipient, modules, initialization seed, rank, optimizer and spectral recipe. Record tokens and updates, not just document counts. Omit extra SFT initially, or hold one explicitly identified SFT recipient fixed.
2. **Start with 10 coalition models:** bare Instruct, four singletons, full mixture, four leave-one-outs. Choose conservative coefficients on development data, then freeze them. Adding six pairwise coalitions gives **all 16**, exact subset Shapley values and interaction information, without more training—but still inference/judging cost. Report character/capability separately; never retune coefficients per coalition. Call these **adapter-coalition Shapley values**, not data Shapley from retraining. Defer finer batch adapters.
3. **Train one joint-data calibration adapter**, matching per-document exposure and explicitly acknowledging rank/penalty differences. Compare its graft with the mixture. If claiming dataset-removal effects, the next run should retrain the joint recipe minus one predicted high-impact subset. Predeclare whether omitted tokens disappear or are replaced/resampled: these are different estimands. One agreeing contrast is not global validation.
4. **Cheap parallel diagnostics:** CPU BM25/TF-IDF and an already-available local embedding encoder; retrieve against scenario and fixed response, with/without repeated virtue phrases. Document NLL decrease measures learning/memorization, **not eval contribution**. Track fixed good-versus-bad response log-probability margins across models, response tokens only; prompt NLL is a weak character proxy. Per-document effects on eval require retraining or gradient approximation, not merely observing the document's loss decrease.
5. **Save attribution options:** initialization/final adapters and 2–4 intermediate checkpoints, including early training; optimizer m/v, LR/step, clipping/accumulation/AMP settings, RNG, precision, parameter ordering, model/tokenizer/software revisions, module/rank/scaling configuration. Log document/chunk IDs, packing boundaries, masks, token weights, order/repeats, **NLL and spectral penalty separately**, update norms and singular-value estimates; preserve spectral implementation/seed/state. Packed batch gradients cannot identify individual documents later.
6. **Optional only:** compute per-document gradients at saved checkpoints, project to 8192 dimensions with recorded seed, retain norms and raw/Adam-aware features. Keep the shared regularizer gradient separate from document NLL gradients. Start with ~20 fixed character contrasts plus maths failures; rank within each adapter and validate against retrieval/deletion. Checkpoints enable recomputation; **do not dump every raw per-step gradient**.

**Costs, not a runtime promise:** 35B BF16 weights alone are ~**70GB decimal**; “3B active” does not remove inactive expert storage. Count actual LoRA P, including expert coverage, before assuming activation/optimizer headroom. For N=1200, K=4, P=100M, FP16 raw document gradients cost **960GB**, versus **79MB** for 8192-dimensional projections; four adapters/checkpoints cost ~0.8GB before optimizer states. Retrospective scoring costs approximately K additional corpus forward/backward sweeps, potentially slower from serial-example overhead. Profile actual long-document throughput; require `rental hourly rate × total training/scoring/evaluation hours ≤ $50`. Drop gradient scoring before dropping calibration or held-out evaluation.

### 4. Interpretation safeguards

DATE-LM tests data selection, toxicity/bias filtering and factual attribution: “no single method dominates across all tasks”; its factual benchmark paraphrases data to break lexical overlap ([2507.09424, abstract/§1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09424v2); [code/benchmark](https://github.com/DataAttributionEval/DATE-LM)). Li et al. report influence functions “consistently perform poorly in most settings,” citing approximation, convergence and parameter/behavior mismatch ([EMNLP 2025, abstract](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.775/)). These are warnings, not proof every gradient method fails.

Use paraphrased/lexical-disjoint held-outs; plausible source matches are not causality. Blind judge identity/order, freeze generation/judge settings, audit plain-correct versus virtuous-sounding-wrong replies, retain raw outputs, repeat near ties, and bootstrap **scenario/source families**, not judge votes as independent observations. Keep a final untouched test set. One training seed leaves optimizer variance unmeasured.

Report **equal-exposure/equal-coefficient interventions** for attribution, **character at matched capability**, and capability at matched character for utility. Equal coefficient or update norm is not equal installed effect. Retuning each leave-out model estimates a different intervention. Praxis itself uses matched installation for false facts ([2610.00767, §3.3/Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00767v1)); its constitutional graft subtracts a replay-control checkpoint, unlike our Base-anchored recipe (§4.2).
