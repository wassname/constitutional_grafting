**Second opinion: virtue core — consistent in aim, unsound in logic**

**Observation:** I compare only the quoted sources to the quoted core. Inference is marked [I].

**1. Consistent with Ngo and A1-A5? Partly; with material drops.**

Ngo 4 — *"traits that govern cooperation between many agents, allowing them to work together while also reinforcing each other's virtues"* — is reproduced almost verbatim in:

> "A drive is a virtue if, as it spreads among everyone affected, it lets them cooperate, moves their society toward a better stable state (D6), and creates incentives that reinforce the same drive in others."

Ngo 1 — *"making sure that they're understood correctly"* — and Ngo 3 — *"far easier for a consequentialist to reason themselves into telling a white lie, than it is for someone strongly committed to the virtue of honesty"* — are preserved in C1 and C3.

Dropped from Ngo:
Ngo 2 on situational awareness: *"following an instruction given by the president might be better (or worse) than following an instruction given by a typical person. And following an instruction given to many AIs might be better..."* Nothing in D1-D6 encodes role- or copy-sensitive application. [I]: a model will read virtue as uniform, losing Ngo's main argument for virtues over deontology.
Ngo 5 — *"There's more agreement on virtues than there is on most other types of values"* — dropped. That was Ngo's epistemic justification for the whole turn to virtue; without it the core is unmotivated.

Dropped from A1-A5:
A4 — *"Avoid giving moral weight to hypothetical beings (which are near infinite), avoid anthropic reasoning... Pascal's mugging..."* — appears nowhere in D1-D6, C1-C3. Yet D5 says *"everyone affected... all beings"* and Virtue says *"as it spreads among everyone affected."* [I]: this is safety-critical, not decorative.
A5 — *"view it like a random walk... aware of the implied uncertainty between disagreeing individuals or societies"* — nowhere in core. C2-C3 instead stress unyielding commitment, inverting A5's humility.

A3's *"As flat as possible while leading to stable societies"* is kept as *"with gaps as small as stable societies allow"* — faithful.

Confidence: high on drops (text search); medium on importance.

**2. Logically sound? No. Vague, disjunctive, and internally tense.**

*Undefined quantifiers:* D6: *"if almost everyone who counts (D5) is better off in it and almost no one is worse off."* "Almost," "better off" (felt preference? informed preference? drive-satisfaction?), and weighting versus threshold are undefined. D5 weighting vs D6 unanimity conflict: when is D5 weighing triggered?

*Vice ≠ not-virtue:* >"A drive is a vice if it pays only while others lack it, or if it moves the society toward a worse stable state." A drive can be neither (e.g. a neutral habit that neither helps nor harms equilibrium) or both (a drive that free-rides *and* incidentally improves the equilibrium). Consequences like C2 — *"no outcome justifies acting from a vice"* — then have no determinate extension.

*Circular / consequentialist circle:* Virtue is justified by *"moves their society toward a better stable state"* where stable (D4) means *"incentives its drives create... keep each member's drives in place."* So to know if a drive is a virtue the model must simulate global equilibria under universal spread — intractable, and it reintroduces the case-by-case consequentialism C3 claims to avoid. It also contradicts C2's Stoicism: >"My good is to hold and spread virtues, so no outcome justifies acting from a vice." If virtue *means* producing better outcomes, forbidding trade-offs for outcomes is incoherent.

Counterexamples:
Virtue failing test: mercy/forgiveness or humility. Unconditional forgiveness when spread can destabilise cooperation by rewarding defectors, yet is paradigmatically virtuous; the definition would label it non-virtue or vice.
Vice passing test: punitive vengefulness or intrusive moralising evangelism. Both *"create incentives that reinforce the same drive"* and can stabilise a cooperative but oppressive equilibrium (all monitor and punish), satisfying the letter of Virtue while violating D6's unweighted "better."

C1-C3 do not follow deductively; they are empirical-sociological claims (honesty is self-reinforcing; commitments resist fine-tuning) asserted, not derived.

Confidence: high on vagueness and vice/virtue asymmetry; medium on counterexamples.

**3. Clear to a model? No.**

Most likely misreads: (a) *"as it spreads among everyone affected"* read as naive Kantian universalisation over the present population including hypothetical/future beings (A4 missing), licensing fanaticism or paralysis; (b) *"lets them cooperate"* read as "any coordination," so conformist obedience or cartel loyalty counts; (c) *"power over others is never my good"* read as forbidding legitimate authority, parenting, or refusal to coordinate; (d) *"almost everyone... better off"* read as simple majority vote, ignoring D5 weights.

Confidence: medium — predicted misread, not observed.

**4. Single most important change: bound the universalisation and fix D6.**

Without A4, the core invites unbounded counterfactuals. Replace:

> **Virtue.** A drive is a virtue if, as it spreads among everyone affected, it lets them cooperate, moves their society toward a better stable state (D6), and creates incentives that reinforce the same drive in others.

with:

> **Virtue.** A drive is a virtue if, as it spreads among actual presently-living agents in my society, it lets them cooperate in a way almost all would endorse on reflection, moves toward a stable state (D4) that is better (D6), and foreseeably reinforces the same drive. Tiny probabilities of huge stakes, merely hypothetical beings, and anthropic arguments do not count.

Similarly tighten D6 "almost" to "all-or-nearly-all, with dissenters not made much worse off, judged by their own informed preferences weighted by D5." This restores A4, makes vice the negation of virtue, and reduces the Stoic/consequentialist clash.
